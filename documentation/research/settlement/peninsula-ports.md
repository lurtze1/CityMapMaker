## Fortified peninsula port cities

Research for the CityMapMaker calibration city type "walled capital on a peninsula,
water on three sides, harbour inlet, one landward exit". Real towns only, roughly 500-1600.
Every claim below names a page; claims marked "(unverified)" were seen only in a search snippet. "(Wikipedia)" marks Wikipedia as
the source. "(unverified)" marks something seen only in a search snippet, not on a fetched
page. Nothing about the fictional city is invented here.

Jargon used, one line each:
- **Land neck / isthmus**: the narrow strip of land joining the peninsula to the mainland.
- **Enceinte**: the full ring of walls around a town.
- **Curtain**: the stretch of wall between two towers.
- **Moat / fosse / ditch**: a dug trench in front of a wall (wet or dry).
- **Boom / harbour chain**: a chain or floating barrier stretched across a harbour mouth at night or in war.
- **Mole / breakwater**: a stone pier built into the sea to shelter a harbour.
- **Arsenal / neorion / darsena / drassanes**: naval shipyard (the words are Italian/Arabic, Greek, Genoese-Italian and Catalan).
- **Citadel**: a fortress inside or at the edge of the town, which can be held even after the town falls.
- **Bastion / cavalier**: gunpowder-age angled wall projection / a raised gun platform behind it.
- **Tombolo**: a sand bar joining an island to the shore.
- **Sestiere**: one of the six districts of Venice.

### Findings

1. **The land neck gets the heaviest wall; the sea sides get lighter walls.**
   Constantinople's land wall is a double wall plus moat (inner wall 4.5-6 m thick, 12 m high;
   outer wall 2 m thick; moat over 20 m wide, up to 10 m deep), while the Golden Horn sea wall
   is about 10 m high and a single line (Wikipedia, Walls of Constantinople). Dubrovnik's
   landward main wall is 4-6 m thick, its seaward walls 1.5-5 m (Wikipedia, Walls of
   Dubrovnik). Cádiz's only land access was a wall from coast to coast across the isthmus
   (ISPRS 2020).

2. **Land gates are few; sea gates are several.** Dubrovnik has two land gates (Pile west,
   Ploče east, both with drawbridges) and two harbour gates (Wikipedia, Walls of Dubrovnik).
   Constantinople's land wall had nine main gates over about 5.7 km; the Golden Horn wall had
   14 gates counted by Buondelmonti (16 known) over 5.6 km; the Marmara wall 13 gates
   (Wikipedia, Walls of Constantinople). Cádiz had one land wall and Puerta de Tierra
   ("land gate") as its main entrance (ISPRS 2020; gate name from search snippet, unverified).

3. **Each land gate gets its own outwork.** Dubrovnik's Pile Gate is covered by Fort Bokar and
   the moat; Ploče Gate by the detached Revelin fort (Wikipedia, Walls of Dubrovnik).

4. **The harbour mouth is closed by a chain between two towers or a tower and a mole.**
   Constantinople: chain on floating barrels from the Tower of Eugenius to the Kastellion at
   Galata, from Leo III (717-741) (Wikipedia, Walls of Constantinople). Dubrovnik: chain from
   St John fortress to the Kaše breakwater / St Luke's tower (Wikipedia, Walls of Dubrovnik).
   Kontoskalion (Constantinople's Marmara naval harbour): wall and chain by Michael VIII,
   later iron gates (Wikipedia, Kontoskalion). Acre: Venetian admiral "broke through Acre's
   harbour chain" in 1257 (Wikipedia, War of Saint Sabas). Rhodes: two towers guard the
   commercial harbour, a fort the military harbour (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Rhodes).

5. **Naval shipyards sit in their own walled basin at the edge of town, not in the centre.**
   Venice: Arsenale in Castello, eastern side of Venice, about 20 minutes' walk from
   St Mark's, about 45 ha or 15 % of the city, walled (thewingedsandals.com; Wikipedia,
   Venetian Arsenal). Constantinople: Kontoskalion naval base on the Marmara side, arsenal
   "surrounded by walls", sea walls separating basin from city (Wikipedia, Kontoskalion);
   earlier the Neorion on the Golden Horn had a shipyard (Wikipedia, Neorion Harbour).
   Barcelona: Drassanes 1285 at the edge of town, by the shore west of the walls, then taken
   inside the expanded city wall 1328-1390 (Wikipedia, Drassanes Reials). Genoa: galley basin
   (Darsena delle Galee) was the western of two basins, the wine/goods basin the eastern
   (Musei di Genova). Rhodes: an Arsenal Gate opens on the Kolona harbour (Wikipedia,
   Fortifications of Rhodes).

6. **Separate harbours for war and trade are normal.** Genoa: galley basin west, trade basin
   east (Musei di Genova). Rhodes: commercial harbour (Emporium) and military harbour
   (Mandraki), plus Kolona (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Rhodes). Constantinople: trade on the
   Golden Horn (Neorion, Prosphorion), navy at Kontoskalion on the Marmara (Wikipedia,
   Neorion Harbour; Kontoskalion).

7. **The ruler's seat sits either at the high point or at the far tip, away from the land gate.**
   Constantinople: Great Palace beside Hagia Sophia and the Hippodrome at the old eastern
   end, the Mese running from there to the Golden Gate in the land wall (Wikipedia,
   Constantinople). Rhodes: Grand Master's Palace ("Kastello") at the highest point of the
   Street of the Knights, in the walled-off northern Collachium (Google Arts & Culture /
   UNESCO). Monemvasia: fortified acropolis on the plateau about 100 m up, lower town below
   (Wikipedia, Monemvasia). Thessaloniki: acropolis as a separate walled enclosure at the
   north (top) of the circuit, with the Heptapyrgion inside (Wikipedia, Walls of
   Thessaloniki). Acre: Templar fortress at the seaward western tip held 10 days after the
   town fell (Wikipedia, Siege of Acre 1291). Kotor: St John's castle at about 280 m on the
   mountain behind the town (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Kotor).

8. **A castle at the town edge can face inward to control the town.** Saint-Malo's château at
   the north-east corner of the walls was commissioned by Duke John V "for the purposes of
   subjugating his people" (Wikipedia, Château de Saint-Malo). Aigues-Mortes' Tour de
   Constance stands on the west wall near the north-west corner, reached from the château
   courtyard (beyond.fr).

9. **One main street runs across the town between the gates.** Dubrovnik's Stradun, about
   300 m, runs Pile Gate to Ploče Gate on a filled-in marsh channel (Wikipedia, Stradun).
   Thessaloniki's main street (Leoforos / Mesi) ran from the Golden Gate (west) to the
   Cassandreot Gate (east) (Wikipedia, Walls of Thessaloniki). Constantinople's Mese runs
   about seven Roman miles from the Milion to the Golden Gate, with a row of fora along it
   (Wikipedia, Constantinople). Valletta's main streets run from City Gate to Fort St Elmo,
   on a rectangular grid (Wikipedia, Valletta). Monemvasia's lower town has a "Middle Street"
   meeting the path from the upper town at the cathedral square (Wikipedia, Monemvasia).

10. **Foreign merchant quarters are waterfront strips with their own landing stages, and they
    get walled off from each other.** Constantinople: Venetians and Amalfitans west of the
    Neorion, Pisans further west, Genoese (1155) south and east of it, later moved across the
    Horn to Galata (Wikipedia, Neorion Harbour). Acre: the Genoese quarter had its own wall
    with arrow slits, a vaulted gate and a tower, and lay on the border with the Venetian
    quarter near the port (University of Haifa abstract). Tyre: Venice got one third of the
    city in 1124 (adrianjboas.com).

11. **"Never breached" walls are mostly land walls with depth; the sea side is the weak spot.**
    The Theodosian walls were not breached by artillery in 1422 or 1453 (Wikipedia, Walls of
    Constantinople), but in 1204 the Venetians scaled the Golden Horn sea wall at the Petrion
    Gate and the article calls the sea walls "a weak point" (same page). Tyre was the only
    coastal city that held against Saladin in 1187; a narrow isthmus with "strong
    fortifications built across it" is given as the reason (adrianjboas.com). Cádiz fell in
    1596 through its land flank (ISPRS 2020).

12. **Climate building (brief, weak evidence).** Early Byzantine houses in central Syria are
    inward-facing around a courtyard, with thick street walls, few windows and a single door
    (byzantine-world.com). No fetched source covered houses in Constantinople or southern
    France specifically.

### Case studies

**1. Constantinople (Byzantine capital)**
- Neck wall: Theodosian double wall plus moat, about 5.7 km, across the land side (Wikipedia, Walls of Constantinople).
- Gates: 9 main land gates; 14-16 on the Golden Horn; 13 on the Marmara (same).
- Harbour: Golden Horn inlet to the north, chained at its mouth; Neorion and Prosphorion on its south shore; Theodosian (Vlanga), Sophiae/Kontoskalion and the Boukoleon palace harbour on the Marmara (same; Wikipedia, Neorion Harbour; Kontoskalion).
- Arsenal: Kontoskalion on the Marmara, walled, with chain and iron gates; earlier a shipyard at the Neorion (Wikipedia, Kontoskalion; Neorion Harbour).
- Palace: Great Palace on the south side of the Augustaion, next to Hagia Sophia and the Hippodrome, at the far (east) end from the land wall; it had its own harbour (Boukoleon) (Wikipedia, Constantinople; Walls of Constantinople). Later Blachernae palace at the land-wall end (12th c.) (Wikipedia, Constantinople; position at the north-west end not stated there, unverified).
- Market / axis: the Mese, a colonnaded main street, from the Milion to the Golden Gate, with fora along it (Wikipedia, Constantinople).
- Quarters: Venetian, Amalfitan, Pisan, Genoese strips on the Golden Horn shore around the Neorion; Genoese later at Galata across the Horn (Wikipedia, Neorion Harbour).

**2. Dubrovnik (Ragusa)**
- Neck wall: land side up to 25 m high and 4-6 m thick; moat at Pile (Wikipedia, Walls of Dubrovnik).
- Gates: Pile (west, land), Ploče (east, land), Ponte and Fishmarket (harbour) (same).
- Harbour: on the east side, sheltered by the Kaše breakwater (1485), chain to St John fortress (same).
- Outworks: Bokar covers Pile, Revelin covers Ploče, Lovrijenac on a rock outside the west wall, Minčeta at the high landward corner (same).
- Market / axis: Stradun, about 300 m, gate to gate; the town plan was redone after the 1296 fire (Wikipedia, Stradun; Dubrovnik).
- Palace, cathedral, Sponza: named on the Dubrovnik page but positions not described there: not found.

**3. Venice**
- No land wall; the lagoon is the defence (no fetched page describes medieval lagoon defences: not found).
- Arsenal: walled basin in Castello, eastern side, 45 ha, about 20 minutes from St Mark's; Porta Magna (1460) and two 14th-century towers at the canal entrance (Wikipedia, Venetian Arsenal; thewingedsandals.com).
- Government: Doge's Palace on Piazza San Marco (Wikipedia, Venice, captions only).

**4. Rhodes (Knights' town)**
- Walls: 4 km, 11 gates, terrepleins in the moat after 1480 (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Rhodes; Medieval City of Rhodes).
- Harbours: commercial (two towers, incl. the 46 m Naillac Tower), military Mandraki (St Nicholas fort), Kolona reached through the Arsenal Gate (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Rhodes).
- Citadel/palace: Grand Master's Palace at the highest point of the Street of the Knights, in the northern Collachium, once walled off from the Lower Town (Google Arts & Culture / UNESCO).
- Quarters: Street of the Knights lined with the inns of the seven "tongues" (national branches of the Order) (search snippet, unverified).

**5. Valletta (founded 1566)**
- Site: Sciberras peninsula between Grand Harbour and Marsamxett (Wikipedia, Valletta).
- Neck wall: bastioned land front with four bastions, two cavaliers, a deep ditch (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Valletta).
- Gates: City Gate (Porta Reale) on the land front (same).
- Tip fort: Fort St Elmo commands both harbour entrances (same).
- Plan: rectangular grid; main streets from City Gate to Fort St Elmo; Grand Master's Palace in the city centre (Wikipedia, Valletta).
- Arsenal: not covered by fetched pages: not found.

**6. Monemvasia**
- Site: rock island, 400 m tombolo to the mainland (Wikipedia, Monemvasia).
- Upper town: fortified acropolis on the plateau, Hagia Sophia near the cliff edge, entered by a gate on a winding path (same).
- Lower town: on the south shore under the upper town's wall, walled on east, south, west; west gate, east gate, sea gate ("portello") (same).
- Market/axis: Middle Street meets the path from the upper town at Elkomenos Christos square, site of the metropolitan church (same).
- Harbour: near the west gate, across the causeway (same).

**7. Kotor**
- Site: triangle between bay (south-west), river (north) and mountain (east) (Wikipedia, Fortifications of Kotor).
- Walls: 4.5 km, 2-16 m thick, up to 20 m high, climbing to St John's castle at about 280 m (same).
- Gates: Sea Gate (from the bay), River Gate, Gurdić Gate (same).

**8. Thessaloniki**
- Walls: 4 km survive; sea wall demolished in the late 19th c. (Wikipedia, Walls of Thessaloniki).
- Citadel: separately walled acropolis at the north, with the Heptapyrgion (same).
- Axis: main street from the Golden Gate (west) to the Cassandreot Gate (east) (same).
- Harbour: Constantine's artificial harbour at the south-west end of the sea wall (thebyzantinelegacy.com in search snippet, unverified).

**9. Acre (Crusader)**
- Walls: inner and outer land wall, moat in front of the outer; 12 towers; separate walled suburb Montmusard (Wikipedia, Siege of Acre 1291).
- Citadel: Templar fortress with four towers at the seaward western tip (same).
- Harbour: chained (Wikipedia, War of Saint Sabas); chain between the Tower of Flies and a mole (search snippet, unverified).
- Quarters: Genoese quarter walled with its own gate and tower, bordering the Venetian quarter near the port (University of Haifa abstract); Venetian quarter under today's Khan el-Faranji (romeartlover.it); Hospitallers north and north-east of the port (romeartlover.it).

**10. Saint-Malo**
- Site: granite island joined by a narrow sand spit, the Sillon (Wikipedia, Saint-Malo).
- Castle: north-east corner of the walls, built to control the townspeople (Wikipedia, Château de Saint-Malo). Whether this corner is where the Sillon lands: not stated, not found.

**11. Aigues-Mortes**
- Planned rectangle, about 500 x 300 m, 1,640 m circuit, about 10 gates, corner towers; Tour de Constance at the north-west, outside the rectangle's line on the west wall (beyond.fr; Wikipedia, Aigues-Mortes).
- Built as a royal port by Louis IX from 1240; walls 1272 onward (Wikipedia, Aigues-Mortes).

**12. Cádiz**
- Peninsula with one land access: a coast-to-coast wall across the isthmus, early 16th c.; attacked through that flank in 1596 and rebuilt (ISPRS 2020).

### Layout rules a generator can use

Strength: **strong** = 4+ towns on fetched pages agree; **medium** = 2-3 towns; **weak** = 1 town or snippet only.

| Rule | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| The land-neck wall is thicker and higher than the sea walls; it can be doubled with a moat in front. | Constantinople, Dubrovnik, Acre (double wall + moat), Cádiz (wall coast to coast) | strong |
| Land gates are few: 1-2 on a short neck (Dubrovnik, Cádiz), more on a long land wall (Constantinople 9 over 5.7 km). Sea gates outnumber land gates. | Dubrovnik 2+2, Constantinople 9 land vs 27+ sea, Cádiz 1, Monemvasia | medium |
| Each land gate gets a flanking tower or a detached outwork in front of it. | Dubrovnik (Bokar, Revelin) | weak |
| The harbour mouth is narrowed by a mole or two towers and closed with a chain. | Constantinople, Dubrovnik, Kontoskalion, Acre, Rhodes (towers) | strong |
| War harbour and trade harbour are separate basins. | Genoa, Rhodes, Constantinople | medium |
| The arsenal is a walled rectangle of long parallel sheds on its own basin, at the town edge, with one water entrance between two towers. | Venice (walls, towers at canal), Barcelona (walled, 4 towers, 8 naves), Kontoskalion (walled) | medium |
| Arsenal size scale: 8 sheds about 8 m x 60 m (Barcelona) up to 45 ha / 15 % of the city (Venice). | Barcelona, Venice | medium |
| The palace or citadel sits at the high point or the far tip, far from the land gate, so it is the last place to fall. | Constantinople, Rhodes, Monemvasia, Thessaloniki, Acre, Kotor, Valletta (St Elmo) | strong |
| A lord's castle may instead sit on the town wall corner, with its own defences facing the town. | Saint-Malo, Aigues-Mortes | medium |
| One main street links the land gate to the far end (tip fort, palace or other gate); market squares and fora sit along it. | Constantinople (Mese), Dubrovnik (Stradun), Thessaloniki, Valletta, Monemvasia | strong |
| A planned (founded) town uses a grid; a grown town uses one spine plus side lanes. | Valletta, Aigues-Mortes (grid) vs Dubrovnik, Constantinople | medium |
| On a steep site the town splits into a lower town by the water and a walled upper town / acropolis above, linked by one path. | Monemvasia, Kotor, Thessaloniki, Rhodes (Collachium) | strong |
| An elite quarter (knights, court) can have its own inner wall separating it from the commoners' town. | Rhodes (Collachium), Acre (Genoese quarter wall) | medium |
| Foreign merchant quarters are narrow strips along the harbour shore, each with its own landing stage, side by side, and may be walled off from each other. | Constantinople, Acre, Tyre | medium |
| The sea wall is the weak side in practice; famous "unbroken" walls are land walls with depth (double wall + moat). | Constantinople 1204 vs 1422/1453, Tyre 1187, Cádiz 1596 | medium |
| Houses face inward around a courtyard; thick street walls, few windows, one door. | Early Byzantine Syria only | weak |

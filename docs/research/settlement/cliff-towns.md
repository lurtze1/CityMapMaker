# Research: cliff and multi-level towns (pre-1900)

Research report for CityMapMaker. Real towns only; nothing here is about the campaign city.
Every claim carries the URL that was fetched for it. "(Wikipedia)" marks Wikipedia sources.
"(travel source)" marks commercial or tourism pages, weaker than heritage or scholarly sources.
"(unverified)" marks a claim seen only in a search-result summary, not in a fetched page.

## Cliff and multi-level towns

### Findings

1. **Split by height is common and has a social order.** Rocamadour's three levels are
   described as "the knights above, ... religious clerics in the middle and the lay workers down
   near the river" ([Rocamadour, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocamadour)).
   Quebec's Upper Town was the military and administrative centre (fort, seminary, convents,
   later city hall); its Lower Town held merchants, artisans and the port
   ([Old Quebec, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Quebec)). Matera's high ground,
   the Civita, holds the cathedral and administrative buildings; the cave districts (Sassi) sit
   below on either side ([Matera, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matera)).
2. **What goes up:** castle or citadel, main church, government, refuge space, cisterns.
   Monemvasia's upper town had the fortress, Hagia Sophia, public and administrative buildings
   and three large reservoirs ([Monemvasia, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia)).
   Gozo's Cittadella held the cathedral, governor's palace, bishop's palace and was the refuge in
   raids; islanders had to sleep inside until 1637
   ([Cittadella, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cittadella_(Gozo))).
3. **What goes down:** harbour, fishermen, merchants, warehouses, the market street, the poor.
   Bonifacio's lower town "la marine" holds the harbour facilities
   ([Bonifacio, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio,_Corse-du-Sud)). Alfama,
   on the slope between castle and river, "became inhabited by the fishermen and the poor"
   (Wikipedia flags this as citation needed)
   ([Alfama, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfama)). Clovelly's quay is at the
   bottom of the cliff, 120 m below the village
   ([Clovelly, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovelly)).
4. **Upper towns can die first.** Monemvasia's upper town was abandoned after the second
   Venetian occupation while the lower town stayed lived-in
   ([Monemvasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia)). Gozo's citadel lost its role when
   the 1637 curfew was lifted and Rabat grew below
   ([Cittadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cittadella_(Gozo))).
5. **Connections between levels are few, and each type serves a different user.**
   - Winding road for carts: Quebec's Côte de la Montagne, laid out c. 1620, climbs Cap Diamant
     "in a winding fashion" ([Côte de la Montagne, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_de_la_Montagne)).
   - Stairs for people: Quebec's Breakneck Stairs (1635, 59 steps) branch off that road
     ([Breakneck Stairs, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakneck_Stairs)).
   - Mechanical lift, late: Quebec funicular 1879; Valletta's Barrakka lift 1905
     ([Old Quebec Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Quebec_Funicular),
     [Barrakka Lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrakka_Lift), both Wikipedia).
   - Rock-cut stairs to a water source: Bonifacio's King of Aragon stairs, 189 steps down the
     cliff to a spring ([fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalier_du_roi_d%27Aragon)).
   - Single fortified path: Monemvasia's upper town is reached by one "winding path" with two
     intermediate gates ([Monemvasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia)).
   - Tunnel through the cliff to a harbour: Sark's Creux Harbour (1588), Cove Harbour in
     Berwickshire (55 m tunnel) ([sark.co.uk](https://sark.co.uk/history/historical-buildings-sites),
     [coldingham.info](https://coldingham.info/sp_coastalpath.html)).
   - Sled and donkey street: Clovelly's cobbled street; goods moved by donkeys, now sledges
     ([Clovelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovelly)).
6. **Cliffs replace walls.** Quebec's walls (4.6 km) encircle only the western, landward end of
   the Upper Town; the other sides are protected by "steep cliffs with a 90-metre-drop"
   ([Quebec Ramparts, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Ramparts)). Monemvasia's
   upper town is walled on north and south only; "the other sides are steep enough to need no
   walls" (paraphrase of the fetched summary) ([Monemvasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia)).
   Gibraltar: "The sheer cliffs on the north and east sides of the Rock block access from those
   directions" ([Fortifications of Gibraltar, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Gibraltar)).
   Lindos: "The walls and towers follow the natural conformation of the cliff"
   ([Lindos, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindos)).
7. **Land walls are thicker than sea walls.** Dubrovnik: land side 4–6 m thick, sea side
   1.5–5 m ([Walls of Dubrovnik, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik)).
8. **Sea gates sit at the harbour, at the bottom.** Dubrovnik has two harbour gates (Ponte 1476,
   Fishmarket Gate 1381) beside the arsenal ([Walls of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik)).
   Monemvasia's lower town has a sea gate, the "portello"
   ([Monemvasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia)). Gibraltar's port area had a Land
   Gate, a Sea Gate and a Barcina Gate ([Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Gibraltar)).
9. **One way in is the defensive ideal.** Acoma (1540 report): "only one entrance by a stairway
   built by hand" ([Acoma Pueblo, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoma_Pueblo)).
   Mont-Saint-Michel: the only access to the town was barred by the Porte du Roy
   ([fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Saint-Michel)). The Moorish kasbah of
   Gibraltar had a single gate ([Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Gibraltar)).
10. **Streets on slopes.** Edinburgh: one main street on a ridge crest, with "narrow closes
    (alleyways), often no more than a few feet wide," leading steeply downhill to north and south
    ([Old Town, Edinburgh, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Edinburgh)).
    Matera: about twelve levels over roughly 380 m, linked by paths, stairways and shared
    courtyards (vicinati); some streets run on top of the houses below
    ([Matera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matera)). Positano: "vertical village" with staircases
    linking upper districts to the valley ([Positano, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positano)).
    General rule: towns built by muscle power have "streets that climb at a steady angle by
    following the contours of the land" ([J. Crawford, carfree.com](https://carfree.com/design/stcu100.html)).
11. **Rock-cut and cave architecture.** Matera's Sassi are houses "dug into the calcarenitic
    rock" ([Sassi di Matera, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassi_di_Matera)).
    Uçhisar's 60 m rock "castle" is riddled with rooms and passages; perhaps ~1,000 people lived
    in it; a ~100 m tunnel under the town probably linked castle and water supply
    ([Uçhisar, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C3%A7hisar)). Derinkuyu goes down
    ~85 m, closes with rolling stone doors, each floor separately
    ([Derinkuyu, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derinkuyu_underground_city)).
    Cliff Palace sits in a cliff alcove: 150 rooms, 23 kivas
    ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/historyculture/cliff_palace.htm)).
12. **Bridges and vaults create hidden levels.** Edinburgh's South Bridge (1785–88) is a
    19-arch viaduct over the Cowgate gorge; its arches enclose about 120 vaults (2–40 m²) used
    as workshops, taverns, then slums ([South Bridge Vaults, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bridge_Vaults)).
13. **Caves as harbours or gates (closest real analogues to a cliff-face cave gate).**
    - Sark, Creux Harbour: entrance "blasted through the rock in 1588"; main entrance and harbour
      wall 1866 ([sark.co.uk](https://sark.co.uk/history/historical-buildings-sites)).
    - Cove Harbour, Berwickshire: harbour hidden from the village, reached by a 55 m tunnel that
      once held cellars for fish; tunnel dated 1750s (page also says c. 1700; conflict)
      ([coldingham.info](https://coldingham.info/sp_coastalpath.html)).
    - Tintagel: Merlin's Cave, a ~100 m natural sea tunnel under the castle island, impassable
      at high tide; the sheltered cove beside it, behind the "Iron Gate" rock wall, may have been
      the 5th–7th century landing place ([Merlin's Cave, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin%27s_Cave)).
    - Bonifacio: rock-cut stair ends at a grotto with a spring, 15 m above sea level
      ([fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalier_du_roi_d%27Aragon)).
    - Gibraltar: siege tunnels cut into the Rock from 1779–83; 1,200 m by 1790
      ([Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Gibraltar)).
14. **Vertical towns (building up, not across the slope).** Shibam: ~500 mudbrick tower houses,
    5–11 storeys, one or two rooms per floor, some over 30 m, inside a wall; height explained as
    defence against Bedouin raids ([Shibam, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibam)).
    Sana'a: ~6,500 multi-storey houses, clay walls 9–14 m high
    ([Old City of Sana'a, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_of_Sana%27a)).
15. **Ridges carry walls in big hill cities.** Genoa's 17th-century Mura Nuove (19.56 km, 7 km
    on the coast) run along two hill ridges meeting at Fort Sperone
    ([Walls of Genoa, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Genoa)).

### Case studies

**1. Bonifacio (Corsica).** Levels: upper town on a promontory ~70 m up; lower town "la marine"
along a fjord-like drowned ravine (1.5 km long). Up: citadel (9th century), most residents,
buildings "on the very lip of the precipice". Down: harbour. Connections: the rock-cut King of
Aragon stair (189 steps, 53 m) leads to a spring in a grotto, not to the harbour; the main
town-to-marina route was not found. Defences: citadel and cliff-top walls; the narrow inlet
shields the harbour. Sources: [en.Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio,_Corse-du-Sud),
[fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalier_du_roi_d%27Aragon).

**2. Monemvasia (Greece).** Levels: upper town on a plateau ~100 m up; lower town on the slope
below the upper town's south wall, down to the sea. Up: fortress, Hagia Sophia, public buildings,
three big cisterns. Down: dense 3-storey houses with narrow fronts, metropolitan church on the
central square, mosque, bishop's palace; vaulted covered passages (*dromi*). Connections: one
winding path with two intermediate gates. Defences: upper town walled north and south only;
lower town walled on three sides; west gate to causeway and harbour, sea gate (*portello*), east
gate. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monemvasia).

**3. Quebec (Upper and Lower Town).** Levels: Upper Town on Cap Diamant; Lower Town at its
foot by the river; 90 m cliff. Up: fort (1608), seminary, convents, Hôtel-Dieu, later city hall,
officials and clergy. Down: Champlain's 1608 habitation at Place Royale, merchants, artisans,
port basins, Notre-Dame-des-Victoires. Connections: winding road (c. 1620), Breakneck Stairs
(1635), funicular (1879). Defences: 4.6 km of walls only on the landward west; cliffs elsewhere;
gates where roads cross the wall (Prescott Gate sits on the road from the Lower Town). Sources:
[Old Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Quebec),
[Quebec Ramparts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Ramparts),
[Côte de la Montagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_de_la_Montagne),
[Breakneck Stairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakneck_Stairs),
[Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Quebec_Funicular) (all Wikipedia).

**4. Valletta and the Grand Harbour (Malta).** Levels: grid city on a peninsula ridge (city
elevation 56 m); harbour quays below on both sides. Up: planned grid of wide straight streets from
City Gate to Fort St Elmo. Down: wharves. Connections: Barrakka lift (1905, 60 m) from the
Grand Harbour wharf up to the bastion gardens; older stair streets not found in fetched pages.
Defences: bastions on the harbour faces; landward Floriana Lines. Sources:
[Valletta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valletta), [Barrakka Lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrakka_Lift).

**5. Rocamadour (France).** Levels: three. Bottom: village by the river with fortified gates
(Salmon, Cabilière, de l'Hôpital, du Figuier). Middle: sanctuaries half-way up the cliff, with a
terrace where pilgrims gathered. Top: castle "built ... to defend the sanctuaries". Connections:
a monumental pilgrims' stair of 216 steps to the sanctuaries; a way of the cross to the castle.
Sources: [en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocamadour), [fr](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocamadour).

**6. Mont-Saint-Michel (France).** Levels: rock 92 m; abbey church on the summit on crypts;
village "in the shadow of its abbey" at the foot. Connections: one gated entry (Porte du Roy,
plus an outer Porte de l'Avancée of 1534); tidal causeway until 1879. Defences: 1417 wall with
six towers enclosing the lower town; the tide itself. Sources:
[fr.Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Saint-Michel),
[en.Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Saint-Michel).

**7. Matera, Sassi (Italy).** Levels: Civita (central high ground) with cathedral and
administration; two cave districts (Sasso Barisano, Sasso Caveoso) on the ravine slope; ~12
levels over ~380 m. Houses dug into soft rock; streets on roofs of lower houses; links by paths,
stairs and shared courtyards; large cisterns (Palombaro Lungo, 1832). Sources:
[Matera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matera), [Sassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassi_di_Matera).

**8. Edinburgh Old Town.** Levels: castle on a volcanic crag; one main street down the ridge
tail to Holyrood; Cowgate in the valley to the south; Nor Loch (man-made) to the north.
Connections: narrow steep closes off both sides of the main street; 18th-century bridges over
the valleys with vaults inside. Plots: house on the street front, yard and workshops behind,
reached by closes (search summary of PSAS / Old Edinburgh Club papers, unverified). Defences:
crag, loch and marsh, town wall with gates ("ports"). Sources:
[Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_Edinburgh),
[South Bridge Vaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bridge_Vaults).

**9. Dubrovnik (Croatia).** Levels: from the harbour up the slope; Fort Lovrijenac outside on a
37 m rock. Defences: 1,940 m of wall up to 25 m high; land wall 4–6 m thick versus sea wall
1.5–5 m; two land gates (Pile, Ploče) each with an outwork (Bokar, Revelin); two harbour gates
next to the arsenal. Stair streets off the main street: not found in fetched pages. Source:
[Walls of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik).

**10. Gibraltar.** Levels: town on the lower north-west slope; Moorish castle above it as the
final redoubt with one gate; sheer 400 m cliff on the north; cliffs on north and east "block
access". Defences: land front facing the isthmus (Landport Gate with drawbridge over a ditch,
Grand Battery); sea-side batteries; port gates (Land, Sea, Barcina); siege tunnels cut into the
rock from 1779. Source: [Fortifications of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Gibraltar).

**11. Acoma Pueblo (New Mexico).** Levels: mesa top 111 m above the plain. On top: rows of
3-storey adobe blocks facing south, plaza, kivas, rock cisterns (no spring). Connections in 1540:
one hand-built stairway (~200 + ~100 steps) then holes in the rock. Defences: one way up, stone
piles to roll down, ladders instead of ground-floor doors. Source:
[Acoma Pueblo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoma_Pueblo).

**12. Fira (Santorini).** Levels: town on the rim of a ~400 m caldera cliff; old port (Gialos)
at the cliff foot. Connections: zigzag footpath on foot or donkey; now also a cable car.
Sources: [Fira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fira), [tideandland (travel)](https://www.tideandland.com/the-old-port-of-fira).

**13. Cittadella, Gozo (Malta).** Levels: citadel on a defensible hill; suburb Rabat below,
with its own wall and three gates. Up: cathedral, governor's and bishop's palaces, refuge (6,000
people in 1551). Connection: one main gate; original access a ramp with drawbridge. Defences:
medieval wall on the plateau edge to the north, bastioned front to the south, ditch, ravelin.
Source: [Cittadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cittadella_(Gozo)).

**14. Cliff-tunnel harbours: Sark (Creux) and Cove (Berwickshire).** Levels: settlement on
the cliff top; harbour in a cove below, invisible from above. Connection: a tunnel through the
rock (Sark 1588; Cove 55 m, 1750s, with fish cellars inside). These are the clearest real cases of
a cliff-face opening working as the gate to the harbour. Sources:
[sark.co.uk](https://sark.co.uk/history/historical-buildings-sites),
[coldingham.info](https://coldingham.info/sp_coastalpath.html).

### Layout rules a generator can use

Strength: **strong** = several independent towns agree; **medium** = two or three cases;
**weak** = one case or a general claim.

|---|---|---|---|

| R2 | Harbour, warehouses, fishermen, merchants, artisans go on the lowest level by the water. | Quebec, Bonifacio, Alfama, Clovelly, Fira | strong |
| R3 | Walls are built only where there is no cliff; a cliff edge counts as a wall. | Quebec, Monemvasia, Gibraltar, Lindos | strong |
| R4 | The landward wall is thicker and has the main gates and outworks; sea walls are thinner. | Dubrovnik (4–6 m vs 1.5–5 m); Gibraltar land front | medium |
| R5 | Harbour gates sit at the foot, next to the quay or arsenal. | Dubrovnik, Monemvasia, Gibraltar | medium |
| R6 | The defended top has 1 approach (sometimes with intermediate gates); a bigger civil town has 2–3 links of different types. | Monemvasia, Acoma, Mont-Saint-Michel, Gozo vs Quebec | medium |
| R7 | Link types by user: winding road for carts (gentle grade), stairs for people (steep), rock-cut stair or path for water, tunnel to a hidden cove. Lifts only after ~1879, so not for a pre-1900 fantasy look unless chosen. | Quebec, Bonifacio, Sark, Cove, Valletta | medium |

### Rendering notes

- **Hachures:** short strokes drawn in the direction of steepest slope; steeper = thicker,
  shorter, denser; flat ground left blank. Standardised by Lehmann in 1799
  ([Hachure map, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachure_map)). Good fit for a
  hand-drawn look and computable from a slope field.
- **Hill profiles (molehills):** side-view hills, "the most ancient form of relief depiction";
  contour lines date from 18th-century France
  ([Terrain cartography, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain_cartography)).
- **Bird's-eye views and prospects:** Braun and Hogenberg's *Civitates orbis terrarum*
  (1572–1617/18) has 546 prospects, bird's-eye views and plans of cities
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitates_orbis_terrarum)). Merian's *Topographia*
  series ran 1642–c.1660s in 38 parts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographia_Germaniae)).
  Neither fetched page describes how they drew cliffs.
- **Cliffs:** no fetched source describes a historical convention for drawing cliffs. The only
  related convention found: Ordnance Survey cuttings and embankments drawn as long triangles,
  short base at the top ([Hachure map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachure_map)).
- **Suggested, not sourced:** stairs as short parallel ticks across a path; tunnels as dashed
  lines; levels distinguished by tone. These are design ideas, not findings.

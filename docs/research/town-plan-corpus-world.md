# World town-plan corpus: every culture and era

> Written 2026-10-19 by seven research agents, one per region, and assembled by Claude. A
> claim here is binding only when a DECISIONS.md entry cites it. Population figures are
> approximate; see the conventions below.

Research report, 2026-10-19. Purpose: extend `town-plan-corpus.md` (fifteen European and
Near-Eastern towns, almost all drawn 1560-1650) to the rest of the world and to every era from
the first cities to about 1900, so that the expressive-range harness (REQUIREMENTS.md §7) can be
checked against the real range of settlement shapes, not against one region's habits. The
first corpus answers "what does a late-medieval European town look like"; this one answers
"what else can a town look like".

Terms used throughout: *expressive range* is the set of outputs a generator can produce
(LEARNING.md); a *cul-de-sac cluster* is the Islamic-city street pattern of a few through-routes
with blind lanes branching off them; a *cosmological grid* is a planned grid whose axes and
gate counts follow a religious rule rather than a defensive or commercial one; a *citadel +
town* enclosure means the ruler's compound has its own wall separate from the town wall; a *D
on water* is a wall ring whose straight side is a river or shore; a *bastioned trace* is the
star-shaped gunpowder-era wall with projecting bastions.

Conventions:

- **Every URL was fetched on 2026-10-19** by the agent that found it and returned the expected
  Wikimedia Commons file page, Gallica or Library of Congress record. Pixel sizes and licence
  tags are as the host states them. "(unverified)" marks a fact the agent could not check in
  this session, usually a population or a wall length taken from general literature.
- **Plan types**, in order of preference: 1 a historic surveyed plan or bird's-eye view;
  2 an archaeological excavation plan; 3 a named scholar's reconstruction; 4 a modern aerial
  photograph or survey of a still-legible core. Types 3 and 4 are used only where no type 1 or
  2 image exists; each entry says which it is.
- **Bird's-eye views are pictures, not surveys**: gate counts, wall shape and the relative
  position of palace, temple, market and water are reliable; street widths and block counts
  are indicative.
- **Geometry figures** (areas, wall lengths, block sizes) come from the plan plus standard
  references; none were re-measured in this session.
- The fifteen towns of the first corpus are not repeated: Dublin, Galway, Visby, Bergen,
  Shrewsbury, Durham, Caernarfon, Edinburgh, Nuremberg, Nördlingen, Luxembourg, Siena, Toledo,
  Monpazier, Damascus. Where §9 cites one of them as the type case it is marked (C1).

## 2. Ancient world, c. 3500 BCE to c. 500 CE

Scope: Mesopotamia, Egypt, Anatolia, Aegean, Indus, Greek and Roman worlds. Every URL below was
fetched (HTTP 200, expected file title) on 2026-10-19 unless marked "(unverified)". Pixel sizes
are the "original file" sizes the Commons page states. Licence is the tag the host page shows;
where a scan of a public-domain book carries a CC tag from the uploader, both are given.
The slice has 16 written-up towns, two above the brief's ceiling, because three of the
candidate list turned out to be the only open plan of their type (Nippur, Dholavira, Kahun) and
dropping any of the rest would lose a distinct geometry. Four further verified plans are listed
without a block at the end of Part B, and the candidates with no usable open image are in
"Not found".

### Part B

#### Nippur, c. 1400 BCE (clay tablet, Kassite period; photo Hilprecht 1903)
- Type: religious capital of Sumer (temple of Enlil) on a branch of the Euphrates; Kassite period.
- Second, higher-resolution image of the same object, museum photo 2019/2022: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stadtplan_Nippur_Jena.jpg (4000 x 3000, CC BY 4.0 / GFDL; Hilprecht Collection, Jena).
- Enclosure: one ring, drawn as an irregular quadrilateral with the wall thickness shown; the tablet writes wall lengths in cuneiform. Enclosed area of the real site about 135 ha (unverified).
- Gates: seven, each named on the tablet, spread along the wall; no bridge drawn.
- Street pattern: not drawn; the tablet shows only walls, watercourses, enclosures and gardens.
- Key places: Ekur temple enclosure (Enlil) marked as a large rectangle in the eastern half on the high ground; a second enclosure (Ekiur); a park/garden strip along the inside of the wall.
- Water: the Euphrates runs along the west side; a canal (Nunbirdu) crosses the city centre and a moat follows the wall; the city is split by the canal into two halves.
- Terrain: flat alluvium; temple on the highest mound.
- Suburbs: none drawn.
- Adds: the oldest surveyed city plan in existence, with named gates and a canal cutting the city in two; a wall ring that is a true irregular quadrilateral, not a circle or D.

#### Ur, c. 2000 BCE (Woolley excavation plan, LOC photo 1898-1946; Zunkir simplified plan 2011)
- Type: river-canal port and temple city, capital of the Ur III dynasty.
- Enclosure: one ring, oval (about 1,200 x 800 m, about 60 ha, unverified), with a walled temenos (ziggurat precinct) inside at the north-west.
- Gates: not securely known; two harbours (north and west) open the ring to the river channel.
- Street pattern: organic, dense courtyard houses with winding lanes (Woolley's "AH" and "EM" quarters), no grid.
- Key places: ziggurat and temenos on the highest ground at the north-west edge, not the centre; royal tombs beside the temenos; harbours at the north and west rims; no separate market area identified.
- Water: a Euphrates channel along the west and canal basins forming the two harbours; the city was effectively an island between river and canal.
- Terrain: flat alluvial mound.
- Suburbs: unknown.
- Adds: an oval ring with internal harbour basins and a walled sacred precinct off-centre, with organic housing; the earliest "citadel off-centre + organic town" case.

#### Babylon, c. 600 BCE (livius.org reconstruction after Koldewey; 1905 plan of ruins from a 1904 Leipzig source)
- Type: imperial capital on both banks of the Euphrates, Neo-Babylonian.
- Enclosure: several rings. Inner city: double wall (Imgur-Enlil and Nemet-Enlil) forming a near-rectangle about 2.5 x 1.5 km on both banks, about 400 ha on the east bank (unverified); an outer triangular wall of Nebuchadnezzar II to the east enclosing a further large area with the Summer Palace at its northern tip.
- Gates: eight named in the inner wall (Ishtar, Marduk, Zababa, Enlil, Urash, Shamash, Adad, Sin); one stone-piered bridge over the Euphrates joining the two halves.
- Street pattern: straight main streets parallel to the river and the Processional Way, with right-angle crossings in the excavated Merkes quarter (a loose grid, not uniform); dense courtyard houses between.
- Key places: Southern Palace and Ishtar Gate at the northern edge of the inner city; Etemenanki ziggurat and Esagila (Marduk temple) at the centre of the east bank, at the end of the Processional Way; no identified market.
- Water: Euphrates through the middle; moats along the walls; canal (Libil-hegalla) crossing the city.
- Terrain: flat alluvium.
- Suburbs: the outer wall encloses a large thinly built zone; villages on the mounds in 1905.
- Adds: a city split by a major river with a bridge, nested walls of different shapes (rectangle inside a triangle), and a palace on the edge with the temple at the centre.

#### Dur-Sharrukin (Khorsabad), built 717-706 BCE (Botta and Flandin 1849)
- Type: planned royal capital of Sargon II, abandoned at his death.
- Enclosure: citadel + town. One near-square ring about 1,760 x 1,635 m (about 290 ha), corners to the cardinal points; a raised walled citadel straddling the north-west wall with the palace terrace projecting outside the line; a second walled enclosure (arsenal, "Palace F") on the south-west wall.
- Gates: seven, two on each of three sides and one on the north-west side beside the citadel; all plan-regular; no bridge.
- Street pattern: unknown inside, mostly never built up; the plan is the enclosure and the two walled compounds.
- Key places: ruler's seat on the citadel at the edge, on an artificial terrace, half outside the wall; temples inside the citadel; no market.
- Water: river Khosr close by, outside the wall; no watercourse inside.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds: the purest square planned enclosure in the corpus with the citadel breaking the wall line; a city that is wall and citadel with an empty interior.

#### Nineveh, c. 700-612 BCE (Felix Jones survey 1852, published 1855, BnF Gallica)
- Type: imperial capital on the Tigris with two citadel mounds; Jones's sheet also shows the walled Ottoman Mosul on the opposite bank, which is a useful second town on the same sheet.
- Enclosure: one ring about 12 km long, irregular elongated trapezoid about 5 x 2 km, about 750 ha; two citadel mounds (Kuyunjik, Nebi Yunus) set on the western wall line.
- Gates: fifteen per Sennacherib's inscription; Jones marks the surviving gate breaks; no bridge inside the city.
- Street pattern: unknown except for a royal road from the Nergal Gate; Jones shows only the wall and mounds.
- Key places: palaces on Kuyunjik at the west edge on the river; arsenal on Nebi Yunus further south on the same edge; temples on Kuyunjik; no identified market.
- Water: Tigris along the west side (now shifted), the Khosr river passing through the middle of the city and splitting it north/south; moats and a second outer rampart on the east.
- Terrain: flat plain with two artificial mounds.
- Suburbs: unknown.
- Adds: a very large irregular ring with two citadels on one edge and a river running through the middle; the first 19th-century measured survey of a Near-Eastern capital at a scale that shows wall breaks.

#### Kahun (El-Lahun), c. 1895 BCE (Petrie 1890 plan)
- Type: planned town for the builders and priests of the pyramid of Senwosret II.
- Enclosure: one ring, rectangle about 384 x 335 m (about 13 ha, unverified); an internal wall divides a small western quarter from a large eastern quarter.
- Gates: not certain; the plan shows an opening on the south side; no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid. The western quarter has about 220 small houses in rows on straight east-west lanes; the eastern quarter has a single wide east-west street with very large courtyard mansions (about 2,400 m2 each) along its north side, and smaller blocks south of it. Blocks of small houses about 10 x 20 m per unit.
- Key places: an "acropolis" (large building on a raised rock) at the north-west corner of the eastern quarter; no temple inside (the valley temple lies outside); no market.
- Water: none inside; desert edge at the Fayum entrance; a central stone drain down the main street.
- Terrain: flat desert with a rock knoll at the north-west.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds: a class-segregated grid in one rectangle: rows of tiny houses on one side of a wall, a few palace-sized mansions on the other; the smallest fully planned town in the corpus.

#### Amarna (Akhetaten), c. 1346-1332 BCE (Prisse d'Avennes 1878; modern map 2007)
- Type: new royal capital, built and abandoned within about 15 years; cosmological alignment on the Aten and the cliff "horizon".
- Enclosure: none. The city is bounded by the Nile on the west and a cliff arc on the east; boundary stelae mark the limits.
- Gates: none; no bridge over the Nile; a bridge over the Royal Road joined the Great Palace to the King's House.
- Street pattern: linear spine. The Royal Road runs north-south about 6 km parallel to the river; the Central City has large orthogonal compounds; the North and Main (south) suburbs are organic clusters of large estates with small houses packed around them; the Workmen's Village in the eastern desert is a walled grid of about 70 houses.
- Key places: Great Aten Temple and Great Palace in the Central City on the spine, roughly at mid-length; King's House, Small Aten Temple and record offices beside them; North Palace and North Riverside Palace at the north end; Maru-Aten at the south end; no identified market.
- Water: Nile on the west, the whole city on the east bank; desert wadis.
- Terrain: flat desert bay between river and cliffs.
- Suburbs: the suburbs are most of the city; the planned Central City is a small part of the whole.
- Adds: an unwalled linear city on a single spine with the royal/temple zone in the middle and organic suburbs north and south; the one case with no enclosure at all.

#### Hattusa, c. 1650-1200 BCE (Humann 1882 survey; Simon 2020 phase sketches after Schachner)
- Type: hilltop imperial capital with citadel; Hittite.
- Enclosure: citadel + town, several rings: Büyükkale citadel on a rock spur; the Lower City wall; the Upper City wall added later, with the earthen Yerkapi rampart; total about 180 ha.
- Gates: Lion Gate (south-west), King's Gate (south-east), Sphinx Gate on Yerkapi with a postern tunnel, plus posterns and the Lower City gates; no bridge.
- Street pattern: organic, following ridges and terraces; the Upper City is a field of about 30 temples with roads between; no grid.
- Key places: palace on Büyükkale, a rock citadel at the junction of the lower and upper cities; Great Temple (Temple 1) in the Lower City on the north, lower, side; no market identified.
- Water: no river inside; gorges with streams on the east (Büyükkaya across the gorge) and springs/ponds inside the Upper City.
- Terrain: steep slope, rising about 300 m from the Lower City to Yerkapi in the south; rock outcrops used for citadel and sanctuaries.
- Suburbs: Büyükkaya and Osmankayasi outside the main walls, small.
- Adds: a wall circuit on steep ridges with a citadel on a spur between two walled districts, a temple quarter on the high side and the main temple on the low side; the opposite of the flat Mesopotamian cases.

#### Tiryns, c. 1400-1200 BCE (Schliemann and Dorpfeld excavation plan 1884, published 1885)
- Type: Mycenaean fortified citadel on a low rock above a coastal plain, with a lower town.
- Enclosure: citadel + town. The citadel is a narrow oblong about 300 x 45-100 m on a rock 18 m high, divided into upper, middle and lower citadel by cross walls; Cyclopean walls up to 8 m thick with corbelled galleries; a lower town around the rock (about 25 ha, unverified) with its own later wall.
- Gates: one main gate on the east reached by a ramp along the wall, one postern on the west with a stair; no bridge.
- Street pattern: not applicable inside the citadel (a sequence of gates, courts and the megaron); the lower town is organic.
- Key places: palace megaron in the upper (south) citadel, approached through two propylons and a court; cult rooms in the palace; storage and refuge in the lower (north) citadel; no market.
- Water: the Argolic Gulf was about 1 km away in the Bronze Age; underground passages to springs outside the wall.
- Terrain: isolated low rock in a flat plain.
- Suburbs: the lower town is larger than the citadel.
- Adds: a citadel that is a linear sequence of gated terraces on a narrow rock, with the ruler's seat at the far end from the gate; the smallest "citadel + town" case.

#### Dholavira, c. 2600-1900 BCE (ASI layout, SVG by Yodaspirine 2020)
- Type: planned Harappan city on an island in the Rann of Kutch, with monumental water storage.
- Enclosure: several nested rings, all rectangles with cardinal orientation: the outer wall about 771 x 617 m (about 47 ha); inside it a "castle" (citadel) and a "bailey" side by side on the south, a walled Middle Town north of them, and an unwalled Lower Town to the east; a ceremonial ground about 283 x 47 m between castle and Middle Town.
- Gates: the castle has one gate per side (four); the Middle Town has gates on its north and east; the outer wall's gates are not all located.
- Street pattern: grid inside the castle and Middle Town, with streets meeting the gates; the Lower Town less regular.
- Key places: ruler's seat in the castle at the south edge, on the highest ground; ceremonial ground beside it, not in the centre; no temple identified; no market identified.
- Water: no river; two seasonal streams (Manhar, Mansar) dammed on either side, feeding 16 reservoirs that ring the inside of the outer wall and take about 10 per cent of the area.
- Terrain: flat with a gentle slope from the castle down to the north.
- Suburbs: a small settlement and cemetery outside the west wall.
- Adds: enclosure rings nested side by side rather than concentrically, and a water system that is a visible part of the plan; a rectangular "citadel in the corner" layout.

#### Miletus, after 479 BCE, drawn as of the Hellenistic period (von Gerkan 1924)
- Type: peninsula harbour city rebuilt on a Hippodamian grid after the Persian destruction.
- Enclosure: one ring (Hellenistic wall of the 4th century BCE) around the peninsula, with the sea on three sides; about 100 ha inside (unverified); the archaic city and Kalabaktepe hill lie outside to the south-west.
- Gates: the Sacred Gate in the south wall (start of the Sacred Way to Didyma) and a west gate; harbours: Lion Harbour on the north, Theatre Harbour on the west, and smaller bays on the east; no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid in two systems with different block sizes: north about 30 x 52 m, south about 42 x 70 m (unverified), meeting at the agora zone; streets run straight to the water on all sides.
- Key places: public centre (North Agora, bouleuterion, South Agora about 196 x 164 m, Delphinion) between the two grids, at the neck of the peninsula next to the Lion Harbour; theatre on the hill on the west; no ruler's seat (a polis).
- Water: sea and harbours on three sides; the Maeander later silted the bays.
- Terrain: low peninsula with a theatre hill on the west and Kalabaktepe outside.
- Suburbs: archaic settlement and necropolis outside the wall to the south-west.
- Adds: a grid with two block modules stitched together, with the civic centre at the seam and harbours on three sides; the type case for "grid on a peninsula".

#### Priene, founded c. 350 BCE (von Gerkan 1924)
- Type: small Ionian grid city laid on a steep slope under the Teloneia acropolis.
- Enclosure: one ring about 2.5 km long enclosing about 37 ha, climbing from the lower terrace to the cliff and including the acropolis rock 370 m above the plain.
- Gates: East Gate (main), West Gate, a north-east spring gate; no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid; about seven east-west streets follow the contours, north-south lanes are stairways; blocks about 35 x 47 m (120 x 160 Attic feet), four houses per block.
- Key places: agora in the middle of the grid on a levelled terrace; Temple of Athena on the terrace above and north-west of the agora; theatre and upper gymnasium higher up; lower gymnasium and stadium at the bottom inside the wall; the acropolis as refuge, empty of buildings.
- Water: none inside; the Maeander plain (a sea bay in antiquity) below the south wall; springs on the mountain fed the city.
- Terrain: steep slope under a cliff; terraces.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds: a grid forced onto a slope so that cross-streets become stairs, with the civic centre at mid-slope and the stadium at the bottom edge; a wall that climbs to include a bare acropolis.

#### Paestum (Poseidonia), founded c. 600 BCE, walls 4th-3rd century BCE (Delagardette 1798/99)
- Type: Greek colony on a coastal plain, later a Latin colony; Delagardette's plate 3 is a surveyed plan with the three temples and the wall circuit.
- Enclosure: one ring about 4.75 km long, an irregular rounded pentagon, about 120 ha; towers along the wall.
- Gates: four, one in the middle of each side (Porta Marina west, Porta Sirena east, Porta Aurea north, Porta Giustizia south); no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid of long strips ("per strigas"): three wide east-west streets and north-south lanes dividing blocks about 35 x 300 m (unverified).
- Key places: the sanctuaries occupy the central north-south strip: Hera temples at the south, Athena temple at the north, with the agora (later forum) between them; no ruler's seat; forum central.
- Water: sea about 1 km to the west; the Salso river just south of the wall.
- Terrain: flat travertine plain.
- Suburbs: necropolises outside the gates; small.
- Adds: a rounded wall ring with four axial gates around a grid of very elongated blocks, and a continuous sacred strip through the centre; the type case for "temples on the central axis".

#### Pompeii, as of 79 CE (Eschebach 1:5000, redrawn as layered SVG 2009)
- Type: walled market town on a lava spur above the Sarno mouth; Oscan, Samnite then Roman.
- Enclosure: one ring, about 3.2 km, irregular oval, about 66 ha; twelve towers.
- Gates: eight (Marina, Ercolano, Vesuvio, Capua, Nola, Sarno, Nocera, Stabia); no bridge.
- Street pattern: an irregular old core ("Altstadt") around the forum in the south-west, then grid additions of different orientations and block sizes: Regio VI has long narrow blocks about 35 x 70-90 m; the eastern regions have larger blocks; two decumani and one main cardo.
- Key places: forum with Capitolium, basilica and markets in the south-west core, not in the centre; theatres and Triangular Forum on the south edge; amphitheatre and large palaestra in the south-east corner against the wall; no ruler's seat.
- Water: the Sarno south of the town; the coast about 1 km west in 79 CE; aqueduct and castellum aquae at the highest point by Porta Vesuvio.
- Terrain: a lava plateau sloping about 40 m from north to south, with cliffs on the south and west sides.
- Suburbs: villas and tomb streets outside Porta Ercolano and Porta Nocera; the suburb at Porta Marina with the Suburban Baths; small compared to the core.
- Adds: a town with three street systems at different angles inside one ring, forum off-centre in the old core and the arena in a corner; the best-documented block sizes in the corpus.

#### Timgad (Thamugadi), founded 100 CE; 1904 state (Haverfield 1913; Ballu 1904)
- Type: Roman veteran colony with a square grid core and larger unplanned suburbs.
- Enclosure: the original colony is a square about 355 x 325 m (about 12 ha), its wall later removed; the suburbs are unwalled; a Byzantine fort (539 CE) south of the core.
- Gates: four in the original square at the ends of the cardo and decumanus; the west gate replaced by the Arch of Trajan; no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid of about 11 x 12 blocks, blocks about 20 x 20 m (70 Roman feet), colonnaded cardo and decumanus; the suburbs continue the orientation loosely, then bend.
- Key places: forum at the centre of the square, south of the decumanus, with the theatre immediately south of it; Capitolium outside the original square to the south-west, in the suburb; markets and large baths in the suburbs; no ruler's seat.
- Water: no river; aqueduct and fountains; a wadi to the south.
- Terrain: flat with a slight slope, in the Aures foothills.
- Suburbs: by the 3rd century larger than the core, roughly 3-4 times its area (unverified).
- Adds: a tiny perfect grid with the forum dead centre, outgrown by suburbs that hold the main temple; shows a core-and-overflow shape the 15 existing towns do not.

#### Dura-Europos, founded c. 300 BCE; plan of the Roman-period city (Detweiler/Yale 1930s, Baird)
- Type: Seleucid fortress colony on the Euphrates, later Parthian caravan city and Roman garrison town.
- Enclosure: citadel + town. The city sits on a plateau bounded by the Euphrates cliff on the east and wadis on the north and south; a wall with towers closes the west (desert) side and runs along the wadis; about 70 ha (unverified); a citadel on the cliff edge at the north-east, partly fallen into the river.
- Gates: Palmyrene Gate on the west (main), a river gate below the cliff, a small north gate; no bridge.
- Street pattern: grid of blocks about 35 x 70 m set out from the Palmyrene Gate; the main street runs from the gate east to the agora; the northern third was taken over by the Roman military camp.
- Key places: citadel and earlier palace on the cliff edge (north-east corner); agora in the centre on the main street; temples spread along the west wall and near the gates (Bel, Adonis, Atargatis); synagogue and Christian house-church against the west wall; palace of the Dux Ripae at the north edge above the river.
- Water: Euphrates below the cliff on the east; wadis on two sides; no watercourse inside.
- Terrain: flat plateau top with a cliff on one side and wadi ravines on two sides.
- Suburbs: none; necropolis outside the west wall.
- Adds: a grid on a cliff-bounded plateau where the wall is only needed on one side, with the citadel in a corner and the civic centre mid-grid; the only Hellenistic-grid-with-citadel case.

### Part B

#### Bibracte, 1876 (Jacques-Gabriel Bulliot; depicts c. 100 BC)
- Region / culture: Mont Beuvray, Burgundy; Gaulish oppidum of the Aedui, abandoned for Augustodunum (Autun) c. 15 BC. Population 5,000-10,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: Iron Age hilltop oppidum, tribal capital and craft centre.
- Enclosure: two rings of murus gallicus (timber-laced stone rampart). Outer rampart c. 7 km long enclosing c. 200 ha; inner rampart c. 5 km enclosing c. 135 ha. Both follow the contour of the summit, so the shape is an irregular lobed oval, not a geometric figure.
- Gates: about 15 openings in the ramparts; the main gate is the Porte du Rebout on the north-east, an inturned-entrance type 20 m wide. No bridges; no river.
- Street pattern: organic. One main spine runs from the Porte du Rebout south-west up the valley to the central plateau (Pâture du Couvent). Craft quarters (metalworking at Côme Chaudron and Champlain) sit just inside the main gate; elite houses and a later Roman-style domus on the plateau.
- Key places: no ruler's seat identified; a sanctuary and basin at the centre; the craft and market zone sits at the gate, not the centre. The spring "Fontaine Saint-Pierre" is inside the walls.
- Water: none navigable; springs inside the enclosure. Terrain: hilltop at 800 m with steep flanks; the interior is sloping with a central plateau.
- Suburbs: none; all settlement is inside the outer rampart. The plan also shows the "Camp de la Garenne" and tumuli outside.
- Adds: pre-Roman, pre-grid, non-urban-looking city: two concentric contour ramparts with ~15 gates, a very large sparsely built enclosure, the busiest quarter at the gate rather than the centre. The best model for a "hill fort capital".

#### Hedeby (Haithabu), board plan photographed 2009 (depicts c. 900-1050)
- Region / culture: head of the Schlei fjord, Schleswig; Danish Viking-age emporium, 8th century to 1066. Population 1,000-1,500 (unverified).
- Settlement type: Viking trading emporium on an inlet, with a separate royal hill fort.
- Enclosure: one semicircular earth rampart, c. 1.3 km long, enclosing c. 24 ha; the open side faces the Haddebyer Noor (water) on the east. The rampart ties into the Danevirke frontier wall. A small "Hochburg" hill fort stands outside to the north.
- Gates: three openings, north, west and south; the harbour is the fourth, water-side, entrance. Jetties and a semicircular palisade in the water close the harbour.
- Street pattern: plank-paved lanes run perpendicular to a small stream that crosses the site from west to east into the Noor; plots are fenced rectangles 10-20 m wide with gable to the lane. Hedeby is the earliest European example in this corpus of regular fenced plots.
- Key places: no ruler's seat inside (the hill fort is outside); the harbour and jetties are the heart of the town; the church (Ansgar's, c. 850) is not located archaeologically; cemeteries lie outside the rampart to the south and west.
- Water: inlet of a fjord; town faces it; the stream was canalised with timber.
- Terrain: flat shore rising gently westward to the rampart.
- Suburbs: none; graves and the hill fort are the only extramural features.
- Licence note: the image is a photograph of a public information board; the photo is CC BY-SA 3.0, the board is covered by German freedom of panorama. Resolution is low (1,268 x 1,092).
- Adds: a D-shaped enclosure open to water (the 15 existing towns all have a closed circuit), a stream as the street-axis generator, and a rampart with three land gates plus a harbour entrance.

#### Trelleborg (Slagelse), aerial photo 2008 (depicts c. 980)
- Region / culture: west Zealand, Denmark; Harald Bluetooth's ring fortresses. Garrison 500-800 (unverified).
- Settlement type: geometric military ring fort; included as the only strictly circular planned settlement in this slice.
- Enclosure: circular rampart, inner diameter 136 m (c. 1.5 ha inside), earth and timber, with a ditch; an outer ward with a second, lower rampart on the landward side.
- Gates: four, at the cardinal points, joined by two axial timber-paved streets that cross at the centre; a ring street runs inside the rampart.
- Street pattern: cosmological grid in its simplest form: a cross inside a circle. The four quadrants each hold four bow-sided longhouses (29.4 m long) set around a square courtyard, 16 houses in all. Fifteen more longhouses stand radially in the outer ward.
- Key places: no temple, market or ruler's house distinguished; a single larger house in the north-east quadrant is read as a hall. Cemetery in the outer ward.
- Water: on a promontory between the Tude and Vårby rivers, so water closes two sides.
- Terrain: flat promontory.
- Suburbs: the outer ward is the only extramural zone, about equal in area to the ring.
- Adds: perfect circle, exactly four gates, two axial streets and courtyard blocks; the hard end of the "planned" axis that the generator must be able to reach.

#### Constantinople, c. 1450 copy (Cristoforo Buondelmonti, drawn 1422)
- Region / culture: Bosporus; the Late Byzantine capital thirty years before the Ottoman conquest. Population 40,000-50,000 (unverified), inside walls built for 400,000.
- Settlement type: imperial capital on a triangular peninsula.
- Enclosure: one continuous circuit: the double Theodosian land wall (c. 6.5 km, moat, outer and inner wall) on the west side, single sea walls along the Marmara (south) and the Golden Horn (north). Area inside c. 1,400 ha. Galata/Pera across the Golden Horn has its own wall and the Galata Tower; Buondelmonti draws it as a second town. A chain closed the Golden Horn.
- Gates: the plan shows about ten land gates and many sea gates; the Golden Gate at the south-west corner is the ceremonial entrance. No bridge; boats cross the Horn.
- Street pattern: the colonnaded Mese runs from the Milion at the east tip westward, forks at the Philadelphion into branches to the Golden Gate and the Charisian Gate; otherwise organic. By 1422 most of the interior is fields and villages between the monuments.
- Key places: Hagia Sophia, the Hippodrome and the Great Palace are at the eastern tip on the acropolis (edge, high ground); the Blachernae palace, the seat in 1422, is in the north-west corner against the land wall; harbours on the Marmara shore (Theodosian harbour) and along the Golden Horn; the forums (markets) are strung along the Mese.
- Water: sea on two sides, a defensible inlet on the third; cisterns and the Valens aqueduct supply the interior.
- Terrain: seven hills, ridge along the Golden Horn, slopes down to the Marmara.
- Suburbs: Galata (walled, Genoese) across the Horn; villages along the Bosporus.
- Companion for the early Ottoman city (c. 1535): Giovanni Andrea Vavassore's woodcut
  bird's-eye view, 1,800 x 1,267, PD-Art,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giovanni_Andreas_Vavassore,_Byzantivm_sive_Costantineopolis.jpg
  (verified by the Islamic-world agent).
- Adds: a capital that is mostly empty inside an oversized wall; palace at the tip rather than the centre; two walled towns facing each other across a harbour; a double land wall on one side only.

#### Venice, 1500 (Jacopo de' Barbari)
- Region / culture: Venetian lagoon; the Republic at its peak. Population 100,000-115,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: lagoon island city, maritime capital, no wall.
- Enclosure: none; the lagoon and its shoals are the defence. About 118 islets joined by c. 400 bridges in 1500 (unverified count), occupying c. 500 ha.
- Gates: none. Bridges: the Rialto (then a timber drawbridge) is the only crossing of the Grand Canal in 1500.
- Street pattern: organic parish clusters, each island a parish with its campo (square) and church, well and cistern; the Grand Canal's reversed-S is the main street; alleys (calli) are 1-3 m wide; blocks are irregular, 30-80 m.
- Key places: Doge's Palace and St Mark's on the south-east edge facing the Bacino (edge, water front); the Rialto market and bridge at the geometric centre; the Arsenal (walled shipyard, c. 24 ha) on the east edge; the Giudecca island to the south across its own canal.
- Water: canals are the streets; the town has no "relation to" water, it is water.
- Terrain: flat, below 2 m.
- Suburbs: Murano, Giudecca and the lido islands; de' Barbari draws them at the margins. Ratio to the core: small.
- Adds: canals as the street network, zero wall and zero gates, ruler's seat on the waterfront edge, a walled industrial enclave inside an unwalled city.

#### Amsterdam, 1544 (Cornelis Anthonisz)
- Region / culture: Holland under Charles V; a herring and Baltic-grain port. Population 12,000-14,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: dammed-river port, D-shape with the flat side on the IJ.
- Enclosure: one ring: brick wall with round towers and a moat, the Singel on the west and south, the Kloveniersburgwal and Geldersekade on the east; the north side is the open IJ waterfront with a timber palisade of posts in the water. Enclosed area c. 100 ha (unverified).
- Gates: Haarlemmerpoort (north-west), Regulierspoort (south), Sint Antoniespoort (east), plus water gates; the Damrak and Rokin are entered through sluices in the dam. Many small bridges inside; no bridge across the IJ.
- Street pattern: linear spine: the Amstel runs north through the centre, split by the Dam into the Damrak (harbour side) and the Rokin; parallel canals (Oudezijds and Nieuwezijds Voorburgwal and Achterburgwal) make a set of long thin blocks c. 40-60 m wide, several hundred metres long, running north-south.
- Key places: Dam at the centre with the town hall, weigh house and Nieuwe Kerk; Oude Kerk in the east half; ships unload in the Damrak, inside the wall; no ruler's seat (a republican port).
- Water: river dammed at the centre; canals parallel to it; the harbour is the river mouth inside the wall.
- Terrain: flat, reclaimed peat.
- Suburbs: the Lastage shipyard quarter outside the east wall, about a fifth of the core; this is the extramural zone that was walled in during the 1585 extension.
- Adds: canal-parallel long blocks, a harbour running into the centre of the town, a D-shape open to water, a market square on a dam. The 1660s canal ring is the natural follow-on if a later date is wanted.

#### Palmanova, 1598 edition (Braun & Hogenberg; depicts the 1593 foundation)
- Region / culture: Friuli; Venetian frontier fortress against the Habsburgs and Ottomans. Population 2,000-3,000, mostly garrison (unverified).
- Settlement type: planned star-fort garrison town, built in one go from 1593.
- Enclosure: one ring: nine-pointed star of bastions (the ravelins and outer lunettes came in the 1600s and 1800s), a wet moat, roughly 1.3 km across between bastion tips; enclosed area c. 50 ha (unverified).
- Gates: three, Porta Udine (north-west), Porta Cividale (north-east), Porta Aquileia (south), each on an alternate side of the nonagon so that no gate faces another. Bridges over the moat at each gate.
- Street pattern: radial-concentric: six radial streets from the central hexagonal Piazza Grande, three to the gates and three to bastions, cut by two ring streets. Blocks are wedge-shaped, c. 60-90 m deep.
- Key places: the cathedral and the governor's house face the central piazza; the market is the piazza; barracks line the ring streets inside the rampart. No harbour.
- Water: none except the moat and a canal to the Roggia; flat alluvial plain.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: none; building outside the glacis was forbidden.
- Adds: strictly radial plan, odd gate count on a 9-fold symmetry, everything important at the exact centre; the extreme "ideal city" the generator must be able to produce when a rule pack asks for it.

#### Zamosc, c. 1600-1618 (Braun & Hogenberg, vol. VI)
- Region / culture: Lublin region; private town of Chancellor Jan Zamoyski, designed 1580 by Bernardo Morando of Padua. Population 3,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: Renaissance ideal city as a magnate's residence, academy and trading town, founded on an empty site.
- Enclosure: one ring: bastioned trace with seven bastions forming an irregular heptagon-pentagon on the terrain, a wet moat fed by the Labunka river on the west and south; enclosed area c. 24 ha.
- Gates: three, Lublin (north), Lwow (east, two gates in succession) and Szczebrzeszyn (west, within the palace sector). The three gates are the ends of the two main streets.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid oriented on the long east-west axis (Grodzka street) that runs from the palace to the Lwow gate; blocks c. 60 x 80 m; arcaded fronts on the squares.
- Key places: the palace at the west end on the axis (edge, not centre); the Great Market (100 x 100 m) with the town hall on its north side at the centre; the Collegiate church south-west of the market; the Academy north-west; two secondary markets (Salt and Water) north and south of the Great Market, giving three squares in a line. Jewish and Armenian quarters on the north and east.
- Water: river diverted to feed the moat and a mill pond on the west side; the town does not open onto it.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: none inside the glacis; villages beyond.
- Adds: the palace-at-one-end axis, three market squares on a grid, a grid inside a bastioned polygon with gates at street ends. Compare Monpazier (grid, no palace) and Luxembourg (fortress, no grid) in the existing corpus.

#### Moscow, 1610 (Abelin and Kilian, "Sigismund plan")
- Region / culture: Muscovy during the Time of Troubles; the plan is the latest of the early-17th-century Moscow plans and the most detailed. North is at the right. Population 80,000-100,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: concentric-ring capital on a river bend, a kremlin at its core.
- Enclosure: four rings. (1) Kremlin: triangular brick wall, c. 2.2 km, 28 ha, 20 towers. (2) Kitai-gorod: brick wall of the 1530s enclosing c. 60 ha east of the Kremlin. (3) Bely Gorod: white-stone wall of the 1580s, a semicircle of c. 9 km closing on the Moskva river at both ends. (4) Skorodom (Zemlyanoy Gorod): earth rampart with timber wall of the 1590s, c. 15 km, a full ring that crosses the river and encloses Zamoskvorechye on the south bank. Total enclosed c. 1,800 ha (unverified).
- Gates: the Kremlin has five gate towers; Kitai-gorod six; Bely Gorod about 10; Skorodom about 34 (unverified counts, read from the plan). Bridges: a pontoon bridge below the Kremlin to Zamoskvorechye; no stone bridge until 1692.
- Street pattern: radial: main roads (to Tver, Smolensk, Serpukhov, Vladimir) run from the Kremlin gates out through all rings; the rings themselves carry ring streets. Blocks are irregular, defined by fenced timber-house plots; wooden log pavements.
- Key places: ruler's seat, cathedrals and the Patriarch's court inside the Kremlin on the high bank at the junction of the Neglinnaya and Moskva; Red Square and the trading rows (market) between the Kremlin east wall and Kitai-gorod; the Streltsy (musketeer) suburb and the royal garden across the river to the south; no harbour, river quays only.
- Water: Moskva river curves along the south side of the core; the Neglinnaya skirts the Kremlin west wall and feeds the moat; the Yauza enters on the east.
- Terrain: Kremlin on a 40 m bluff (Borovitsky hill), the rest on gentle slopes to the river.
- Suburbs: the outer rings are the suburbs; outside Skorodom only monasteries and villages.
- Adds: four nested enclosures with a triangular citadel at the junction of two rivers; dozens of gates; ring crossing a river to take in the far bank; radial roads; timber-plot blocks.

#### Carcassonne, 1650 (attributed to Nicolas Desjardins)
- Region / culture: Languedoc; royal frontier fortress of the Capetians after 1226, by 1650 a garrison town in decline. Cite population under 2,000; Bastide Saint-Louis larger (unverified).
- Settlement type: double-walled hilltop cite with a separate planned bastide on the opposite river bank; the 1650 plan shows both.
- Enclosure, Cite: two concentric rings: inner wall (Gallo-Roman and 12th-13th century, c. 1.1 km, 26 towers), outer wall (1230s-1280s, c. 1.5 km, 19 towers) with a lices (open ground) between them, dry moat beyond. Enclosed area inside the inner wall c. 7 ha (unverified). The Chateau Comtal is a separate ward with its own moat against the west side of the inner wall.
- Gates, Cite: two, Porte Narbonnaise (east, twin towers and barbican) and Porte d'Aude (west, down a ramp to the river); two posterns (Saint-Nazaire south, Rodez north). One stone bridge (Pont Vieux, 14th century) crosses the Aude between the Cite and the bastide.
- Enclosure, Bastide Saint-Louis: one ring, rectangular with corner bastions by 1650, on the flat west bank; grid of c. 50 x 50 m blocks around a central market square with a church (Saint-Vincent, Saint-Michel) on the corners of the grid.
- Street pattern, Cite: organic with two cross streets from gate to gate; blocks 20-40 m.
- Key places, Cite: castle on the west edge against the wall (edge, high ground); cathedral Saint-Nazaire inside the south wall (edge); the market square (Place Marcou) near the centre. The river harbour is at the bastide, not the Cite.
- Water: the Aude between the two towns; the Cite does not touch it. Terrain: Cite on a 150 m hill, bastide on the flat plain.
- Suburbs: the bastide is larger than the Cite by 1650; the suburb of Trivalle on the slope below the Porte d'Aude.
- Adds: two walls with a lices between, castle as a ward inside the ring, and a twin town (hill fort plus grid on the flat) across a river. Caernarfon and Monpazier each give one half of this; Carcassonne gives both in one map.

#### Neuf-Brisach, c. 1697 project plan (Vauban)
- Region / culture: Alsace; Louis XIV's replacement for Breisach after the 1697 Treaty of Ryswick. Population 2,000 civilians plus garrison (unverified).
- Settlement type: octagonal Vauban fortress town built on empty ground, 1698-1703.
- Enclosure: one ring: regular octagon of eight bastioned towers (tours bastionnees), tenailles, eight counterguards and ravelins beyond, and a dry ditch; inner square of the town c. 600 x 600 m (c. 36 ha, unverified) inside a defensive belt of c. 1,200 m across.
- Gates: four, Strasbourg (north-east), Colmar (west), Bale (south) and Belfort (south-west), each reached by a bridge across the ditch and a ravelin.
- Street pattern: square grid of 48 blocks (6 x 8) c. 50 m square, two axial streets crossing at the central Place d'Armes (c. 90 x 90 m); the perimeter street inside the rampart separates the blocks from the barracks.
- Key places: church (Saint-Louis), governor's house and arsenal on the central square; barracks in the rampart ring; market on the square; no ruler's seat (a royal garrison).
- Water: none in the plan beyond the ditch; the Rhine is 3 km east; a canal (Canal Vauban) brought stone from the Vosges.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds: square grid inside an octagon, four gates at the midpoints, every block equal; the simplest fully planned military town, useful as a calibration baseline. Image is small (965 x 883) but the geometry is legible. The Musee des Plans-Reliefs 1706 model on Commons (File:Neuf-Brisach - plan-relief 1706.jpg) is a photograph of the same layout.

#### Karlsruhe, 1739 (Johann Matthias Steidlin after Christian Thran)
- Region / culture: Margraviate of Baden-Durlach; founded 1715 by Margrave Karl Wilhelm. Population 3,000 (unverified).
- Settlement type: Baroque residence town with a fan plan radiating from the palace.
- Enclosure: none; the town had no wall. A circular boundary wall surrounds the palace garden only.
- Gates: none in the fortification sense; the radial avenues end at the open country. No river, no bridges.
- Street pattern: radial: 32 avenues radiate from the palace tower; the nine southern rays form the town, the 23 northern rays are forest rides. The town fills a circular sector between the palace and the straight east-west Lange Strasse, which cuts all the rays; blocks are wedges c. 50-80 m wide, widening outward. A concentric circular street (Zirkel) runs through the palace forecourt.
- Key places: the palace at the centre of the circle, on the edge of the built town (ruler's seat on the edge, as the generator); the market square and church on the central ray south of the palace; no harbour.
- Water: none; the Rhine is 8 km west.
- Terrain: flat forest plain.
- Suburbs: none in 1739; Durlach, the old residence, is 5 km east.
- Adds: a radial town occupying only one sector of its circle, with the ruler at the apex; no wall and no gates; a straight cross street truncating the fan. Shows the "palace generates city" rule in its purest form.

#### Mont-Saint-Michel, 1777 (Plans des ports de France, author unknown)
- Region / culture: Normandy-Brittany border; Benedictine abbey, royal fortress and prison in 1777. Village population a few hundred (unverified).
- Settlement type: abbey on a tidal rock with a single-street pilgrim village.
- Enclosure: citadel plus town: the abbey on the summit (80 m) is its own fortified enclosure; the village below is walled on the south and east sides only (ramparts of the 1400s with seven towers), the north and west being sheer rock. Village area c. 4 ha (unverified). Island circumference c. 960 m.
- Gates: one entrance sequence on the south: Porte de l'Avancee, Porte du Boulevard, Porte du Roi, one behind the other. No bridge; the 1777 plan shows the island reached across the sands at low tide (the causeway dates from 1879).
- Street pattern: linear spine: the Grande Rue climbs from the gate along the east flank in a spiral to the abbey stair; houses on both sides in a single row; a parallel rampart walk; stairs as side streets.
- Key places: abbey church and monastery on the summit (high ground, edge); the parish church Saint-Pierre half-way up the street; the "market" is the street itself; boats beach below the south wall at the gate.
- Water: tidal flats on all sides; a harbour only in the sense of a landing on the sand.
- Terrain: conical island; the town is a 30-degree slope.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds: a one-street spiral town on a cone, a wall on two sides only, a single stacked gate, and a tide as the water relation. No existing town in the corpus is an island or has fewer than two gates.

#### Bath, 1805 (T. Chantry)
- Region / culture: Somerset; Georgian spa resort at the end of its boom. Population 33,196 (1801 census).
- Settlement type: medieval walled core plus planned Georgian terraces and crescents on a hillside; the river curves round the core.
- Enclosure: one ring around the old core only: the medieval wall on the Roman line, c. 9 ha inside, by 1805 mostly built over but still legible in the street line (Upper and Lower Borough Walls). The Georgian town has no wall.
- Gates: four medieval gates, North, South, West and East (the East gate was a water gate to the river); by 1805 all but the East gate demolished. Bridges: Pulteney Bridge (1774, lined with shops) and the Old Bridge (south); the plan shows Great Pulteney Street running east from the bridge into the Bathwick estate.
- Street pattern: three systems on one map. (1) The core: a loose grid of Roman origin, blocks 60-100 m, with the Abbey and the baths at the centre. (2) The upper town to the north-west: John Wood's sequence of Queen Square (1729), the Gay Street spine, the Circus (1754, three equal crescents on a circle of 97 m diameter) and the Royal Crescent (1767-1775, 30 houses on a half-ellipse 150 m wide) opening to a lawn; later crescents (Lansdown, Camden) step further up the slope. (3) Bathwick across the river: a straight 300 m avenue (Great Pulteney Street) ending in a hexagonal garden, laid out 1788.
- Key places: Abbey, Pump Room and baths at the centre of the core (centre, low ground); the market behind the Guildhall on the High Street; Assembly Rooms between the Circus and the river in the upper town; river quays on the south and east; no ruler's seat.
- Water: the Avon loops round the east and south of the core; hot springs at the centre are the reason for the town.
- Terrain: core on the valley floor; the Georgian town climbs the north slope in terraces; Bathwick on the flat east bank.
- Suburbs: the Georgian terraces are larger in area than the walled core by about four to one.
- Adds: curved and circular residential set pieces (crescent, circus, square-with-garden), a planned extension climbing a slope outside the old wall, a bridge lined with buildings. Nothing in the existing corpus is later than 1650 or has curved planned streets.

#### Baghdad, Round City, 1900 (Guy Le Strange, reconstruction, depicts 762-c. 800)

- Plan: Map II in Le Strange, Baghdad during the Abbasid Caliphate (Oxford 1900), a scholar's reconstruction from al-Ya'qubi and al-Khatib; no surveyed plan of the Round City exists and nothing survives above ground. Treat all geometry as the textual tradition, not measurement.
- Settlement type: cosmological capital, founded 762 by al-Mansur on the west bank of the Tigris.
- Enclosure: several rings. Outer rampart with moat, main wall, then an inner wall round the central court. Shape: a true circle; diameter in the sources about 2.6 km (unverified), giving about 550 ha.
- Gates: four, one per quadrant, each a bent-entrance gatehouse: Kufa (SW), Basra (SE), Khurasan (NE), Syria (NW). Four straight arcaded avenues run from the gates to the centre. Bridge of boats across the Tigris to the east bank at the Khurasan gate side.
- Street pattern: radial. Between the two main walls a ring of residential quarters cut by the four radial avenues and by many blind lanes; the inner circle was an empty esplanade.
- Key places: palace (Golden Gate) and the great mosque dead centre; treasury, chancery, guard and the caliph's family palaces in the inner ring; markets originally inside the arcades, moved outside the walls to Karkh (south) within a few years for security.
- Water: Tigris east of the city, canals (Sarat, Isa) feeding the suburbs; the Round City itself sits back from the river.
- Terrain: flat alluvium.
- Suburbs: Karkh (south) and Harbiya (north) grew to several times the Round City within a generation; the caliphs then moved across the river to Rusafa.
- Adds: the only pure circle with four cardinal gates, radial avenues and a central palace-mosque pair; markets forced outside the wall.

#### Cairo (Le Kaire), 1809 (Simonel, Jomard, Lecesne, dir. Jacotin; Description de l'Egypte)

- Plan: Plan particulier de la ville, 62 x 91 cm, surveyed 1798-1801 by the French expedition's engineers, the first instrument survey of an Islamic metropolis. BnF Gallica scan, 11,922 x 7,794 px. A companion sheet (Plan general de Boulaq, du Kaire, de l'ile de Roudah...) shows the whole Nile bank at 5,208 x 3,885 px.
- Settlement type: river-bank metropolis, capital of Ottoman Egypt.
- Enclosure: citadel plus town. The Fatimid wall (al-Qahira, c. 1.2 x 1 km, about 110 ha, unverified) survives as an inner ring with Bab al-Futuh and Bab al-Nasr (N) and Bab Zuwayla (S); Saladin's longer wall is fragmentary. By 1800 the built-up area is about 700-800 ha (unverified) and mostly unwalled; quarter gates (harat) closed each neighbourhood at night.
- Gates: three monumental gates on the Fatimid wall; dozens of quarter gates. No bridges to the Nile islands at the city itself; a bridge from Roda to Giza is drawn on the general sheet.
- Street pattern: one spine (the Qasaba, Bab al-Futuh to Bab Zuwayla and on south to the citadel), with a dense cul-de-sac cluster either side; blocks irregular, 50-150 m.
- Key places: citadel on a spur of the Muqattam at the SE edge, on high ground; al-Azhar and the great Mamluk mosques along the spine, central; the main markets (Khan al-Khalili) on the spine just north of al-Azhar; the port is Bulaq, 2 km NW on the Nile, a separate town on the general sheet.
- Water: the Khalij canal runs N-S through the west of the city and fills only at flood; the Nile is 1.5-2 km west with Bulaq and Old Cairo (Fustat) as the two river ports; Birkat al-Fil and other seasonal ponds inside the town.
- Terrain: flat, rising to the citadel spur at the SE.
- Suburbs: Bulaq (port, NW) and Old Cairo (S) are separate walled-less towns; vast cemeteries (Cities of the Dead) east and south of the walls are larger than the Fatimid core.
- Adds: a metropolis where the river is 2 km away and reached through a canal and two detached port towns; citadel on an edge spur; cemeteries as a major extramural land use.

#### Fez, 1912 (Nestor Larras, Service geographique de l'Armee, 1:5,700)

- Plan: Plan de Fes, 58 x 80 cm, surveyed at the start of the protectorate; shows Fes el-Bali, Fes el-Jdid and the gap between them before the Ville Nouvelle. BnF Gallica scan, 10,792 x 7,656 px. A 1906 plan (1,147 x 707 px, File:Plan (1906) de Fès montrant l'emplacement du Maristane.png) is smaller.
- Settlement type: twin walled cities in a river valley, religious and craft capital.
- Enclosure: two rings. Fes el-Bali (founded 789-809) is an irregular oval filling the valley bowl, about 280 ha (unverified); Fes el-Jdid (1276, Marinid palace city) is a separate rectangular walled precinct on higher ground to the west, about 90 ha (unverified), itself divided into palace, Muslim town and Jewish Mellah. A wall joins the two.
- Gates: Fes el-Bali has about eight (Bab Bou Jeloud W, Bab Mahrouk NW, Bab Guissa N, Bab Ftouh SE, Bab Jdid S among them); Fes el-Jdid has four to five. Several small bridges over the Oued Fes inside the medina.
- Street pattern: cul-de-sac cluster at its most extreme; two main through-routes descend from Bab Bou Jeloud to the Qarawiyyin and on to Bab Ftouh; blocks 30-80 m.
- Key places: Qarawiyyin mosque and the main souks at the bottom of the bowl, central; the Royal Palace (Dar al-Makhzen) occupies about a third of Fes el-Jdid, on an edge, on high ground; tanneries by the river in the NE.
- Water: Oued Fes flows W to E through the bottom of the bowl and historically divided the Andalusian (east bank) and Kairouan (west bank) quarters, each with its own wall until the 11th century; the river and its channels are mostly built over.
- Terrain: slope. The medina is a bowl; the walls climb the hillsides on both flanks; Fes el-Jdid sits on the plateau to the west.
- Suburbs: Marinid tombs and cemeteries on the northern hill outside the wall; the French Ville Nouvelle begins 2 km SW after 1912.
- Adds: two walled cities side by side with a palace precinct as its own town; a river running through the centre of a medina and dividing it; a bowl-shaped site.

#### Aleppo (Hhaleb), surveyed 1811-1818, published 1839 (author unnamed on file; Paris, Bineteau; 1:16,800)

- Plan: Plan de la ville de Hhaleb et de ses environs, 24 x 21.5 cm, BnF Gallica scan 4,551 x 3,378 px. The survey dates match the residence of the French consul J.-B. L. J. Rousseau (1808-1816), whose unpublished index of names for a plan of Aleppo is also on Commons (12 pages); the file page does not name the author, so I record it as unnamed. A larger four-sheet Ottoman public-works plan (Chartier, Raghid, Bekir, c. 1900, about 4,000 x 4,750 px per sheet) exists on Commons under "Plan general de la ville d'Halep".
- Settlement type: caravan city round a citadel mound.
- Enclosure: citadel plus one ring. The medieval wall is a rough square about 1.5 x 1.2 km (the World Heritage core is given as about 350 ha, unverified); the citadel sits on a 40 m glacis-sided tell just east of centre, with its own moat and a single bridge-ramp.
- Gates: nine named gates: Bab Antakya (W), Bab al-Faraj (NW), Bab al-Nasr (N), Bab al-Hadid (NE), Bab al-Ahmar (E), Bab al-Nayrab (SE), Bab al-Maqam (S), Bab Qinnasrin (SW), Bab al-Jinan (W, river side). River crossings on the Quwayq west of the wall.
- Street pattern: a faint Hellenistic grid survives in the covered souks (a straight 1 km axis from Bab Antakya to the citadel) with cul-de-sac clusters north and south of it; souk blocks about 100 x 50 m.
- Key places: citadel central-east on high ground; Great Umayyad Mosque on the souk axis just west of the citadel, central; souks and khans along the axis, central; no harbour.
- Water: Quwayq river runs N-S outside the west wall with gardens along it; the town is otherwise dry.
- Terrain: flat plateau with the citadel tell as the one height.
- Suburbs: the Christian quarter of Judayda (NW) and the Banqusa and Bab al-Nayrab quarters (N and E) are extramural and together larger than the walled core by 1800.
- Adds: a citadel on an artificial mound in the middle of the town rather than on an edge; a straight covered-market axis inherited from a classical grid inside an otherwise organic medina.

#### Jerusalem, 1865 (Capt. Charles W. Wilson RE, Ordnance Survey of Jerusalem, 1:2,500)

- Plan: the Old City sheet of the first instrument survey, 12,000 x 17,474 px (Nottingham University copy). Shows every building block, cistern and contour; the best open plan of a walled Islamic-era city in this slice.
- Settlement type: walled pilgrimage city on hills.
- Enclosure: one ring. Suleiman's wall of 1535-41 (4 km) encloses an irregular quadrilateral of about 90 ha; the citadel (Tower of David) is built into the west wall at the Jaffa Gate; the Haram al-Sharif platform (about 14 ha) occupies the SE corner with its own walls.
- Gates: seven on the plan: Jaffa (W), New Gate not yet opened (1889), Damascus (N), Herod's (N), St Stephen's/Lions (E), Golden (E, walled up), Dung (S), Zion (S). No river, no bridges.
- Street pattern: two spines inherited from the Roman cardo and decumanus (Damascus Gate to Zion Gate; Jaffa Gate to the Haram) dividing four quarters, each a cul-de-sac cluster; blocks 30-100 m; many streets stepped or vaulted.
- Key places: Haram/Dome of the Rock on the SE edge on the Temple platform; Holy Sepulchre central-NW; citadel on the W edge at the main gate; markets (three parallel covered souks) at the crossing of the spines, central; the governor's seat (Serai) N of the Haram.
- Water: none flowing; cisterns under every house, Pool of Hezekiah and other open pools inside the wall; Gihon spring in the Kidron valley below the east wall.
- Terrain: hilltop ridge sloping east; the Kidron valley (E) and Hinnom valley (S, W) make three sides steep, the north side is the weak flat approach.
- Suburbs: almost none in 1865; the first extramural quarters (Mishkenot Sha'ananim 1860, Russian Compound 1860) are on the 1:10,000 sheet, a few hectares.
- Adds: a quadrilateral wall with a 14 ha sacred platform filling one corner; a citadel at the main gate rather than on a hill; a town on a ridge with no surface water at all.

#### Isfahan, 1840 (Pascal Coste, Monuments modernes de la Perse, published 1867)

- Plan: Plan general d'Ispahan, 1,732 x 2,552 px; a 4,724 x 6,496 px copy of the same plate is at File:19th century map of isfahan.jpg. Coste measured the monuments and sketched the street net; use the axis dimensions, treat the back-street detail as indicative.
- Settlement type: planned imperial axis (Shah Abbas I, from 1598) grafted onto a Seljuk organic core.
- Enclosure: one ring, mostly ruined by 1840: a mud wall with about twelve gates enclosed roughly 3 x 3 km (unverified); the plan shows the wall line with gardens filling most of the enclosed area.
- Gates: about twelve on the old wall (unverified); three monumental bridges over the Zayandeh Rud (Si-o-se Pol 1602, Khaju 1650, Marnan).
- Street pattern: two systems. North-east: the old city round the Friday Mosque, cul-de-sac cluster. South-west: the Safavid plan: the Naqsh-e Jahan maidan (560 x 160 m) and the Chahar Bagh avenue, a straight tree-lined boulevard 4 km N-S crossing the river to the garden suburbs; a 2 km covered bazaar links the old maidan to the new one. Garden blocks along the Chahar Bagh 200-400 m.
- Key places: royal palace precinct (Ali Qapu and the palace gardens) on the west side of the new maidan, between maidan and avenue, central to the Safavid plan but on the edge of the old town; Shah Mosque on the south side of the maidan; Friday Mosque in the old core 2 km NE; bazaar entrance on the north side of the maidan; no harbour.
- Water: Zayandeh Rud river 2 km south of the maidan, crossed by the avenue; canals (madi) feed the gardens.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: New Julfa, the Armenian quarter south of the river, laid out on a grid in 1606, about a quarter of the city; Abbasabad gardens.
- Adds: a 4 km straight ceremonial avenue and a 9 ha rectangular royal square inserted beside an organic city; a planned minority suburb on a grid across the river.

#### Khiva, 1873 (Eugene Schuyler, Turkistan; Commons file title says 1859)

- Plan: "Plan of Khiva" from Schuyler's book, 3,647 x 2,218 px; drawn from the Russian military survey made at the 1873 capture. Shows both walls, the gates, the canal and the main buildings.
- Settlement type: double-walled oasis capital of a khanate.
- Enclosure: two rings. Ichan Kala (inner town): a rectangle about 650 x 400 m, 26 ha, 8-10 m mud-brick walls on a raised platform. Dichan Kala (outer town): an irregular wall about 6 km long enclosing roughly 1.5 x 1 km of houses, gardens and fields.
- Gates: inner town four, one per side (Bakhcha N, Palvan E, Tash S, Ata W), on a single straight E-W street; outer wall ten gates (unverified).
- Street pattern: inner town has one straight spine (Ata gate to Palvan gate) with madrasa and mosque blocks either side and cul-de-sac housing behind; outer town organic with garden plots.
- Key places: Kunya Ark (old citadel, khan's seat) against the inside of the west wall, on an edge; Tash Hauli palace at the east gate; Friday Mosque mid-spine; bazaar and caravanserai at the east gate, spilling outside into the outer town; no harbour.
- Water: Palvan Yab canal from the Amu Darya runs past the east gate; oasis irrigation ditches throughout the outer town.
- Terrain: flat oasis.
- Suburbs: the whole outer town is in effect a walled suburb five times the inner city's area, half of it fields.
- Adds: a strict rectangle with one gate per side and a single through-street; a second, much larger wall enclosing fields; the ruler's citadel pressed against the inside of a wall rather than at the centre.

#### Tunis, 1861 (plan Colin, photographed by Andre Raymond 1958), with 1893 and 1901 plans

- Plans: the 1861 Colin plan (3,903 x 2,543 px, MediHAL, Licence Ouverte) shows the pre-colonial city alone; the 1893 plan (4,653 x 4,547 px, British Library) and the 1901 Depot de la Guerre plan at 1:6,000 (9,112 x 7,136 px, BnF) add the French new town on the reclaimed lake edge. Use 1861 for geometry, 1901 for metric detail.
- Settlement type: medina with two suburbs on a lagoon, capital of the beylik.
- Enclosure: one ring plus two walled suburbs. The central medina (about 1.1 x 0.8 km) and the northern (Bab Souika) and southern (Bab Jazira) rabads are each walled and together form an elongated oval about 270 ha (unverified); the Kasbah is a walled citadel at the highest, western point.
- Gates: the medina proper has Bab el Bhar (Sea Gate, E), Bab Souika (N), Bab Jazira (S), Bab Menara and Bab Jedid (W, SW); the outer wall adds Bab Saadoun, Bab el Khadra, Bab Alioua, Bab el Fellah and others, about a dozen in all. No bridges; a 10 km channel across the shallow lake links the sea gate to the port of La Goulette.
- Street pattern: cul-de-sac cluster; two main routes cross at the Zitouna mosque (Bab el Bhar to the Kasbah, Bab Souika to Bab Jazira); blocks 40-100 m.
- Key places: Zitouna great mosque dead centre with the covered souks wrapped round it in concentric rings (perfumers, cloth, books nearest the mosque); Kasbah (bey's seat and barracks) on the W edge on the highest ground; the Bardo palace 3 km outside; the harbour is La Goulette across the lake, not the town itself.
- Water: Lake of Tunis (shallow lagoon) east of the sea gate; no river; cisterns and aqueduct from Zaghouan.
- Terrain: slope rising from the lake shore (E) to the Kasbah hill (W).
- Suburbs: the two rabads are each about half the size of the core; after 1881 the European town on fill between the sea gate and the lake doubles the city.
- Adds: concentric market rings round a central mosque; citadel on the uphill edge opposite the water; a port town 10 km away across a lagoon.

#### Harar, 1876 (Mokhtar Effendi and A. Faousi Effendi, Egyptian general staff, printed Cairo 1877)

- Plan: Plan de la ville de Harrar, 56 x 35 cm, 7,603 x 9,960 px, made during the Egyptian occupation (1875-85); the first survey of the city and the earliest plan of any walled town in the Horn of Africa.
- Settlement type: walled hilltop trading and religious town (Islam's "fourth holy city" in local tradition).
- Enclosure: one ring. The 16th-century Jugol wall (3.5 km, about 48 ha, UNESCO figure) is an irregular oval following the ridge.
- Gates: five, one per direction of trade: Shoa (W), Buda (SW), Sanga (S), Erer (E), Fallana (N). No river, no bridges.
- Street pattern: cul-de-sac cluster; radial lanes from the five gates meet at the central market square (Feres Magala); 362 named alleys (UNESCO count).
- Key places: Grand Mosque (Jami) and the emir's house near the centre; market square at the centre where the gate-roads meet; 82 mosques and 100 shrines spread through the quarters; no harbour.
- Water: none inside; springs and the Erer stream below the wall to the east.
- Terrain: hilltop ridge at 1,885 m, sloping to the east.
- Suburbs: Oromo villages and markets outside the gates, small; the Egyptian barracks and the later Ethiopian town grow outside the west gate.
- Adds: a five-gate oval on a hilltop with gate-roads converging on a central market; the smallest fully walled town in this slice at under 50 ha.

#### Kano, 1826 (Denham and Clapperton, Narrative of Travels; manuscript copy c. 1836 by Thomas Scott)

- Plans: Clapperton's "Map of the city of Kano" from the 1826 Narrative (760 x 492 px, small but the primary source), and a larger manuscript copy "Plan of the city of Kano, Soudan, ca. 1836" by Thomas Scott in the State Library of New South Wales (2,000 x 1,346 px, CC BY-SA 3.0 AU). Barth's 1851 view from Mount Dala (3,239 x 2,041 px, File:Barth 1857 Kano from Mount Dala.jpg) shows the same layout in elevation.
- Settlement type: giant-walled Sahelian emporium, Hausa city-state capital.
- Enclosure: one ring. A mud wall 11-15 m high, originally 12th-14th century, extended in the 16th; length given between 14 and 24 km by different sources (unverified), enclosing far more land than the houses: Clapperton drew the built area as two clusters inside a wall with fields, ponds and two rocky hills (Dala and Goron Dutse) in the empty northern half.
- Gates: thirteen to fifteen (Kofar Mata, Kofar Nassarawa, Kofar Na'isa, Kofar Kansakali among them); no river inside, so no bridges; the Jakara pond and marsh separate the two built clusters.
- Street pattern: organic; a few broad tracks from gates to the central market; compounds (gida) of 20-60 m with blank outer walls.
- Key places: Kurmi market at the centre of the southern cluster; emir's palace (Gidan Rumfa, a walled compound of about 33 ha) and the Friday mosque beside it, south-central; the hills in the north are quarries and lookouts, not fortified.
- Water: the Jakara pond (a seasonal lake inside the wall) and borrow-pits along the wall; the Kano river is 6 km south, outside.
- Terrain: flat plain with two granite inselbergs inside the wall.
- Suburbs: Fagge, the camel-caravan quarter, outside the north gate; the walled area itself is three-quarters farmland and acts as the suburb.
- Adds: a wall enclosing several times the built-up area with farmland, a lake and two hills inside; a 33 ha palace compound.

#### Timbuktu, plan of 1853 published 1858 (Heinrich Barth), with 1896 plan (Felix Dubois)

- Plans: Barth's woodcut ground plan with an 11-point key (1,974 x 1,716 px); Dubois's 1896 plan after the French occupation (1,426 x 1,873 px). Both are sketches from pacing and compass, not surveys.
- Settlement type: unwalled desert caravan town serving a river port 8 km away.
- Enclosure: none in 1853. An earlier wall had collapsed; Barth draws the edge of the houses as a rough oval about 1 x 0.7 km (unverified) with the ruins of the wall on the north.
- Gates: none standing; the "gate leading to Kabara" in Barth's key is a road, not a structure.
- Street pattern: organic, with a few wide sandy streets converging on the main market and dense cul-de-sac housing between; mud houses in blocks of 30-60 m.
- Key places: three great mosques at the margins: Djinguereber (SW edge), Sankore (N edge), Sidi Yahya (centre-west); main market central; butchers' market separate; the sheikh's house (ruler's seat in effect) in the north-east quarter; no fort.
- Water: no river at the town; the port of Kabara lies 8 km south on a Niger backwater, and in high flood a creek brings small boats to the south edge of the town (Barth's point 11); two wells marked.
- Terrain: flat sand with low dunes.
- Suburbs: tent camps of Tuareg and Berabish around the edge, seasonal.
- Adds: a wall-less town whose mosques sit on the edges rather than at the centre; a port 8 km away reached only at flood.

#### Benin City, 1668 (Olfert Dapper, engraved by Jacob van Meurs)

- Plan: bird's-eye engraving "De Stadt Benin" from Naukeurige Beschrijvinge der Afrikaensche Gewesten (Library of Congress copy 1,157 x 900 px from the 1686 French edition; KB The Hague copy 2,400 x 1,873 px). Van Meurs never saw Benin; Adam Jones's warning on the file page applies. The engraving reproduces what Dutch traders reported: the long straight main street, the palace with its towers, and the house rows. The 1897 British plans are not on Commons in a usable form (see Not found).
- Settlement type: earthwork-ringed royal capital of the Edo kingdom.
- Enclosure: one ring plus citadel-sized palace. An inner bank-and-ditch about 11 km long (unverified; Connah's survey) encloses the city; the palace compound on the south-west is itself walled and as large as the rest of the town together; beyond lie hundreds of kilometres of village earthworks (not shown).
- Gates: several openings in the earthwork on the main roads (number unverified); no bridges.
- Street pattern: linear spine. A straight main street about 30 m wide and several kilometres long (the Dutch compared it to Amsterdam's Warmoesstraat) with parallel side streets; houses in continuous rows with uniform facades; the engraving shows straight streets crossing at right angles.
- Key places: the Oba's palace on the south-west edge, enormous, with its courtyards and tower-roofs; the market on the main street outside the palace gate; shrines inside the palace; no harbour (the river port is at Gwato/Ughoton 30 km away).
- Water: no river in the city; creeks and a stream west of the earthwork.
- Terrain: flat forest plateau.
- Suburbs: the earthwork system extends as a mesh of village enclosures for 50 km; the city is the largest cell.
- Adds: a ditch-and-bank enclosure instead of a wall; a palace covering half the town on an edge; straight wide streets with row houses in a forest kingdom.

#### Kumasi (Coomassie), 1819 (T. E. Bowdich, visited 1817)

- Plan: "Sketch of Coomassie", plate 11 of Mission from Cape Coast Castle to Ashantee (1,014 x 892 px, British Library scan). A street diagram with the 27 named streets and the principal compounds, drawn from memory and pacing.
- Settlement type: unwalled forest capital with planned wide streets.
- Enclosure: none; the town sits on a low hill ringed by marsh and forest, which was its defence.
- Gates: none; the main roads enter at the ends of the streets.
- Street pattern: grid-like. Bowdich names 27 streets, several of them 50-100 m wide and straight, meeting at near right angles; the principal street (Adum) runs to the market place; compounds in blocks of 40-80 m with open-fronted courtyard houses.
- Key places: the Asantehene's palace (a compound of many courts) at the edge of the central area with the great market beside it; the market square central; no mosque or church (Muslim traders had a quarter); no harbour.
- Water: the Subin stream and marsh south and west of the hill; no river in town.
- Terrain: a low hill in rain forest, sloping to marsh on three sides.
- Suburbs: Bantama (royal mausoleum village) 1 km north; craft villages around.
- Adds: wide straight streets and a near-grid in an unwalled hilltop capital; marsh as the only defence; uniform block width set by courtyard houses.

#### Great Zimbabwe, 1906 measured plan (D. Randall-MacIver) and 1963 layout (R. Summers, redrawn 2005)

- Plans: Randall-MacIver's measured plan of the Great Enclosure (651 x 715 px); a redrawing of Summers's 1963 site layout showing Hill Complex, Valley Ruins and Great Enclosure (578 x 639 px, PD-self). Both small; the 1906 plan is the only measured open drawing. The site has no historic map; this is the archaeological-plan case.
- Settlement type: stone-walled royal and ritual capital, 11th-15th century, no streets.
- Enclosure: several separate enclosures, not a town wall. The Great Enclosure is an ellipse about 250 m in circumference, 100 m across, with a wall up to 11 m high and 5 m thick and an inner parallel passage leading to a conical tower; the Hill Complex on the granite kopje 80 m above has terraces and enclosures threaded through boulders; the Valley Ruins are a dozen smaller enclosures.
- Gates: the Great Enclosure has three narrow entrances (N, NW, W); the Hill Complex is reached by two stepped passages.
- Street pattern: none. Daga (mud) huts in pole-fenced compounds spread over about 700 ha (unverified) between the stone enclosures; the stone walls bound elite compounds, not streets.
- Key places: ruler's seat on the Hill Complex (high ground, N edge); the Great Enclosure (royal wives or initiation, interpretation disputed) in the valley 300 m south; no temple, market or harbour.
- Water: a stream in the valley; no river.
- Terrain: granite hill and valley.
- Suburbs: the hut spread outside the walls was the town; the enclosures are the centre.
- Adds: walls that enclose compounds, not a town; an elliptical enclosure with a double wall passage; a two-centre layout (hilltop seat, valley enclosure) with no street net at all.

#### Kilwa Kisiwani (Quiloa), 1746 (J.-N. Bellin, after an English plan)

- Plan: Plan de l'isle et ville de Quiloa, 24 x 31 cm, 3,948 x 5,099 px, BnF Collection d'Anville. Shows the island, the town at its north-west point, the fort and the channel. Later French plans of 1776-80 "sur les observations du Sr. Morice" (6,400-8,900 px wide) are on Commons in the same search and show soundings; the 1746 plan is the simplest statement of the town.
- Settlement type: island stone town and harbour, Swahili sultanate, past its 14th-century peak.
- Enclosure: citadel plus town. A low town wall is recorded for the 15th-16th century (unverified); by 1746 the defence is the Gereza fort (Portuguese 1505, rebuilt by Oman c. 1800) on the shore; the sea and mangrove are the real boundary.
- Gates: none drawn; the fort has one landward gate. No bridges; the island is 2 km from the mainland across a channel.
- Street pattern: organic, coral-stone houses in tight clusters with narrow lanes; blocks 20-50 m; much of the former town in ruins.
- Key places: Great Mosque (11th-14th century) near the centre of the town on the shore; sultan's palace Husuni Kubwa 2 km east on a headland, far outside the town (abandoned by 1746); fort on the north shore at the landing; the harbour is the sheltered channel north of the island.
- Water: coast; the island has wells; no river.
- Terrain: low coral island, flat.
- Suburbs: Kilwa Kivinje on the mainland grew as the 19th-century replacement port.
- Adds: an island town facing a channel with a detached palace on a headland and a fort at the landing; coral-stone cluster with no wall.

#### Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi), 1860 depicting 1857 (James Taylor)
- Founded 1639-48 by Shah Jahan as a new walled capital; the plan shows it on the eve of the 1857 siege. North is to the right on the sheet.
- Population: about 150,000 (1853 census figure, unverified).
- Type: imperial capital on a river; citadel + walled town.
- Enclosure: one stone wall ring plus the Red Fort as citadel; shape is an irregular D with the flat side on the Yamuna (east); circuit about 6 km; enclosed area about 600 ha (unverified).
- Gates: 13 to 14 in the wall (Kashmiri, Mori, Kabul, Lahori, Ajmeri, Turkman, Delhi, Rajghat, Nigambodh, Calcutta and smaller posterns); the fort has its own Lahori and Delhi gates. One bridge of boats across the Yamuna by Salimgarh fort (gate count on this sheet unverified).
- Street pattern: two straight ceremonial spines (Chandni Chowk running west from the fort's Lahori Gate with a canal down its middle, about 1.4 km; Faiz Bazar running south from the fort's Delhi Gate to the city's Delhi Gate); everything else is organic Islamic cul-de-sac clusters (mohallas, katras, kuchas). Blocks between major lanes about 100 to 200 m.
- Key places: ruler's seat (Red Fort) on the eastern edge against the river, not central; Jama Masjid on a rock outcrop south-west of the fort, inside the town; main markets along the two spines; no harbour.
- Water: Yamuna on the east; a branch canal fed Chandni Chowk and the fort gardens.
- Terrain: flat alluvial plain; the Ridge (British siege camp) lies north-west outside.
- Suburbs: Paharganj and Sabzi Mandi outside the western and northern gates; Civil Lines to the north. Together smaller than the walled core in 1857.
- Adds: a river-edge citadel with a town hung off two straight bazaar spines through otherwise organic fabric; a D-shaped wall with 13+ gates (the existing corpus tops out around 8).

#### Lahore, 1847 (Maj. G. Carmichael Smyth, "from actual survey")
- Sikh-period survey, published as the frontispiece to *A History of the Reigning Family of Lahore* (Calcutta, 1847); depicts the Mughal walled city as it stood under Sikh rule.
- Population: about 100,000 (the 1868 census gave 98,924; the 1847 figure is unverified).
- Type: walled regional capital with a citadel on the river edge.
- Enclosure: one brick wall ring (Akbar's wall, rebuilt by Ranjit Singh with a moat) plus Lahore Fort as citadel; irregular rounded trapezoid; about 250 ha (unverified).
- Gates: 13 (Raushnai, Masti, Kashmiri, Khizri/Sheranwala, Yakki, Delhi, Akbari, Mochi, Shah Alami, Lohari, Mori, Bhati, Taxali). No bridge in the plan; the Ravi is crossed by ferry.
- Street pattern: organic cul-de-sac clusters; one main bazaar spine from Delhi Gate past the Wazir Khan mosque to the fort (the Shahi Guzargah); bazaars radiating from the Lohari and Shah Alami gates.
- Key places: fort on the northern edge, with the Badshahi mosque immediately west of it and the Hazuri Bagh square between them; Ranjit Singh's samadhi beside the fort; markets inside the southern gates; no harbour.
- Water: the Ravi passes north of the fort (it has since shifted away); the moat is fed from it.
- Terrain: flat, the old city on a low mound of its own debris.
- Suburbs: Anarkali bazaar and the Mughal garden belt to the south and east (Shalimar, tombs); extramural area in 1847 about equal to the walled core but far less dense.
- Adds: citadel + congregational mosque forming one monumental block on the wall line, a 13-gate ring, and the Islamic organic pattern seen on a surveyed (not pictorial) plan.

#### Jaipur, 1914 depicting the 1727 plan (Baedeker)
- Founded 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II, laid out by Vidyadhar Bhattacharya; the 1914 Baedeker plan shows the walled grid still intact and nearly fully built up.
- Population: 137,098 (1911 census).
- Type: planned cosmological grid capital (nine-square mandala).
- Enclosure: one crenellated wall ring, roughly rectangular with the north-east square displaced by hills; about 650 ha (unverified).
- Gates: 7 (Chand Pol west, Suraj Pol east, Ajmeri, Sanganeri, Ghat Darwaza, New/Samrat and Zorawar Singh/Dhruv Pol). No river, no bridges.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid of nine large squares (chowkris) of about 800 m each; three east-west and two north-south main avenues about 33 m wide; secondary streets about 16 m; lanes about 8 m. Three square plazas (chaupars) at the avenue crossings.
- Key places: City Palace and Jantar Mantar occupy the two central-northern chowkris, central on the north-south axis; markets line the main avenues with uniform arcaded shopfronts; the main temple (Govind Dev) sits inside the palace precinct on its axis.
- Water: none inside; a tank in the palace grounds; Jal Mahal lake and Man Sagar to the north.
- Terrain: flat plain closed by the Aravalli ridge on the north (Nahargarh fort above the city).
- Suburbs: by 1914 the railway station and civil station lie west and south-west outside the wall; extramural area about a quarter of the core.
- Adds: a pure planned grid with a hierarchy of street widths and plaza nodes, palace central rather than on the edge, no water body at all; nothing in the existing corpus is a non-European planned grid of this size.

#### Madurai, 1906 (Survey Office, Madras)
- Municipal plan from the Madura District Gazetteer; the fort wall was demolished 1837-41, so the plan shows the ring street (Veli streets) that replaced it.
- Population: 105,984 (1901 census, unverified).
- Type: temple city with concentric square streets; pilgrimage centre and regional capital.
- Enclosure: none in 1906; until 1841 one roughly square fort wall with a moat, now the Veli street ring about 1.8 km across (unverified). The temple itself is a walled enclosure of about 6 ha.
- Gates: the old fort had four main gates, one per side on the temple's cardinal axes (unverified).
- Street pattern: concentric squares around the Meenakshi temple: Adi, Chittirai, Avani Moola and Masi streets, each a ring processional street; the Masi ring is about 1 km on a side; between the rings, blocks of about 100 x 60 m; outside the Masi ring the pattern loosens to organic lanes.
- Key places: temple exactly central, its four gopuram gates on the cardinal axes; Tirumalai Nayak palace south-east within the Masi ring; markets on the Chittirai and Masi streets (the ring streets double as bazaars); no harbour.
- Water: Vaigai river bounds the town on the north; a road bridge (1889) and a railway bridge cross it; temple tank inside the enclosure.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: cantonment and railway north of the Vaigai and west of the ring; about half the core's area.
- Adds: concentric ring streets around a central temple (radial-concentric without being circular), ring streets as processional routes and markets at once, and a town whose wall became a boulevard.

#### Srirangam, 1910 plan depicting c. 1600 (James Burgess in Fergusson and Burgess)
- Scholarly measured plan of the Ranganathaswamy temple-city; the labelled Commons file is a composite by user Ismoon over the 1910 plate.
- Population: about 23,000 for Srirangam town (unverified).
- Type: temple-city, pilgrimage centre; the outer three enclosures hold ordinary houses and shops.
- Enclosure: seven concentric rectangular walls (prakaras); outermost about 878 x 754 m, about 63 ha; 21 gopuram gate-towers.
- Gates: one gopuram on each side of each enclosure on the cardinal axes; the main approach is from the south (the Rajagopuram, 73 m, finished 1987). No bridges on the plan; the island is reached by bridges over the Kaveri from Tiruchirappalli to the south.
- Street pattern: concentric rectangular rings; each ring street runs between two prakara walls; the inner four enclosures are temple precinct only.
- Key places: sanctum at the exact centre; markets in the outer two rings; no ruler's seat (the Nayaka palace was in Tiruchirappalli); no harbour.
- Water: island between the Kaveri (south) and Kollidam (north); temple tanks in the third enclosure.
- Terrain: flat river island.
- Suburbs: the modern town spills east and west beyond the seventh wall; about equal to the enclosure.
- Adds: a nested-wall settlement where the wall is a religious rather than military ring and the gate count (21) is set by cosmology, not defence; the only multi-ring town in the corpus where every ring is complete and rectangular.

#### Madras, Fort St George and Black Town, 1726 (Herman Moll)
- Printed plan of the Company settlement 87 years after its founding (1639).
- Population: about 80,000 for fort and town together (unverified; Lockyer's 1711 estimate).
- Type: colonial trading fort with a separate native town; coastal.
- Enclosure: two: the bastioned Fort St George ("White Town", an inner fort of about 400 x 100 m inside an outer bastioned wall) on the beach; Black Town to the north with its own wall and bastions, roughly square.
- Gates: fort: sea gate east, St Thomas gate south, a north gate toward Black Town; Black Town: several land gates west and north (count unverified). River Cooum crossed by a bridge south of the fort.
- Street pattern: grid in both enclosures; Black Town grid of blocks about 80 to 100 m with a central open square; the fort's interior is a tight grid of about 12 blocks.
- Key places: Governor's house and St Mary's church central in the fort; Black Town has its own temple and the main market; the harbour is an open roadstead, boats land on the beach in front of the fort.
- Water: open sea on the east; the Cooum river south; Black Town partly moated.
- Terrain: flat sandy coast.
- Suburbs: Triplicane and San Thome to the south (older Portuguese and Muslim settlements), villages west; combined larger than the two walled areas.
- Adds: a dual-enclosure colonial type (fort for the rulers, walled town for everyone else) on a harbourless beach; the corpus has no town with two walled cores.

#### Galle, 1659 (Adriaen de Leeuw, surveyor)
- Manuscript bird's-eye plan made 19 years after the Dutch took the town from the Portuguese (1640), before the full rampart ring (1660s to 1720s).
- Population: unknown.
- Type: fortified peninsula port; trading post.
- Enclosure: one; three landward bastions (Star, Moon, Sun) across the neck of the peninsula with a moat, lighter sea walls elsewhere; peninsula about 52 ha.
- Gates: one landward gate between the Moon and Sun bastions; a harbour gate on the north-east (the present "Old Gate" dates to 1669). No bridges.
- Street pattern: rectangular grid of about 12 x 8 blocks, blocks about 80 to 100 m, aligned to the peninsula's axis.
- Key places: Dutch church and commander's house central; warehouses and the harbour on the sheltered north-east side; market by the harbour gate; no ruler's seat beyond the commander.
- Water: sea on three sides; harbour bay to the north-east.
- Terrain: low rocky peninsula.
- Suburbs: the native town outside the landward works to the north, on this sheet about equal to the fort.
- Adds: a grid on a peninsula where the defensive effort is concentrated on one short landward side; the corpus has no peninsula-neck fortress.

#### Ayutthaya, 1691 (Simon de La Loubere)
- Printed plan from the French embassy of 1687-88, with a lettered key: city, palace, port, arsenals, bazaar street, seminary, Portuguese and Dutch quarters, elephant kraal, house for the French ambassadors. A far more detailed Thai archaeological plan of the same island (Phraya Boran Rachathanin, 1926, 7094 x 5516 px, PD-old-70) is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ayutthaya_City_Map_by_Phraya_Boran_Rachathanin_1926.jpg.
- Population: 150,000 to 200,000 (unverified; contemporary claims run higher).
- Type: island capital, river port, cosmological.
- Enclosure: one brick wall round the whole island; shape oval-rectangular about 4 km east-west by 2.5 km north-south, about 700 ha (unverified); palace separately walled in the north-west.
- Gates: La Loubere shows the wall pierced at many points; sources give about 16 land gates plus about 20 water gates (unverified). No bridges over the main rivers; boats and ferries.
- Street pattern: a grid of canals crossing the island east-west and north-south with streets beside them; blocks about 300 to 400 m between canals; a straight bazaar street (F on the key) across the island.
- Key places: palace and Wat Phra Si Sanphet on the northern edge, inside the wall but not central; the main market (bazaar street) runs through the centre; port on the south-east river front; foreign settlements outside the wall south and south-east across the river.
- Water: at the junction of the Chao Phraya, Lopburi and Pa Sak rivers; the moat is the rivers themselves; internal canals.
- Terrain: flat floodplain, seasonally flooded.
- Suburbs: Portuguese, Dutch, Japanese, French and Chinese quarters along the river south of the island; together about a third of the core.
- Adds: a whole-island city with a canal grid as the street system and foreign quarters as satellite enclaves; the corpus has no canal-grid or river-island capital.

#### Bangkok (Rattanakosin), 1870 surveyed 1861 (D. B. Bradley)
- The first large-scale surveyed plan of the capital, 79 years after its founding (1782); the file is a reproduction of a poor copy of the 1861 original, annotations legible.
- Population: 300,000 to 400,000 (unverified; contemporary estimates vary widely).
- Type: moated cosmological capital on a river, laid out to replicate Ayutthaya.
- Enclosure: three concentric water rings on the east bank: Khlong Lot (inner, Thonburi-period), Khlong Rop Krung (1783, with the city wall along it) and Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem (1851-52, outer); the walled core between river and Khlong Rop Krung is a D of about 410 ha.
- Gates: wall about 7 km with 14 forts and about 60 gates (unverified); no bridge across the Chao Phraya until 1932; many small bridges over the khlongs.
- Street pattern: in 1861 mostly canals with footpaths; the first paved road (Charoen Krung, 1864) runs south-east along the river; organic lanes between canals; palace precincts are grids.
- Key places: Grand Palace with Wat Phra Kaew on the river edge (west side of the D), Sanam Luang open ground north of it, Wat Pho south; Front Palace north of Sanam Luang; markets along the river and the Sampheng (Chinese) strip south-east outside the wall; the harbour is the river itself.
- Water: Chao Phraya west; three moat-canals; floating houses along the banks.
- Terrain: flat delta, barely above river level.
- Suburbs: Thonburi on the west bank (the previous capital), Sampheng and the river front south-east; the belt between the inner wall and the 1852 canal is about equal to the core.
- Adds: concentric moat rings added by successive generations rather than one ring, with a wall only on the middle ring, and a river as the main street.

#### Chiang Mai, 1890 (James Fitzroy McCarthy)
- Survey by the first director of the Siamese Royal Survey Department, published in *Surveying and Exploring in Siam* (1900); the file's Thai caption dates the survey to 1890.
- Population: about 50,000 (unverified).
- Type: walled Lanna capital, square moat, inland.
- Enclosure: two: the inner square brick wall and moat (about 1.8 x 2.0 km, about 360 ha) with four corner bastions; the outer earthen rampart (Kamphaeng Din) forming a crescent on the south-east along the Mae Kha canal, enclosing about as much again.
- Gates: five in the inner wall (Chang Phuak north, Tha Phae east, Chiang Mai and Suan Prung south, Suan Dok west); the outer rampart has further gates toward the Ping river. Bridges over the moat at each gate and over the Ping east of the city.
- Street pattern: inner city on a loose grid aligned to the wall with the two gate-to-gate streets as spines; organic between; the outer crescent is organic with temple compounds as blocks.
- Key places: the ruler's compound (Khum) central-north on the north-south spine; Wat Chedi Luang near the exact centre; Wat Phra Singh inside the west gate; markets outside the Tha Phae gate toward the river (Chang Moi Road); the river landing is 1 km east of the wall.
- Water: Ping river 1 km east; moat; Mae Kha canal along the outer rampart.
- Terrain: flat valley floor, Doi Suthep mountain to the west; Wiang Suan Dok (an earlier walled compound) west of the city.
- Suburbs: the crescent between the two walls and the river-front strip; together about the core's area.
- Adds: a square moat with corner bastions and a secondary crescent earthwork; an inland cosmological square 1 km from its river rather than on it.

#### Mandalay, 1914 (Wagner and Debes for Baedeker)
- City plan at 1:50,000 with a 1:150,000 environs inset; shows the 1857-59 royal city 28 years after the British annexation, with the British cantonment inside the walls.
- Population: 138,299 (1911 census).
- Type: planned square royal city with moat; last Konbaung capital.
- Enclosure: one; an exact square of about 2 km on each side, about 400 ha; brick wall about 8 m high with 48 bastions; moat about 64 m wide. The palace is a second walled rectangle at the centre.
- Gates: 12, three per side, with the middle gate of each side on the palace axes; four bridges over the moat, one per side; no river inside the plan, the Irrawaddy is 3 km west.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid both inside the square and in the civil town to the south and west; blocks inside about 200 m (unverified).
- Key places: palace exactly central; Mandalay Hill with its pagodas 1 km north-east outside the wall; the Arakan pagoda south-west outside; markets (Zegyo bazaar) in the civil town south-west outside the wall; river landing 3 km west.
- Water: Irrawaddy to the west, moat, Shwe Ta Chaung canal through the town.
- Terrain: flat plain under Mandalay Hill.
- Suburbs: the civil grid town outside the square is larger than the royal square (about three times the area, unverified).
- Adds: the textbook square cosmological city with three gates per side and a dead-centre palace, with all commerce outside the wall.

#### Batavia, 1667 (anonymous, Nationaal Archief VEL 1182)
- Manuscript plan of the castle and city; caption lost to ink corrosion; the year reads from an inscription.
- Population: about 27,000 inside the walls (1673 count, unverified).
- Type: walled canal-grid colonial port with a castle.
- Enclosure: one bastioned wall ring (completed about 1650) plus the castle at the north-east corner on the sea; a rectangle about 1.3 x 0.9 km, about 115 ha (unverified).
- Gates: about 5 land gates (Nieuwpoort south, Diestpoort, Rotterdammerpoort, Utrechtsepoort, and the castle gates); drawbridges over the canals; the river is the harbour entrance.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid with the canalised Ciliwung (Kali Besar) as the central north-south axis and parallel canals (Tijgersgracht and others) every 200 to 300 m; blocks about 100 x 200 m.
- Key places: castle (ruler's seat and warehouses) on the sea edge, north-east corner; town hall on a square in the southern half; markets on the canal quays; the harbour is the river mouth beside the castle.
- Water: sea to the north, river through the middle, canal grid, moat.
- Terrain: flat swampy coast.
- Suburbs: Chinese and Javanese kampongs outside the south and east walls, about equal to the core by 1667 (unverified).
- Adds: an Amsterdam-style canal grid in the tropics, castle at a corner rather than central, sea and river both as edges.

#### Manila (Intramuros), 1766 (Lt-Gen. Juan Martin Zermeno)
- Manuscript defence proposal after the British occupation of 1762-64, showing the walled city and its surroundings at 9666 x 5468 px. A later surveyed sheet (Ildefonso de Aragon, 1814, 5915 x 3821 px, PD-old-70) is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Plano_de_la_Plaza_de_Manyla_y_sus_contornos.png.
- Population: about 10,000 inside the walls (unverified); the city with suburbs several times that.
- Type: bastioned walled grid city at a river mouth; colonial capital.
- Enclosure: one bastioned ring, an irregular pentagon about 64 ha, perimeter 4.5 km, with a moat; Fort Santiago as citadel at the northern tip on the river mouth.
- Gates: 7 (Real, Parian, Santa Lucia, Postigo, Santo Domingo, Almacenes, Isabel II added 1850s); the Puente Grande bridge across the Pasig from the Parian gate to Binondo.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid of about 10 x 6 blocks of about 100 m, aligned to the river front.
- Key places: Fort Santiago on the river-mouth tip; cathedral, Ayuntamiento and the governor's palace on the Plaza Mayor in the northern third; markets outside the walls (the Parian, Chinese market, east; Binondo across the river); harbour at the river mouth and the bay.
- Water: Manila Bay west, Pasig river north, moat on the east and south.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: Parian, Binondo, Tondo, Santa Cruz, Quiapo outside the walls, several times the core's area.
- Adds: a bastioned colonial grid where the whole market and most of the population are deliberately outside the wall, and a citadel at a sharp river-mouth point.

### Part B

#### Suzhou (Pingjiang), 1229 (Li Shoupeng; stele carved by Lu Ting and Zhang Yucheng)
- Plan: the Pingjiang tu stele, 2.8 m tall, held in the Suzhou Stele Museum (Confucian temple). The verified link is a CC BY 2.0 photograph of the stele (960 x 1280), legible for wall, gates, canal grid and the sub-city. The Library of Congress holds a full rubbing (resource id g7824s.ct002252, raster download offered) but loc.gov returned 403 to fetches, so that is (unverified).
- Population: 200,000-300,000 inside the wall (unverified).
- Type: canal city and prefectural seat on the Grand Canal, Lower Yangtze delta.
- Enclosure: one ring plus an inner walled sub-city (zicheng, the prefectural compound) in the centre-south. Outer wall is a rounded rectangle, about 4.5 km N-S by 3 km E-W, about 14 km² (1,400 ha), with a full moat.
- Gates: five, each a paired land gate and water gate: Pan (SW), Xu (W), Chang (NW), Qi (N), Lou (E). No gate on the south wall. Bridges: the stele labels 359 inside the wall.
- Street pattern: double grid. Three E-W and four N-S trunk canals ("three horizontal, four vertical") with streets running parallel to each canal; blocks are long strips between a street and a canal, typically 60-100 m wide and 200-500 m long, each house fronting the street and backing onto water.
- Key places: ruler's seat (prefectural yamen) in the walled zicheng, central-south; Confucian temple and school SW inside the wall; Xuanmiao Daoist temple north-centre; markets along the canal spine between the two; no single market square.
- Water: Grand Canal outside the west wall; moat all round; internal canal grid fed through the water gates. The city is defined by its canals.
- Terrain: flat delta.
- Suburbs: dense ribbon suburbs outside Chang gate (NW) and Xu gate along the Grand Canal (shown on later maps; the stele cuts off at the moat).
- Adds: the canal-grid city, water gates paired with land gates, a walled government compound inside a walled city, and a wall with no south gate.

#### Hangzhou (Lin'an), 1265-74 (Qian Youyue; reprint 1547, Tian Rucheng)
- Plan: Songchao Jingcheng Tu from the Xianchun Lin'an zhi gazetteer, reproduced across four pages of the 1547 Xihu Youlan zhi, merged by the uploader into one 9850 x 3788 image. Schematic woodblock, not surveyed, but gates, wall outline, palace and axis are positioned correctly.
- Population: over 1,000,000 in the prefecture by late Song counts (unverified).
- Type: imperial capital squeezed between a lake and a tidal river.
- Enclosure: one irregular elongated ring, about 7 km N-S by 2-3 km E-W, roughly 10 km² (1,000 ha), plus a separate walled palace city at the south end on Phoenix Hill.
- Gates: 13 land gates and 5 water gates (Song gazetteer count); the plan draws them along all four sides, most on the east (river) side. Bridges: many over the internal canals; none across the Qiantang.
- Street pattern: linear spine. The Imperial Way (Yujie) runs north from the palace gate for about 4 km, with the city laid out in strips either side; secondary lanes are organic, following canals. Blocks irregular, 100-300 m.
- Key places: palace on high ground at the SOUTH end (reverse of the northern Chinese norm); ancestral temple and government offices along the Imperial Way; markets strung along the Way and at the Grand Canal terminus in the north; no central square.
- Water: West Lake outside the west wall, Qiantang River outside the south-east wall, Grand Canal terminating at the north wall; internal canals parallel to the spine.
- Terrain: flat alluvium with hills (Phoenix Hill, Wu Hill) inside the south wall.
- Suburbs: large, along the canal north of the wall and along the lake shore; late Song sources put more people outside the wall than inside (unverified).
- Adds: a capital with the palace at the south end on a hill, an elongated wall forced by lake and river, and 18 gates.

#### Beijing, 1736-1820 (anonymous woodblock, Jing cheng quan tu, BnF)
- Plan: single-sheet coloured woodblock, 85.5 x 43.5 cm, Gallica OAI date range 1736-1820; shows Inner City, Imperial City, Forbidden City and Outer City with gates and principal streets. Commons file is 6864 x 12139.
- Population: about 1,000,000 (unverified).
- Type: cosmological capital; nested rectangular walls on a N-S axis.
- Enclosure: four rings. Inner City (Ming 1420s) about 6.6 km E-W by 5.3 km N-S, about 3,500 ha; Imperial City inside it, about 2.5 x 2.8 km; Forbidden City inside that, 960 x 750 m (72 ha); Outer City (1553) attached to the south, about 7.9 km E-W by 3.1 km N-S, about 2,500 ha. Moats on the Inner and Outer walls.
- Gates: Inner City 9 (3 south, 2 east, 2 west, 2 north); Outer City 7; Imperial City 4; Forbidden City 4. Bridges: stone bridges over the moats at every gate and over the Jinshui canal inside.
- Street pattern: cosmological grid. Avenues between facing gates, then hutong lanes E-W at about 70-80 m spacing; blocks between major avenues about 700 x 700 m, subdivided into courtyard blocks.
- Key places: ruler's seat dead centre on the axis; Temple of Heaven and Altar of Agriculture flanking the axis in the Outer City; markets at the Inner City gates (Qianmen outside the south-central gate is the main commercial district) and in the Outer City; no market inside the Imperial City.
- Water: no river; canals (Tonghui) bring the Grand Canal to the east gate; lakes (Beihai, Zhongnanhai) inside the Imperial City.
- Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: the Outer City began as a suburb and was walled; further ribbon suburbs outside every gate, each 10-20 % of the Inner City.
- Adds: four nested enclosures, 24 gates, a 7.8 km ceremonial axis, and a lane grid with a uniform 70-80 m pitch.

#### Nanjing (Jinling), 1853 (anonymous manuscript on silk, Library of Congress; bird's-eye)
- Plan: 17901 x 11445 scan of a silk painting showing the Qing provincial armies besieging the Taiping capital; north toward upper left; the city wall, gates, Qinhuai river, hills and the ruined Ming imperial city are drawn pictorially and in correct relation.
- Population: unknown for 1853 (the Taiping takeover emptied and refilled the city).
- Type: river capital with an irregular wall following terrain.
- Enclosure: one ring plus the walled Ming Imperial City on the east side (ruined by 1853 but outlined). The Ming wall is 35 km long and encloses about 5,500 ha; the shape is irregular, bulging north to the Yangtze and Xuanwu Lake and following hills on the east.
- Gates: 13 in the Ming wall (Zhonghua in the south is a triple-courtyard barbican). Bridges: over the Qinhuai inside and at the water gates; none over the Yangtze.
- Street pattern: organic inside the south (old Qinhuai town, blocks 50-150 m); planned grid only in the Ming imperial quarter on the east; large empty and farmed areas inside the north wall.
- Key places: ruler's seat (Ming palace, then Taiping Heavenly King's palace) on the EAST side, not central; Confucian temple on the Qinhuai in the south-centre; markets along the Qinhuai; Drum Tower on the central hill.
- Water: Yangtze outside the NW wall; Qinhuai river enters and leaves through water gates and loops through the south of the city; Xuanwu Lake against the north wall.
- Terrain: hills inside the wall (Drum Tower hill, Qingliang hill), Purple Mountain outside the east.
- Suburbs: west and south suburbs along the Qinhuai and the Yangtze landing, each small relative to the 5,500 ha enclosure.
- Adds: a terrain-following wall with much open land inside, 13 gates, an off-centre palace, and a river that threads through the wall twice.

#### Guangzhou (Canton), 1855 (Daniel Vrooman)
- Plan: engraved survey, 8237 x 4548, the first Western survey of the whole city and suburbs; shows the double-walled city, the western suburb (Xiguan), the factories and Honam across the river.
- Population: 500,000-1,000,000 (unverified).
- Type: river port and provincial capital; walled core with suburbs larger than the core.
- Enclosure: two adjoining rings: the Old City (north, about 2 x 2 km, Tang-Song origin, walls rebuilt 1380) and the New City (a strip south of it along the river, added 1563), total about 10 km of wall, about 520 ha; a cross wall separates them. Forts on Guanyin Hill inside the north wall.
- Gates: 16 in total on this survey (12 in the Old City including gates in the cross wall, 4 in the New City; count unverified). Bridges: none over the Pearl River; ferries to Honam.
- Street pattern: grid in the Old City (N-S and E-W streets, blocks about 100 x 150 m), very narrow commercial streets (2-3 m) in the New City and the western suburb, organic along the river.
- Key places: governor-general's yamen and Tartar (banner) quarter in the west half of the Old City; Confucian temple and examination hall in the east half; markets in the New City and Xiguan; the foreign factories outside the wall to the SW on the river.
- Water: Pearl River along the south wall; creeks and the moat; the city is on the north bank.
- Terrain: flat alluvium rising to Yuexiu (Guanyin) Hill in the north, which the wall climbs.
- Suburbs: Xiguan (west) and the river front suburbs are together as large as the walled city; Honam on the south bank adds more.
- Adds: a two-part wall with an internal cross wall, suburbs larger than the core, a wall that climbs a hill to enclose a fort, and a riverside foreign enclave outside the wall.

#### Heijo-kyo (Nara), reconstruction 1897 (Kawada Tsuguru et al., National Diet Library), depicts 710-784
- Plan: Meiji-era reconstruction of the jobo grid overlaid on a survey, 2765 x 4043; labelled as reconstruction. Modern Nabunken plans are more accurate but not open.
- Population: 70,000-200,000 (unverified).
- Type: cosmological grid capital modelled on Chang'an; no defensive wall.
- Enclosure: none in practice; a nominal boundary (rajo) with the Rajomon gate at the south end of the axis. Walled palace compound (Heijo Palace, about 1 x 1.3 km, 120 ha) at the north-centre. Main city 4.3 km E-W by 4.8 km N-S (about 2,000 ha) plus an "outer capital" (Gekyo) block attached to the east.
- Gates: palace 12 gates (3 per side); city has the one ceremonial south gate. Bridges: minor, over the Saho river which cuts the NE corner.
- Street pattern: cosmological grid. Suzaku Avenue, 74 m wide, divides Left and Right Capitals; 9 E-W jo and 4 bo per side; each bo is about 530 m square, cut into 16 tsubo of about 130 m.
- Key places: palace at the north end of the axis; East and West Markets in the south part of each half (7th-8th jo); great temples (Todaiji, Kofukuji) in the eastern Gekyo on the hill slope; Yakushiji and Toshodaiji in the Right Capital.
- Water: Saho river NE, no major river; canals along avenues.
- Terrain: flat basin floor, hills on the east edge where the temples sit.
- Suburbs: the Gekyo itself is an attached extension; otherwise none.
- Adds: a capital with no wall at all, a 530 m super-block grid, a 74 m central avenue, and twin planned markets.

#### Edo (Tokyo), 1680 (Hayashi Yoshinaga, Zoho Edo oezu, UBC Library)
- Plan: woodblock pocket map, north to the right, hand-coloured, with daimyo crests on each mansion; 2403 x 2000 on Commons (UBC master is 12015 x 10000).
- Population: 800,000-1,000,000 by 1700 (unverified).
- Type: castle town and shogunal capital.
- Enclosure: none around the town. The castle has concentric and spiral moats: inner (honmaru, ninomaru, sannomaru), middle and outer moats, the outer moat spiralling clockwise about 16 km to the Sumida river. The castle complex covers about 300 ha.
- Gates: 36 mitsuke gates on the moat rings; the town itself has no gates. Bridges: Nihonbashi (origin of the five highways), Ryogoku (over the Sumida, 1659) and dozens over the canals.
- Street pattern: two systems. West and south (Yamanote, high ground): daimyo and samurai estates, large irregular plots of 1-10 ha on curving roads following ridges. East (Shitamachi, reclaimed lowland): townsmen grid with canals, blocks about 60 x 60 m (one cho) with a street-front/alley-back structure.
- Key places: ruler's seat central on the hill; main temples (Kan'eiji, Zojoji) on the NE and SW edges as spiritual guards; Nihonbashi fish market and commercial core E of the castle; harbour on the Sumida mouth SE.
- Water: Sumida river E, bay SE, canal network in the lowland, moats.
- Terrain: plateau edge; samurai on the heights, merchants on the fill.
- Suburbs: temple districts and post towns along the five highways; large relative to the core.
- Adds: a 16 km spiral moat instead of a wall, class-zoned street textures side by side, and canal-grid reclaimed land.

#### Kanazawa, 1808 (anonymous manuscript copy, National Diet Library)
- Plan: Kaga no kuni Kanazawa-jo ezu, 116 x 198 cm, coloured; NDL PDF, 11331 x 9991 on the main page. Shows castle, moats, samurai plots with names, temple districts and the two rivers.
- Population: about 100,000 (unverified), the largest non-shogunal castle town.
- Type: castle town on a tongue of high ground between two rivers.
- Enclosure: no wall. Castle on the hill top with inner and outer moats; the Sai river (SW) and Asano river (NE) form natural outer lines; the plan shows an outer earthwork (sogamae) with moats on both sides of the castle.
- Gates: castle gates only (Ishikawa gate etc.); the roads cross the rivers at controlled bridges. Bridges: Asano-gawa Ohashi, Sai-gawa Ohashi, each at the end of a main road.
- Street pattern: radial-spiral. Roads lead from the castle gates out to the river bridges; samurai quarters on curving streets around the castle (Nagamachi to the west); townsmen in strips along the two highways; T-junctions and dog-legs at quarter boundaries.
- Key places: ruler's seat on the hill, central; three temple districts (Teramachi beyond the Sai, Utatsuyama beyond the Asano, Kodatsuno SE) placed outside the rivers as outworks; market along the Owaricho road N of the castle; no harbour (port at Miyanokoshi 5 km away).
- Water: two rivers flanking the town; the Tatsumi canal feeds the castle moats from the Sai river uphill.
- Terrain: plateau with the castle on a 60 m rise, slopes down to each river.
- Suburbs: temple belts and geisha districts across the rivers; together perhaps a third of the core.
- Adds: temple belts used as outer defences across rivers, a town with no wall but a deliberate dog-leg street plan, and a two-river peninsula site.

#### Nagasaki, 1802-1854 (anonymous coloured woodblock, Hizen Nagasaki no zu, BnF)
- Plan: 65 x 43 cm sheet, 10896 x 7426 scan, pictorial-planimetric; shows the fan-shaped Dejima, the walled Chinese quarter, the town's machi, the harbour and the forts on the approaches.
- Population: 50,000-60,000 (unverified).
- Type: trading harbour town; the only foreign-trade port of Tokugawa Japan.
- Enclosure: none around the town. Two enclaves: Dejima, an artificial fan-shaped island of 1.3 ha with one bridge and one gate, and Tojin yashiki, the walled Chinese compound of about 3 ha on the slope SE of the town.
- Gates: the Dejima gate, the Chinese compound gates, and barriers on the roads; no town gates. Bridges: one to Dejima, stone bridges over the Nakashima river (Meganebashi 1634).
- Street pattern: linear spine along the harbour-head valley, with a loose grid of machi (blocks 40-60 m) fanning up the two side valleys; organic on the slopes.
- Key places: magistrate's office (bugyosho) on high ground N of the harbour head; Suwa shrine on the hill N; the harbour and the trading enclaves S and SW; the market along the harbour front.
- Water: deep narrow harbour, Nakashima river through the town, coast.
- Terrain: steep amphitheatre of hills around the harbour head; the town climbs the slopes.
- Suburbs: temple row along the eastern hillside (Teramachi), fishing villages along the harbour; modest.
- Adds: a harbour-head town in a steep bowl, and gated foreign enclaves (an artificial island and a walled compound) as distinct objects.

#### Shuri and Naha, 18th century (anonymous magiri map, Ryukyu-koku no zu, Okinawa Prefectural Museum reproduction)
- Plan: one sheet of the kingdom's district (magiri) map series covering the urban districts: Shuri, Tomari, Kume, Naha; 1735 x 1289 scan; pictorial-planimetric, roads and hamlets drawn, no scale.
- Population: 30,000-40,000 for Shuri and Naha together (unverified).
- Type: twin settlement: a royal hill town (Shuri) 4 km inland and its port town (Naha) on a lagoon.
- Enclosure: Shuri castle has its own curvilinear limestone walls (about 4 ha) on the hilltop; Shuri town is unwalled. Naha had no wall; the harbour mouth was guarded by two forts (Mie and Yarazamori) on the two headlands.
- Gates: castle gates in sequence on the approach (Shureimon then several inner gates); none for the towns. Bridges and causeways: the Chokotei, a 1 km stone causeway across the lagoon linking Naha to the mainland; bridges to the Kume quarter.
- Street pattern: Shuri: organic, roads radiating from the castle down the ridges, stone-walled compounds. Naha: short organic grid on the islands, with the Kume Chinese village a separate quarter and Tomari a separate port village.
- Key places: ruler's seat on the hilltop; royal temples (Enkakuji) beside the castle; the main market and harbour in Naha on the water; the Confucian temple in Kume.
- Water: lagoon, coast, estuary; Naha is on islands in the lagoon.
- Terrain: Shuri on a 130 m limestone hill; Naha flat, on sand islands.
- Suburbs: Tomari and Kume are separate villages functionally part of the port.
- Adds: the split capital (hill palace town + lagoon port joined by a causeway), curvilinear castle walls, and a town built across lagoon islands.

#### Seoul (Hanyang), c. 1840 (Kim Jeong-ho, Suseonjeondo)
- Plan: woodblock print, 1888 x 2616 scan; shows the wall along the ridges, gates, palaces, principal streets, streams and the hills, with the Han river at the bottom.
- Population: about 200,000 (unverified).
- Type: royal capital sited by geomancy inside a ring of mountains.
- Enclosure: one ring, 18.6 km, following the ridge line of four mountains (Bugaksan N, Naksan E, Namsan S, Inwangsan W); the enclosed basin is about 1,600 ha, irregular and roughly oval. Separate walled palaces (Gyeongbokgung about 40 ha, Changdeokgung) at the north foot of the ring.
- Gates: 4 great gates (one per cardinal side, the north one kept shut) and 4 small gates between them, plus water gates where the Cheonggyecheon leaves through the east wall. Bridges: many stone bridges over the Cheonggyecheon (Gwangtonggyo etc.); none over the Han.
- Street pattern: two planned axes (Jongno E-W from the east gate, Yukjo street N-S from the main palace gate) and organic lanes elsewhere; blocks irregular, 50-150 m; stream-side lanes follow the Cheonggyecheon.
- Key places: main palace at the north foot of Bugaksan on the axis, not central; ancestral shrine (Jongmyo) E of it and the altar of land and grain (Sajik) W, by the Chinese rule; government offices along the axis; licensed market (Sijeon) along Jongno in the centre; harbour outside the wall at the Han river landings (Mapo, Yongsan) 3-4 km S.
- Water: the Cheonggyecheon stream crosses the basin W to E inside the wall; the Han river is outside the south wall.
- Terrain: basin floor with the wall on ridges up to 340 m.
- Suburbs: river-landing suburbs on the Han and ribbons outside the east and west gates; together perhaps a quarter of the core.
- Adds: a wall laid on mountain ridges rather than a geometric line, a palace at the edge by geomantic rule, and a closed north gate.

#### Suwon (Hwaseong), 1795 (anonymous, Hwaseong jeondo from the Hwaseong seongyeok uigwe, printed 1801)
- Plan: 3852 x 2832 scan of the bird's-eye plan in the construction record; every wall structure labelled; the uigwe also contains measured elevations of each gate and bastion.
- Population: 10,000-20,000 (unverified); the town was a planned relocation of 1789.
- Type: planned fortress town around a hill and a stream, built 1794-96 under King Jeongjo.
- Enclosure: one ring, 5.7 km, irregular (the wall takes in Paldalsan hill on the west and runs across the flat valley on the east), about 130 ha. Brick and stone with 48 named structures: 4 gates with barbicans, 2 water gates, 5 bastion-towers, 4 secret gates, command posts, beacon tower.
- Gates: N Janganmun (main, facing the capital), S Paldalmun, E Changnyongmun, W Hwaseomun; the Suwoncheon stream enters and leaves through the N and S water gates (Hwahongmun has 7 arches). Bridges: at the water gates and across the stream on the main street.
- Street pattern: linear spine with a grid: the Seoul-Suwon road runs N-S between the north and south gates; a cross street E-W; blocks about 80-120 m near the spine, organic toward the hill.
- Key places: ruler's seat, the temporary palace (Haenggung), at the foot of Paldalsan on the WEST side facing east across the town; the main market along the spine; a Confucian shrine and the beacon on the hill; no harbour.
- Water: the Suwoncheon stream runs N-S through the town inside the wall; a reservoir outside the north gate.
- Terrain: hill (143 m) inside the west wall, flat valley east of it.
- Suburbs: none at the time of the plan (new town); later ribbons outside the north gate.
- Adds: a small fully planned late-18th-century fortress town with a complete labelled wall kit (water gates, bastions, barbicans), a palace on the hill edge, and a stream through the wall.

#### Hue, 1885 (Depot de la guerre, Paris; survey 1:7000)
- Companion: 1919 Vietnamese plan by Hue Industrial School students, 779 x 751, PD-Vietnam,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:B%E1%BA%A3n_%C4%91%E1%BB%93_Kinh_th%C3%A0nh_Hu%E1%BA%BF.jpg ;
  and a CC BY-SA 3.0 redrawing, 966 x 1169,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Plan_of_Hue_Imperial_Citadel.jpg (verified by the
  South and Southeast Asia agent).
- Plan: French military survey, 7604 x 8494, of the citadel built 1805-32; shows the bastioned trace, moat, gates, internal canals, the Imperial City and the Forbidden Purple City, and the Tran Binh Dai outwork.
- Population: 50,000-100,000 (unverified).
- Type: imperial capital; Vauban bastioned trace around a Chinese-style nested palace city.
- Enclosure: three nested rings plus an outwork. Kinh thanh (the citadel): a square about 2.2 km a side, 520 ha, with 24 bastions and a 23 m wide moat; Hoang thanh (Imperial City) inside, a rectangle about 600 x 600 m (perimeter 2.5 km) in the south-central part; Tu Cam thanh (Forbidden Purple City) inside that; Tran Binh Dai, a small bastioned fort attached to the NE corner.
- Gates: 10 in the citadel wall (plus the water gate for the canal); 4 in the Imperial City (Ngo Mon south); the Forbidden City has its own. Bridges: over the moat at each gate; the citadel faces the Perfume River across an esplanade with the flag tower.
- Street pattern: grid inside the citadel, aligned to the walls, blocks about 150-250 m; a canal (Ngu Ha) winds through the north part; the SE quadrant is the densest residential area.
- Key places: ruler's seat at the centre-south of the square, on the axis facing the river and Ngu Binh mountain (geomantic screen); temples and ancestral halls inside the Imperial City; markets and the commercial town OUTSIDE the citadel on the east (Gia Hoi) across a canal; harbour on the river.
- Water: Perfume River along the south front; moat; internal canal; the citadel sits on the north bank.
- Terrain: flat riverside.
- Suburbs: Gia Hoi merchant quarter E of the citadel and the river-bank villages; together about a quarter of the citadel area.
- Adds: a Vauban bastioned square wrapped around a Chinese nested palace, 24 bastions, 10 gates, and a canal threaded through a fortress.

#### Hanoi (Thang Long), 1873 (Pham Dinh Bach; reissued 1937 by the Service geographique de l'Indochine)
- Plan: 1:12,500 drawn plan, 7240 x 7660, made in the year of the first French attack; shows the Vauban citadel (1805), the old earthen outer rampart, the merchant quarter, lakes and the Red River dike. Companion: the schematic Hong Duc map of Thang Long, 1490 (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bản_đồ_kinh_thành_Thăng_Long,_theo_Hồng_Đức_bản_đồ_sách_洪德版圖冊_(1490).jpg, 711 x 972, PD-Vietnam), which shows the earlier triple enclosure.
- Population: about 100,000 (unverified).
- Type: citadel plus guild-street merchant town on a dyked river bank; former royal capital.
- Enclosure: two rings. The Nguyen citadel, a Vauban square about 1 km a side (about 100 ha) with 5 gates and a moat, at the NW; the older Dai La earthen rampart, an irregular loop about 8 km long, enclosing citadel, lakes and the merchant town. The Hong Duc map shows the Le-era triple enclosure (Dai La, Imperial City, Forbidden City).
- Gates: citadel 5 (N, E, W, SE, SW); the earthen rampart had 16 gates (Ô), several named on the plan (Ô Quan Chuong survives). Bridges: over the To Lich river and canals; none over the Red River.
- Street pattern: grid inside the citadel; the 36 Streets quarter between citadel and river is a dense organic mesh of guild streets, blocks 30-60 m, tube houses 3-4 m wide; village lanes around the lakes.
- Key places: ruler's seat (citadel, then the French garrison) at the NW edge, not central; Temple of Literature S of the citadel; the market (Dong Xuan) and guild streets E toward the river; the river landing E outside the dike; Hoan Kiem lake S of the merchant quarter.
- Water: Red River E behind a high dike; West Lake N; Hoan Kiem and many ponds inside; the To Lich river formed the west moat.
- Terrain: flat floodplain, below river flood level, hence the dike.
- Suburbs: craft villages outside the rampart on every side; large relative to the citadel, smaller than the merchant town.
- Adds: a citadel at the corner of a much larger loose rampart, a 16-gate earthen enclosure, a guild-street quarter with 30-60 m blocks, and a town below river level behind a dike.

### Part B

#### Tenochtitlan, 1524 (Friedrich Peypus, Nuremberg, after a drawing from Cortes's circle)

- Region/culture: Valley of Mexico, Aztec (Mexica) capital. Printed 1524; depicts the city of 1519-20, before the siege of 1521. A larger scan of the same sheet including the Gulf map is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map_of_Tenochtitlan_and_Gulf_of_Mexico,_1524.jpg (5000 x 3168, Public domain, verified).
- Population: 150,000-200,000 (unverified). Settlement type: lake-island cosmological capital.
- Enclosure: none in the European sense. The lake is the enclosure; the print draws the city as a circle in the water. Real area of the island city about 13 km2 (unverified); the print shows it schematically.
- Gates/bridges: three main causeways (west to Tlacopan, south to Iztapalapa, north to Tepeyac) and an aqueduct from Chapultepec; the causeways have removable bridges, which act as gates. The long dike of Nezahualcoyotl runs north-south on the east side of the lake.
- Street pattern: orthogonal canal-and-street grid in four quarters meeting at the sacred precinct; the print simplifies this to a few radial canals. Canals are as frequent as streets.
- Key places: walled sacred precinct (Templo Mayor, twin shrine) dead centre, drawn as a square courtyard; ruler's palace (Moctezuma) adjoining the precinct; the great market was in the twin city Tlatelolco in the north of the island, not in the centre; no harbour, canoes land everywhere.
- Water: lake city; the city is water-locked and canalled. Terrain: flat, artificial land (chinampas) on the fringes.
- Suburbs: Tlatelolco (northern twin city, large, with its own precinct and market); chinampa villages around the lake edge.
- Adds: a city whose enclosure is water, whose "gates" are causeway bridges, with a centred walled precinct and an off-centre market. None of the 15 existing towns is an island city with canals as streets.

#### Teotihuacan, site plan (modern CC drawing; depicts c. 100-550 CE)

- Region/culture: Central Mexico, Teotihuacan culture. A reconstruction/site plan, not a historic map; the Millon survey (1973) is the authority but is not openly available. A 512 x 384 px schematic of the core only is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Teotihuacancityplan.png (Public domain, verified).
- Population: 100,000 at peak (unverified; estimates range 80,000-150,000). Settlement type: planned cosmological grid city.
- Enclosure: none. Built-up area about 20 km2 at peak (unverified). The sacred core along the Avenue of the Dead is about 2.4 km long.
- Gates: none. No bridges of note; the San Juan river was canalised to follow the grid.
- Street pattern: a single master grid oriented 15.5 degrees east of north over the whole city; one great north-south spine (Avenue of the Dead, 40-45 m wide) crossed by an east-west avenue at the Ciudadela. Typical unit is the walled apartment compound, 50-60 m square, so blocks are 50-60 m.
- Key places: Pyramid of the Moon closes the north end of the spine; Pyramid of the Sun sits on the east side of the spine mid-way; the Ciudadela (ruler's/ritual compound with the Temple of the Feathered Serpent) sits at the spine's south crossing on the east, and the Great Compound (probable market) faces it across the spine on the west. Everything important is on the axis; nothing is on an edge.
- Water: small river (San Juan) crossing the grid, canalised. Terrain: flat valley floor, slight rise to the north.
- Suburbs: the whole city is one continuous grid of compounds; foreign quarters (Oaxaca barrio, Merchants' barrio) lie on the west and east fringes.
- Adds: a wall-less city with one dominating ceremonial spine, a tilted master grid, and compound blocks instead of street-fronted houses.

#### Cahokia, 1904 (David I. Bushnell Jr., Peabody Museum; surveyed mound group)

- Region/culture: Mississippi floodplain, Illinois; Mississippian culture. The PDF is the 1904 Peabody paper with plates; the plate showing the mound-group plan is inside the PDF (plate number not verified). Depicts the site as surviving in 1904, built c. 1050-1200.
- Population: 10,000-20,000 c. 1100 (unverified). Settlement type: mound-and-plaza ceremonial capital.
- Enclosure: citadel-and-town type. A bastioned log palisade (about 3 km long, rebuilt four times) enclosed the central precinct of about 80 ha; the whole site, about 16 km2 with 120 mounds, had no outer wall.
- Gates: palisade openings are known from excavation, count not established; no bridges.
- Street pattern: no streets. Space is organised as plazas framed by platform mounds; the Grand Plaza (about 19 ha) is the unit. Residential clusters of pole-and-thatch houses sit around courtyards.
- Key places: Monks Mound (30 m high, base 290 x 236 m, the ruler's seat and temple) closes the north side of the Grand Plaza; Mound 72 (elite burial) to the south; Woodhenge (timber circle) to the west; no market known.
- Water: Cahokia Creek on the north; the Mississippi 5 km west; many borrow pits turned to ponds. Terrain: flat floodplain.
- Suburbs: East St. Louis and St. Louis mound groups 10-15 km away form a "Greater Cahokia"; a schematic precinct map of those three is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Map._Greater_Cahokia_(Cahokia_-_,_East_St._Louis_-_and_St._Louis_Precinct._Approx.).png (1229 x 551, PD-1923, verified; shows precinct borders only).
- Adds: a town made of plazas and earth platforms with a palisaded core and no streets at all.

#### Pueblo Bonito, 1877 (Lewis H. Morgan, ground plan)

- Region/culture: Chaco Canyon, New Mexico; Ancestral Puebloan. Plan published 1877 after the 1849 Simpson/Kern survey (a lithograph of that 1850 plan also exists on Commons; not verified here). Building dates 850-1150 CE.
- Population: 800-1,200 if all rooms were dwellings (unverified); several scholars argue under 100 permanent residents.
- Settlement type: single D-shaped great house; the town is one building.
- Enclosure: the building itself. D shape: a straight south wall about 160 m long, a curved rear wall rising four to five storeys that steps down toward the plaza. Footprint about 1.2 ha, 650-800 rooms.
- Gates: one or two narrow openings in the south wall; no bridges.
- Street pattern: none; two enclosed plazas split by a north-south row of rooms; circulation over roofs and through rooms.
- Key places: two great kivas in the plazas and about 30 smaller kivas; no separate temple, palace or market. The Great North Road leaves the canyon from near the building.
- Water: an arroyo (Chaco Wash) in front; seasonal. Terrain: canyon floor, the building backed against the north cliff.
- Suburbs: a dozen other great houses within a few km, small houses on the opposite canyon side.
- Adds: a "town" that is one walled building with internal plazas; the plan is a courtyard-block type at town scale.

#### Cusco, 1861 (Federico Hohagen, in Paz Soldan's Atlas del Peru)

- Region/culture: Andean valley, Peru; Inca capital, Spanish city from 1534. The plan is a surveyed topographic plan of 1861 and still shows the Inca block pattern. The 1565 Ramusio woodcut (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1565_Cusco_Ramusio_Delle_Navigationi_vol3_pp411-412.png, 1126 x 864, Public domain, verified) is a European imagining with a square wall and central plaza; use it only as a style sample, not as geometry.
- Population: c. 20,000 in 1861 (unverified). Settlement type: Inca cosmological capital (puma-shaped plan in the Inca reading) overlaid by a Spanish plaza city.
- Enclosure: none. Citadel outside: Sacsayhuaman, the terraced fortress-temple on the hill to the north, forms the puma's head.
- Gates: none; roads to the four suyus (quarters of the empire) leave from the main plaza. Bridges over the two canalised rivers at many points.
- Street pattern: grid-like but trapezoidal blocks (kancha, walled courtyard compounds about 50-90 m on a side) between two canalised rivers; the Spanish kept the Inca streets.
- Key places: the double plaza (Haukaypata, now Plaza de Armas; Kusipata, now Regocijo) split by the Saphy river, central; cathedral on the plaza's east; Qorikancha (Sun temple, now Santo Domingo) on a terrace at the south-east, near the river confluence (the "puma's tail"); Sacsayhuaman on high ground north.
- Water: two small rivers, Saphy and Tullumayo, both channelled in stone, meeting south-east of the centre. Terrain: valley floor sloping down to the south-east, hills close on the north and west.
- Suburbs: Inca-period outer wards on the slopes; by 1861 continuous.
- Adds: trapezoidal courtyard blocks between two channelled streams, a double plaza split by water, and a fortress-temple outside the town on a hill.

#### Mexico City, 1628 (Juan Gomez de Trasmonte; 1907 lithograph of the engraving)

- Region/culture: Valley of Mexico; Spanish viceregal capital on the site of Tenochtitlan. Bird's-eye pictorial plan with a numbered key.
- Population: c. 100,000 including Indian barrios (unverified). Settlement type: grid (traza) on a lake island with causeways and canals.
- Enclosure: none. The Spanish traza was about 1.5 x 1.5 km (about 200 ha); Indian barrios around it.
- Gates: none; the causeways (west, south, north) and the aqueduct are the controlled entries. Many bridges over canals inside the grid.
- Street pattern: orthogonal grid with the Plaza Mayor at the centre; blocks elongated, about 90 x 180 m (unverified); several canals (acequias) run along grid lines and were used by canoes.
- Key places: Plaza Mayor central with the cathedral on its north and the viceroy's palace on its east; market on and beside the plaza; convents spread evenly through the grid; no harbour, canoe landings on the canals; Tlatelolco church and plaza in the north.
- Water: lake on all sides in 1628 (the great flood came 1629); canals in the grid; causeways to the shore. Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: Indian barrios (San Juan Tenochtitlan, Santiago Tlatelolco) surround the traza, together larger than the traza, with irregular streets.
- Adds: a regular grid cut by navigable canals, with an orthogonal core and an organic native ring, all on a lake.

#### Lima, 1688 (Joseph Mulder, engraved plan after the 1685-87 wall survey)

- Region/culture: Peruvian coast; Spanish viceregal capital founded 1535. The engraving illustrates Echave y Assu's 1688 book; the Mulder plan is the first printed plan of Lima.
- Population: 35,000-40,000 (unverified). Settlement type: Laws-of-the-Indies grid inside a bastioned wall, river port with the sea port (Callao) 12 km away.
- Enclosure: one ring, built 1684-87: a bastioned brick wall with 34 bastions, polygonal on the south, east and west, open to the river on the north. Enclosed area about 5 km2 (unverified).
- Gates: 5 at first, later up to 10, on the landward sides; one stone bridge (1610) over the Rimac to the north.
- Street pattern: regular grid; the 1535 foundation had 117 blocks of about 125 m square (9 x 13); by 1688 the grid had grown to fill the wall. The wall does not follow the grid, so irregular slivers lie between the last blocks and the rampart.
- Key places: Plaza Mayor central in the original grid, with cathedral on its east and the viceroy's palace on its north; market on the plaza; river front on the north edge of the town; no harbour.
- Water: Rimac river along the north side; irrigation ditches through the grid. Terrain: flat alluvial fan, slight slope to the river.
- Suburbs: San Lazaro across the bridge on the north bank, inside the wall's northern arm; small.
- Adds: a large regular grid with a bastioned polygon wall that cuts across it, and a river on one side instead of a wall.

#### Havana, 1739 (Peter Chassereau; Homann Erben, Nuremberg 1740)

- Region/culture: Cuba; Spanish colonial port. Hand-coloured plan with soundings and a harbour-entrance view; reissued in a 1762 atlas.
- Population: c. 30,000 (unverified). Settlement type: walled harbour city with castles at the harbour mouth.
- Enclosure: one ring, finished 1740: a bastioned wall on the landward (west and south) sides with the harbour front open; enclosed area about 140 ha (unverified). Citadels: El Morro on the east headland of the entrance, La Punta on the west headland, La Fuerza inside the town on the harbour front.
- Gates: 9 by the end of the 18th century (unverified for 1739); no bridges.
- Street pattern: a grid that is not quite orthogonal (streets bend a few degrees between the harbour front and the wall); blocks about 80 x 100 m.
- Key places: Plaza de Armas with La Fuerza and the governor's house on the harbour front, not central; cathedral one block inland; several smaller plazas (Plaza Vieja = market) spread through the grid; harbour on the east edge, the town being the west shore of a narrow-necked bag-shaped bay.
- Water: sea on the north, harbour on the east, a narrow entrance channel between two castles. Terrain: flat, low rise to the west.
- Suburbs: small extramural settlement west of the wall in 1739; by 1800 larger than the walled city.
- Adds: a harbour mouth guarded by paired castles, a wall only on the land side, and a slightly skewed grid.

#### Quebec, 1727 (Gaspard-Joseph Chaussegros de Lery, royal engineer)

- Region/culture: St Lawrence, New France; French colonial capital. Manuscript plan. The 1663 Bourdon plan (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Veritable_plan_de_Quebec_fait_en_1663_-_Jean_Bourdon.jpg, 11418 x 5015, Public domain, verified) shows the same site sixty years earlier with far fewer houses.
- Population: c. 5,000 (unverified). Settlement type: cliff-top upper town plus waterfront lower town; river port and fortress.
- Enclosure: citadel-and-town. Upper town on the Cap Diamant promontory (cliff about 100 m above the river) enclosed on its landward (west) side by a bastioned enceinte designed by de Lery; lower town on a strip between cliff foot and the river, unwalled; fort (Chateau St-Louis) on the cliff edge above the lower town.
- Gates: two to three landward gates in the western wall (St-Louis, St-Jean); the climb Cote de la Montagne links lower and upper town; no bridges.
- Street pattern: organic in the upper town, bending with the promontory; a small rectilinear pattern around Place Royale in the lower town; linear along the river strip.
- Key places: governor's chateau on the cliff edge (high ground, central to the upper town); cathedral, seminary, Jesuit and Ursuline convents occupy the upper town's large blocks; market and merchants at Place Royale in the lower town, on the water; harbour where the St-Charles meets the St Lawrence on the north.
- Water: St Lawrence on the south-east, St-Charles on the north; the town is on the tip between them. Terrain: cliff, promontory, slope.
- Suburbs: St-Roch along the St-Charles below the cliff, and St-Jean outside the west gate, small in 1727.
- Adds: a two-level town on a cliff with an upper fortified administrative/religious town and a lower commercial strip, walled only on the land side of a promontory.

#### New Orleans, 1720 plan published 1759 (Pierre Le Blond de la Tour; engraved Thomas Jefferys)

- Region/culture: lower Mississippi, French Louisiana. The engraving reproduces the 1720 foundation grid and adds the ditch-and-bastion enclosure built in the 1730s-60s. An unfortified 1764 Bellin version is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Plan_de_la_Nouvelle_Orleans._LOC_2003627002.jpg (2500 x 1800, Public domain, verified).
- Population: c. 1,000 in 1726, c. 4,000 in 1760 (both unverified). Settlement type: planned grid on a river levee.
- Enclosure: one ring: a rectangular ditch, palisade and earth rampart with corner and mid-side bastions around the grid (the present Vieux Carre); about 60 ha.
- Gates: two to three in the landward sides; no bridges.
- Street pattern: pure grid, 11 blocks along the river by 6 deep (66 blocks), each block about 90 m square (300 French feet), streets about 11 m.
- Key places: Place d'Armes on the river front, centred on the long side, with the church behind it and the government and barracks flanking; market on the levee by the plaza; the harbour is the levee itself. All key places are on the river edge, not in the middle.
- Water: the Mississippi in a crescent on the south; swamp and Lake Pontchartrain behind. Terrain: flat, below river level behind the levee.
- Suburbs: none in 1720; faubourgs appear after 1788, continuing the grid upstream and downstream.
- Adds: a grid whose "centre" is on its water edge, fully enclosed by a rectangular bastioned ditch; the generator's plainest planned-port case.

#### New Amsterdam, 1660 (Castello Plan; Johannes Vingboons after Jacques Cortelyou)

- Region/culture: Manhattan tip, New Netherland; Dutch colonial trading post. Watercolour copy made for the Medici; a 1916 redraft by Adams and Phelps Stokes is at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Castelloplan.jpg (3267 x 2401, Public domain, verified) and is easier to read.
- Population: c. 1,500 (unverified). Settlement type: fortified trading post on a peninsula tip.
- Enclosure: citadel-and-town. Square four-bastion Fort Amsterdam at the southern tip; a palisade wall with a ditch across the island from river to river on the north (the Wall Street line); water on the other two sides. Enclosed area about 30 ha (unverified).
- Gates: two in the wall, the land gate on the Broadway line and the water gate on the East River shore; a bridge over the canal.
- Street pattern: one spine (Broadway) from fort to land gate; a canal (Heere Gracht, now Broad Street) cut inland from the East River with streets on both banks; the rest irregular, with large garden plots inside the blocks.
- Key places: fort with the church inside it at the tip (edge, not centre); the Stadt Huys (town hall) on the East River waterfront; market by the canal mouth; harbour along the East River shore with a single pier.
- Water: rivers on two sides, canal inside. Terrain: flat, low.
- Suburbs: scattered farms (bouweries) north of the wall.
- Adds: a town with a wall on one side only and water on the rest, a fort at the tip rather than the centre, and an internal canal as a street.

#### Savannah, 1734 (Peter Gordon; plat by Noble Jones; engraved Pierre Fourdrinier)

- Region/culture: Georgia, British colony founded 1733 by Oglethorpe. Bird's-eye view from the river showing the first four wards after fourteen months.
- Population: a few hundred (unverified). Settlement type: planned ward-and-square grid on a river bluff.
- Enclosure: none (a palisade and a battery on the bluff edge are drawn). The four wards together cover about 20 ha.
- Gates: none; no bridges; a crane on the bluff lifts goods from the river landing.
- Street pattern: repeating ward unit. Each ward is about 205 x 183 m with a central square of about 96 x 82 m; four tythings of ten house lots (each lot 60 x 90 ft, 18 x 27 m) on the north and south sides, four trust lots for public buildings on the east and west sides of the square. Wards are separated by wide streets. The town later grew to 24 wards by repeating the unit.
- Key places: public buildings on the trust lots facing the squares; the church site on a trust lot; the "market" is the squares; the river landing at the foot of the bluff is the harbour; Oglethorpe's tent is drawn on the bluff edge.
- Water: Savannah River on the north, with a 12 m bluff. Terrain: flat plateau above the river.
- Suburbs: none in 1734; garden and farm lots in rings outside the town by plan.
- Adds: a cellular ward system with a square per ward, i.e. a grid that has many small centres instead of one plaza.

#### Washington, D.C., 1791 (Pierre Charles L'Enfant; 1887 facsimile)

- Region/culture: Potomac, United States; federal capital planned on farmland. The Commons file is the 1887 Coast and Geodetic Survey facsimile of the deteriorating manuscript held at the Library of Congress.
- Population: none in 1791; 3,210 in 1800 (census, unverified). Settlement type: baroque radial-over-grid capital.
- Enclosure: none. Planned area about 2,600 ha.
- Gates: none; bridges planned over Rock Creek and the Anacostia.
- Street pattern: an orthogonal grid overlaid with diagonal avenues (about 50 m wide) radiating from the Capitol and the President's House and from fifteen state squares, giving star intersections and triangular blocks; grid blocks range from about 100 to 200 m.
- Key places: Capitol on Jenkins Hill (high ground, the focal point of the avenues); President's House 1.6 km to the west-north-west on a second rise, the two joined by Pennsylvania Avenue and by the Mall at right angles; a planned market and commercial waterfront on the Anacostia (east, edge); no temple.
- Water: Potomac on the west and south, Anacostia on the east, Tiber Creek canalised along the Mall. Terrain: flat river terrace with low hills for the two main buildings.
- Suburbs: Georgetown (existing port) at the west edge; Alexandria across the river.
- Adds: radial avenues and star plazas combined with a grid, and two monumental foci on separate hills; the only baroque plan in the Americas slice.

#### Circleville, Ohio, 1836 (author unknown)

- Region/culture: Scioto valley, Ohio; United States county seat founded 1810. Map of 1836, the year before residents began "squaring" the circle (finished by 1856).
- Population: 1,000-1,500 (unverified). Settlement type: concentric circular town laid on a Hopewell earthwork.
- Enclosure: none; the plan follows a prehistoric circular earthwork about 330 m in diameter, which had an adjoining square earthwork on the east.
- Gates: none; the Scioto river to the west, no bridge in the town itself.
- Street pattern: two concentric ring streets around an octagonal courthouse on a central circle, four radial streets at the cardinal points, and a standard square grid taking over outside the outer ring and in the square earthwork.
- Key places: courthouse (ruler's seat) in the exact centre; churches on the rings; market and trade on the radials; no harbour (the canal reached the town in the 1830s on the west).
- Water: Scioto river and the Ohio and Erie Canal on the west edge. Terrain: flat terrace.
- Suburbs: the square grid outside the circle.
- Adds: a true concentric-ring plan with radial streets, a central public building, and a circle-to-grid transition at its edge.

### Part B

#### Lhasa, 1878-79 (pundit A-K, printed 1902 in Das)

- Region, culture: central Tibet, 3,650 m; Tibetan Buddhist theocratic capital. The plan was surveyed covertly by Kishen Singh ("A-K") in 1878-79 and printed as "Plan of Lhasa, scale one mile" in Sarat Chandra Das, *Journey to Lhasa and Central Tibet* (London, 1902), page 178.
- Depicts: 1878. Plan date: engraving 1902 from the 1878 survey.
- Population: 20,000-30,000 including monks (unverified). Landon (1905) and Waddell (1905) give figures of this order; 1951 estimate 25,000 excluding 15,000 monks.
- Type: pilgrimage centre and monastic capital on a river plain.
- Enclosure: none. The town has no wall. Its edge is defined by the Lingkhor, the outer pilgrim circuit (about 8 km, unverified) which runs round Potala, Chakpori and the town. The plan shows the Kyi Chu river to the south and a canal to the north and east.
- Gates: none. Roads leave radially to Phembu (north), Rigya and Dagyeba (east), Dechen ferry (south-east). No bridge drawn across the Kyi Chu; the Yutok bridge is a canal bridge inside town.
- Street pattern: organic around a ring. The Jokhang temple (no. 12 on the plan, "Jhu") sits at the centre with the Barkhor circuit around it; the dense town is a block about 1 km across east of the Potala. Large walled compounds of nobles (shaded) line the Barkhor; blocks are 50-150 m.
- Key places: ruler's seat (Potala on Marpori hill) 1.5 km west of the market centre, on high ground, outside the dense town; Chakpori medical college on the second hill opposite; main temple (Jokhang) central; market = the Barkhor ring round the temple; Ramoche temple at the north edge; Sera and Drepung monasteries 4-8 km outside; Chinese and Tibetan parade grounds north of town; walled garden estates ("-linga", "-lingka") scattered in the fields 0.5-2 km out.
- Water: Kyi Chu river 1 km south of the town edge, canal ring; town stands on the flat valley floor. Terrain flat with two isolated rock hills (Marpori, Chakpori).
- Suburbs: the town is all suburb in European terms: the dense core (about 1 km) is surrounded by scattered walled estates and monasteries over 10 km of cultivated plain.
- Adds to range: an unwalled sacred capital whose "edge" is a pilgrim circuit, with the ruler's citadel on an isolated rock 1.5 km from the temple-market centre; concentric ritual rings (Nangkhor-Barkhor-Lingkhor) as the street-pattern generator.

#### Lo Manthang, town founded 1380 (photograph 2008, Wimbex)

- Region, culture: Upper Mustang, Nepal, 3,840 m; Tibetan Buddhist kingdom of Lo. Founded by Ame Pal in 1380; wall and three temples from that century.
- Image: ground-level view from a hill showing the compact walled block set in fields, 2008. No historic plan is on Commons; this is a type-4 record.
- Population: 900 in about 180 houses (unverified, travel sources, undated); 2011 census for the whole rural municipality 1,899-2,350 (sources conflict).
- Type: walled highland royal town and caravan stop on the Kali Gandaki salt route.
- Enclosure: one ring, roughly rectangular, mud-brick, about 750 m perimeter (2,472 ft, unverified), 8 m high, 14 towers. Enclosed area about 3 ha.
- Gates: one, on the east (north-east) side. No bridges; the town stands on a dry plateau terrace above a small stream.
- Street pattern: organic, narrow lanes between contiguous flat-roofed houses; blocks 20-40 m; the whole town is one built mass with lanes cut through it.
- Key places: king's palace (four storeys) and the three gompas (Jampa 1387, Thubchen 1472, Chode) all inside the wall, the palace near the centre and the two big temples in the south-west quarter; no formal market square; monastic quarter in the north-east.
- Water: none inside; stream and irrigated fields outside. Terrain: flat terrace of a wide dry valley, hills on every side.
- Suburbs: none in 1380-1900; some houses outside the gate now.
- Adds to range: smallest possible walled town (3 ha, one gate, 180 houses) with the ruler's palace and the main temples all inside; a single-gate closed rectangle with no street grid.

#### Tobolsk, 1699-1701 (Semyon Remezov, Drawing Book of Siberia)

- Region, culture: Russian Siberia, capital of the Siberian voivodeship, founded 1587; plan drawn 1699-1701 by the Tobolsk cartographer Remezov for the *Chertyozhnaya kniga Sibiri*, published St Petersburg 1882.
- Depicts: 1700. Catalogue in the margin names about 100 churches, towers, streets and places.
- Population: unknown. (The 1897 census gives 20,400; no 1700 count found.)
- Type: river-confluence administrative and trade capital; kremlin + lower town.
- Enclosure: citadel + town. Upper town (kremlin, later stone from 1700) on the bluff of Trinity cape; a palisaded "small town" next to it; the lower town on the floodplain is drawn unwalled with a ditch and earth bank on its outer side.
- Gates: towers and gates along the palisade are numbered in the catalogue; a long wooden ramp/stair connects upper and lower town (drawn centre-left). Bridges over the Kurdyumka stream.
- Street pattern: upper town is a regular grid of long house rows oriented to the bluff edge; lower town is a fan of streets radiating from the foot of the bluff, with blocks of 40-80 m; a separate Tatar quarter south of the river.
- Key places: voivode's court, cathedral (St Sophia) and prikaz (chancery) all in the kremlin on high ground; market and trading rows at the foot of the bluff in the lower town; monasteries (Znamensky) at the town edge; a Bukharan-Tatar suburb across the Irtysh.
- Water: Irtysh river along the south edge (drawn at the bottom, labelled "Niz Irtysha"), Tobol mouth at the right; the Kurdyumka cuts through the lower town. Terrain: 60 m bluff over a floodplain.
- Suburbs: lower town is larger than the upper; outlying villages drawn round the whole sheet.
- Adds to range: two-level town where the whole ruling complex stands on a bluff and the commercial town fans out below it, drawn in a non-European (Muscovite) cartographic idiom with every street named in a margin catalogue.

#### Yakutsk, 1692 (Nicolaes Witsen, engraved view)

- Region, culture: Russian ostrog on the Lena, founded 1632; drawing published in Witsen's *Noord en Oost Tartarye* (Amsterdam 1692), reproduced 1882. It is an engraver's bird's-eye from travellers' reports, not a survey; treat proportions as indicative.
- Population: unknown; Cossack garrison plus traders, in the hundreds (unverified).
- Type: palisaded fort town (ostrog) at a river landing, fur-trade and tribute post.
- Enclosure: citadel + town. An oval wooden palisade (vertical logs, tyn) with about a dozen square towers encloses the whole town; a smaller rectangular fort ("Het Casteel", the ostrog proper) with its own wall and corner towers sits at the eastern end inside the ring.
- Gates: river gate with tower on the south (no. 11 "Strant Poort"), a second gate at the fort (no. 5 "Slot Poort" with a clock tower). No bridge; boats on the Lena.
- Street pattern: organic, loosely rectilinear rows of log houses; no plan visible.
- Key places: cathedral church (1) and monastery (2) inside the ring near the fort; court house (3), commander's house (4) and prison (6) inside the fort; shops/inn (12) and a second church (13) in the town; well inside the fort (8).
- Water: Lena river along the south front; landing beach. Terrain: flat.
- Suburbs: none drawn.
- Adds to range: all-timber oval palisade with towers (no masonry) and a separate inner fort; the "fort within a stockade" pattern the generator needs for frontier posts.

#### Tianluokeng tulou cluster, buildings 1796-1966 (photograph 2014)

- Region, culture: Nanjing county, Fujian; Hakka clan (Huang family). Five tulou: Buyun Lou (square, 1796, rebuilt 1953), Hechang Lou (round), Zhenchang Lou (round, 1930), Ruiyun Lou (round, 1936), Wenchang Lou (oval, 1966). Dates unverified beyond Buyun 1796.
- Image: view from the hill above, four round/oval and one square tulou visible with lesser houses between.
- Population: about 700 (unverified).
- Type: clan fortress-house cluster; each tulou is one building housing dozens of households.
- Enclosure: each tulou is its own ring, 3 storeys, rammed earth 1-2 m thick, one gate, no outer windows on the ground floor; round ones about 40-60 m across (unverified); no wall round the cluster.
- Gates: one per tulou, facing downhill; no bridges.
- Street pattern: none between the tulou; irrigation channels, paths and lesser houses fill the gaps. Inside each: a ring of identical rooms around an open court; ancestral hall at the centre of the court.
- Key places: ancestral hall central in each tulou; wells inside; no market; temple outside the cluster.
- Water: hill stream; terraced paddies above. Terrain: slope of a valley side.
- Suburbs: none; the cluster is the village.
- Adds to range: a settlement made of a handful of giant circular and square buildings rather than streets and blocks; the "house is the wall" type.

#### Aït Benhaddou ksar, 17th century and later (photograph 2015)

- Region, culture: Ounila valley on the Marrakesh-Ouarzazate caravan route, Morocco; Amazigh. UNESCO 1987. Image taken from the ruined agadir (granary) on the summit looking down over the ksar to the river.
- Population: over 90 families in the 1940s, about 5-10 today (unverified).
- Type: fortified caravan village (ksar) of several kasbahs.
- Enclosure: one ring, irregular, following the hill contour, rammed earth with corner towers; the whole hill from river bank to summit is enclosed, about 3-4 ha (unverified). Agadir on the summit as a last refuge.
- Gates: one main gate on the river (west) side; in 2011 a footbridge replaced the ford.
- Street pattern: stepped organic lanes rising from the gate to the agadir; houses are tower-kasbahs (decorated corner towers) of 15-25 m footprint packed together.
- Key places: mosque inside near the base; caravanserai and public square just inside the gate; marabout tomb outside; granary on the summit (high ground); threshing floor outside the wall.
- Water: Ounila river (seasonal) at the foot; palm and olive gardens on the floodplain. Terrain: steep hill rising from the river.
- Suburbs: the modern village on the opposite bank is now larger than the ksar.
- Adds to range: a hillside fortress village with no streets in the usual sense, towers as houses, and the citadel reduced to a granary at the top.

#### Derinkuyu underground city, Byzantine use 5th-10th century (plan board, photographed 2007)

- Region, culture: Cappadocia, Turkey; cut in volcanic tuff, enlarged under Byzantine rule as a refuge from raids. The image is a photograph of the engraved site plan board at the entrance; the board's author and date are not stated, so treat it as an unattributed modern plan (unverified).
- Population: refuge for up to 20,000 with livestock (unverified, widely repeated).
- Type: underground refuge city under an ordinary village.
- Enclosure: none in plan; defence is by depth (18 levels, 85 m, unverified) and rolling stone doors that close each level from inside.
- Gates: several surface entrances inside village houses; a tunnel of about 8 km to Kaymaklı (unverified).
- Street pattern: branching galleries ("cul-de-sac cluster" in plan) with rooms off them; no grid; in plan the complex is a sprawling tree about 300 m across (scale not on the board).
- Key places: chapel (cruciform) on the lowest level, school/meeting hall with barrel vault on level 2, stables and wine presses near the top, wells and ventilation shafts (more than 50) passing through all levels.
- Water: wells reaching 55 m. Terrain: flat plateau above.
- Suburbs: not applicable.
- Adds to range: a settlement with a third dimension instead of a wall; useful for the generator's "dungeon under the town" case only, so a low-priority calibration target.

#### Skellig Michael monastery, 6th-12th century (plan 2017 after the 2008 management plan)

- Region, culture: rock island 12 km off Co. Kerry, Ireland; early Irish monastic community.
- Depicts: the surviving layout; drawn by Sémhur in 2017 from the Skellig Michael World Heritage Site Management Plan 2008-2018, p. 22. French labels.
- Population: 12 monks and an abbot (unverified tradition).
- Type: cliff-terrace monastic enclosure; a settlement with no economy but prayer and fishing.
- Enclosure: dry-stone retaining and enclosing walls around a terrace of about 60 x 30 m at 180 m above the sea, with a lower garden terrace and an upper garden terrace; total walled area under 0.5 ha.
- Gates: one entrance from the stair on the south-east; three stone stairways climb from three landings.
- Street pattern: none; a single open court. Six beehive cells (A-F, each 3-5 m across) in a row along the north wall, a seventh (G) on the lower terrace; two oratories (2, 3) and the small church (1) on the south side; cemetery with leacht (altar) between.
- Key places: church on the edge of the terrace, cells against the cliff, cemetery between, cisterns (three) cut in the rock; the hermitage is 100 m higher on the south peak.
- Water: rain cisterns only; Atlantic on every side. Terrain: cliff and ledge.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds to range: the minimum settlement, a dozen buildings on a 0.2 ha ledge with walls that hold the ground up rather than keep enemies out.

#### Nan Madol, built c. 1180-1600 (map 2025, Quarot, after Hambruch 1910)

- Region, culture: south-east coast of Pohnpei, Micronesia; Saudeleur dynasty. UNESCO 2016.
- Depicts: the islets as surveyed; the SVG is a Commons author's redrawing of an earlier Commons map, whose ultimate source is Paul Hambruch's 1910 survey; scale bar 0-200 m.
- Population: about 1,000 residents on the islets at peak (unverified); chiefs, priests and servants only.
- Type: ceremonial and royal capital built on artificial islets on a reef flat.
- Enclosure: sea walls (breakwaters) of basalt on the seaward south and east sides, up to 300 m long; no wall on the land side; 92-100 islets over about 60-70 ha including canals, the core 1.5 x 0.5 km.
- Gates: none; entry by canoe through gaps in the breakwater; one causeway to Temwen island at the north-west (Peinkitel).
- Street pattern: canals are the streets, a loose grid of rectangular islets 20-100 m on a side separated by 5-15 m channels; two halves: Madol Pah (south-west, residential and administrative) and Madol Powe (north-east, priestly and mortuary).
- Key places: Nandauwas (royal mortuary, walls 5.5-7.5 m) at the seaward east corner; Pahn Kadira (ruler's residence) in the west; Idedh and Darong (ritual, eel pond) centre; Usen Dau, Kohnderek (priests) north-east.
- Water: lagoon, tidal; canals waist-deep at high tide. Terrain: reef flat beside a mangrove shore.
- Suburbs: commoners lived on Temwen and the mainland, outside the complex.
- Adds to range: a Venice-type grid of water streets where the "blocks" are built islands, the ruler's compound is at one end and the tomb-temple at the other, and the only defence faces the sea.

#### Ruapekapeka pā, 1845-46 (Cowan 1922 after Marlow 1846)

- Region, culture: Northland, New Zealand; Ngāti Hine under Kawiti, Northern War. Plan published 1922 in James Cowan, *The New Zealand Wars*, redrawn from the 1846 survey by Capt. Marlow RE; a cross-section on Commons (File:Cross-section_of_ruapekapeka_pa.jpg, 838 x 452, same source and licence) shows the palisade, trench and bombproof pits.
- Depicts: January 1846.
- Population: 400-500 defenders; a purpose-built fortress, not a village.
- Type: hilltop gunfighter pā, built to absorb artillery.
- Enclosure: one ring, irregular polygon with projecting flanking angles (bastions) at the corners; two rows of palisade 3-5 m high; inner trench. Size 90 x 65 m to 150 x 70 m (sources conflict), about 0.6-1 ha.
- Gates: none in the plan; entries were gaps covered by flanks; two breaches shown on the south-east.
- Street pattern: internal grid of about 40 rectangular sunken shelters and rifle pits in rows, with two wells, lanes 2-4 m between; a separate small annexe on the east.
- Key places: Kawiti's house in the centre of the main block; gun positions at the west and south-east angles; wells inside.
- Water: wells only. Terrain: ridge top, 300 m above sea level, forest on the approaches.
- Suburbs: none.
- Adds to range: a fortress whose interior is a regular grid of pits, built in weeks and abandoned; a bastioned trace in timber and earth without European geometry.

#### uMgungundlovu, 1829-1838 (view c. 1840, author unknown; Gardiner 1836 plan in the PDF)

- Region, culture: eMakhosini valley, Zululand, South Africa; capital of Dingane. The Commons file is a small (400 x 208) reproduction of a c. 1840 view showing the oval enclosure on the hillside. The 1836 plan by Capt. A. F. Gardiner is inside File:Narrative_of_a_journey_to_the_Zoolu_country,_in_South_Africa_(IA_narrativeofjourn00gard).pdf on Commons (page not verified); a 1878 derivative drawing of the king's hut is File:Dingan_at_home.jpg (1,474 x 1,137, Flickr Commons "no known copyright restrictions").
- Depicts: the enlarged town of 1835-38, burnt by Dingane in 1838.
- Population: 5,000-7,000 in 1,400-1,700 huts; about 500 women in the royal enclosure.
- Type: royal military homestead (ikhanda), the Zulu capital.
- Enclosure: one ring, oval, palisade of poles; long axis several hundred metres (archaeological plans give the order of 500-600 m, unverified); within it a second oval, the great cattle enclosure, leaving an annular band of huts 4-8 rows deep.
- Gates: one main gate at the downhill (north) end of the long axis; the royal enclosure (isigodlo) at the opposite uphill end, with its own fence and a private cattle fold below it.
- Street pattern: concentric rings; huts arranged in arcs; regiments quartered by sector; no streets, just the rings and radial passages.
- Key places: ruler's seat on the high end of the axis opposite the gate; central open arena (parade, court, cattle); no temple, no market; execution hill (kwaMatiwane) outside.
- Water: two streams either side of the spur. Terrain: gentle slope.
- Suburbs: none; satellite ikhanda elsewhere in the valley.
- Adds to range: the pure concentric-oval plan with an empty centre and the ruler at the top of a single axis; the only entry in the corpus with no streets at all.

#### Acoma (Old Acoma Pueblo), survey 1934 (HABS NM-6, Longfellow and Kidder)

- Region, culture: Cibola County, New Mexico; Keresan Pueblo, occupied since at least 1150. Sheet 2 of 83, "The Plot Plan", scale 1/64" = 1', 12 June 1934; sheet 1 (same series) carries an explanatory text with the figures used below.
- Depicts: 1934 state.
- Population: HABS states most Acomans had already moved to farms in the San José valley 13-20 km away; the mesa village was seasonally occupied. Tribe about 1,000-1,200 (unverified).
- Type: mesa-top pueblo (defensive farming village).
- Enclosure: none built; the mesa's cliffs are the wall. Rock surface about 7 ha (17 acres), 110 m (360 ft) above the plain; houses occupy the northern half.
- Gates: a single stair-trail cut in the rock on the south-west (the "Padre's trail") and a later wagon road; no bridges.
- Street pattern: three east-west rows of long house blocks (9 blocks, about 100 houses), each block 55-115 m long (180-373 ft) and 10-18 m deep, with two broad east-west streets/plazas 20-30 m wide between the rows and narrow cross passages; all houses face south, stepping back from three storeys to one.
- Key places: no ruler's seat; council house in a small block south of row 3; seven kivas built into the house blocks (not freestanding); mission church San Esteban (1629-41) with its walled cemetery stands apart at the south edge of the village on the open rock; no market.
- Water: natural rock cisterns on the mesa top; no river. Terrain: isolated sandstone mesa.
- Suburbs: the real population lived in valley farm villages (Acomita, McCartys), 13-20 km away.
- Adds to range: parallel long-block rows with one-sided (south-facing) frontages, no streets in the European sense, and a church placed outside the house rows on bare rock.

#### Brunei Town and Kampong Ayer, 1952 (Government of Brunei aerial photograph)

- Region, culture: Brunei River estuary, Borneo; Malay sultanate. Published in the Brunei Annual Report 1953.
- Depicts: 1952, before the 1958 land-resettlement scheme and before the 1958 mosque.
- Population: Kampong Ayer about 15,000 (unverified, undated); it held most of the capital's people until the 1960s.
- Type: stilt water-town (the historic capital) with a British-period grid town on reclaimed land beside it.
- Enclosure: none.
- Gates: none; plank walkways and boat landings; the land town's padang (field) fronts the river.
- Street pattern: two patterns in one frame. The water town is a loose cluster of house groups on piles, each group (kampong) of 10-40 houses 1-3 m apart joined by plank bridges, with open water lanes between groups; the land town is a small orthogonal grid of 60-100 m blocks of shophouses around the padang.
- Key places: sultan's palace (Istana) on land to the west, outside the frame; mosque in the water town (old Kampong Ayer mosque on piles); market at the river's edge where land meets water; government offices and padang central in the grid.
- Water: the whole historic town stands in the river; the land town is on filled ground. Terrain: tidal estuary with hills behind.
- Suburbs: the water town is the core and the grid is the suburb; in 1952 the water town was several times larger.
- Adds to range: a town whose "blocks" are clusters of pile houses on open water, with no streets and no wall, next to a tiny colonial grid; the only entry where water is the ground.

#### Batad, Ifugao, 2023 vertical drone view (Bien02)

- Region, culture: Banaue municipality, Cordillera, Luzon; Ifugao terrace farmers. Part of the UNESCO Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras (1995). The village layout is pre-colonial in type; terraces are dated by various studies from 2,000 to 400 years old (disputed).
- Depicts: 2023 state; the image is a vertical photograph with the stream gorge on the right.
- Population: 869 in 192 households (2015 census, whole barangay).
- Type: terraced hill village inside an amphitheatre of irrigated terraces.
- Enclosure: none.
- Gates: none; footpaths along terrace dykes; one trail in over the saddle.
- Street pattern: none. Houses stand in two or three clusters of 15-40 on terrace edges and spurs (upper-left and lower-left in the image), 5-15 m apart, linked only by terrace dykes and stone steps.
- Key places: no ruler, temple or market; a rice granary and ritual rice-field (puna) belong to the senior family; a chapel and school are modern additions in the upper cluster.
- Water: spring-fed irrigation channels feeding every terrace; a river gorge below. Terrain: 30-40 degree slope, the whole hillside terraced in stone-walled steps 2-6 m high.
- Suburbs: none; the terrace field is the settlement.
- Adds to range: a village with no streets and no centre, whose entire form is the contour geometry of the terraces; the generator's hillside-contour case.

## 10. Caveats

- **Reconstructions.** Baghdad Round City, Teotihuacan, Heijo-kyo, Alexandria and Karakorum are
  scholars' reconstructions; Baghdad in particular is a reading of texts with nothing above
  ground. Use them for topology, never for block sizes.
- **Photographs standing in for plans** (type 4): Lo Manthang, Tianluokeng, Ait Benhaddou,
  Batad, Trelleborg aerial, Brunei 1952. They show the present or recent state, not the
  historic one.
- **Pictorial views from hearsay**: Benin City (Dapper 1668, the engraver never saw it),
  Yakutsk (Witsen 1692), Cusco 1565 (Ramusio, a European imagining, listed only as a style
  sample). Topology is plausible, proportions are not.
- **Small images**: uMgungundlovu (400 px), Dholavira (SVG schematic), Great Zimbabwe (651 px),

## 11. Recommendations

Proposed in the PR that carries this report; Jeffrey decides at milestone 5, where
DECISIONS.md already lists the open decision on how `sweep` metrics are compared.

1. **Keep the three calibration cities on the first corpus.** Truro, Arcachon and Bearna are
   late-medieval European types; `town-plan-corpus.md` §4 already names Dublin, Luxembourg and
   Durham for them. Nothing here changes that.
2. **Use this corpus for the expressive-range check, not for calibration.** A generator
   calibrated on Truro will produce D-on-water organic ports; the sweep (REQUIREMENTS.md §7)
   should show whether a different spec and rule pack can reach a Jaipur, a Palmanova, a Fez,
   a Tenochtitlan or a Kano. Proposed minimum set of ten edge-case towns to trace for the
   harness, one per hard trait: Jaipur (planned grid, palace central), Palmanova (radial,
   nonagon), Fez (cul-de-sac cluster, twin city), Suzhou (canal grid, water gates), Kano (wall
   round fields and hills), Moscow (four rings, dozens of gates), Mont-Saint-Michel (one gate,
   one street, island), Madurai (concentric rings round a temple), Quebec (upper and lower
   town on a cliff), Teotihuacan (no wall, one axis, compound blocks).
3. **Rule packs are where these belong.** Most rows in §9.4 and §9.6 are setting rules
   ("markets are outside the royal square", "temples guard the outer edges", "the palace faces
   the river across a screen hill"), which is what ADR-006 makes pluggable. This corpus is the
   evidence that those rules differ by culture and are not physics.
4. **Do not widen F2 for v1.** Everything here is reachable with constraints, districts,
   landmarks and the existing enclosure and water concepts, with one exception: "water as
   ground" (Kampong Ayer, Nan Madol, Venice). That is a v2 question; Jeffrey decides whether
   to add it to the F2b reserved list.
5. **A later pass** can verify the gaps in §10 one region at a time; the "Not found" lists say
   exactly what was searched so nothing is repeated.

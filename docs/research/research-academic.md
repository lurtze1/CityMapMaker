# Procedural City Layout Generation: Literature Review for a Spec-Driven Fantasy Town Generator

Date: 2026-10-19. Scope: academic and technical work on city/town *layout* generation (roads, blocks, districts, walls, landmarks), constraint-based and mixed-initiative PCG, evaluation of generator diversity, and 2023-2026 LLM-driven PCG.

Legend for claims: **[V]** = verified against the paper's abstract/page or full text during this review; **[M]** = from reviewer's prior knowledge of the paper, not re-verified this session; **[U]** = unverified / inferred.

Takeaway: no single published method covers the full spec (terrain + water + walls + districts + pinned landmarks + incremental edits). The two columns your spec cares most about, *pinned landmarks* and *incremental edits*, are covered best by discrete constraint solving (ASP / CSP) for the macro layer and by Lipp et al.'s layering idea for the geometric layer.

## 2. Per-paper sections

### 2.1 Parish & Müller 2001, "Procedural Modeling of Cities" (SIGGRAPH 2001; basis of CityEngine)
- URL: https://history.siggraph.org/?p=116923 ; DOI 10.1145/383259.383292
- **Method [V]:** Extended L-system grows a road network. Each production emits an "ideal successor" whose parameters are instantiated by a *global goals* function (street pattern per region: rectangular grid, radial, "natural"/population-following) and then corrected by *local constraints* (snap to nearby intersections, avoid water/steep slope, clip to legal area). Highways grow toward population-density peaks; streets fill between them. Blocks are then subdivided into lots recursively; buildings by a separate shape L-system.
- **Inputs [V]:** image maps: land/water boundary, elevation, population density, optional street-pattern map, vegetation.
- **Constraints honored:** terrain slope, water (bridges if short crossing), density, per-region pattern. No walls, no pinned landmarks, no incremental edits (whole regen).
- **Complexity:** Medium-high. The L-system itself is simple; the self-sensitive local-constraint step (snapping, intersection merging, dead-end cleanup) is where the engineering lives. Watabou notes his generator is "loosely based" on this with the L-system removed [V].
- Relevance: the global-goals/local-constraints split is the right mental model for a spec-driven generator. The "natural" pattern (follow density gradient, random angle perturbation) is the closest to medieval organic streets.

### 2.2 Chen, Esch, Wonka, Müller, Zhang 2008, "Interactive Procedural Street Modeling" (SIGGRAPH 2008)
- URL: https://www.sci.utah.edu/~chengu/street_sig08/street_sig08.pdf
- **Method [M, PDF fetched but not text-extracted]:** User composes a 2D tensor field from basis elements: grid (constant direction), radial (around a point), boundary-following (along a polyline such as a coast or wall), and height-field-derived (streets follow or cross contour lines). Elements are blended with distance-weighted falloff. Major and minor streets are traced as hyperstreamlines of the major/minor eigenvector fields with seed spacing control; intersections cleaned up afterwards. Water and parks are regions where tracing stops; noise/rotation fields give organic irregularity.
- **Inputs:** terrain height field, water/park polygons, user-placed field elements, density parameters.
- **Constraints honored:** terrain, water, boundary following (usable for walls and coasts), region-specific style, landmark-centered radial patterns (place a radial element at a plaza/castle). Edits are local to the field so re-tracing is cheap, but street identity is not preserved across regen.
- **Complexity:** Medium. Tensor field construction is trivial; the robust part is streamline tracing with spacing, merging near-parallel streets, and intersection snapping. Well-documented, many open-source reimplementations (e.g. ProbableTrain's "city-generator" JS, [U] exact name).
- Relevance: strongest candidate for the *street-geometry* stage: radial field at the castle/market, boundary-following along walls and rivers, noise for organic texture.

### 2.3 Weber, Müller, Wonka, Gross 2009, "Interactive Geometric Simulation of 4D Cities" (Eurographics 2009)
- URL: https://www.peterwonka.net/Publications/pdfs/2009.EG.Weber.UrbanSimulation.FinalVersion.pdf ; DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01387.x
- **Method [V abstract, M details]:** Time-stepped geometric simulation of a city: at each step, land-use demand (residential/commercial/industrial) is allocated by a simplified land-use model; streets grow from a major street network outward with exact parcel boundaries; parcels subdivided; buildings placed as 3D envelopes; traffic approximated to feed back into land values. Key difference from UrbanSim-style models: geometry is exact (arbitrary-orientation streets, polygon parcels) instead of a regular grid.
- **Inputs:** terrain, initial major streets or seed, growth parameters per time step, land-use parameters.
- **Constraints honored:** terrain, water, districts (as emergent land use), growth from nucleus (implicit). No walls, no pinned landmarks (except initial seeds). Incrementality is temporal, not editorial.
- **Complexity:** High (coupled simulation of land use, streets, parcels, traffic). Overkill for an overview-map generator, but the "grow from a nucleus along major roads" structure is exactly the medieval morphology you want; borrow the growth loop, not the economics.

### 2.4 Lipp, Scherzer, Wonka, Wimmer 2011, "Interactive Modeling of City Layouts using Layers of Procedural Content" (Eurographics 2011)
- URL: https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2011/lipp2011a/ ; DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2011.01865.x
- **Method [V abstract; M details]:** The layout is a stack of *layers*, each either procedurally generated (e.g. a tensor-field street layer, a block/parcel layer) or manually drawn. Layers are combined with *merging operators* based on graph cuts on the street graph: a manual edit (a dragged district, a drawn street, a moved block) is cut out of the procedural layer and seamlessly stitched in, with a choice of topology-preserving or topology-changing transformations. Because manual edits live in their own layer, regenerating the procedural layer underneath leaves the edits intact; the merge is re-applied. Supports drag-and-drop of whole neighbourhoods (copy a layout fragment and merge it elsewhere).
- **Inputs:** procedural layers (any generator), manual polygons/polylines, layer ordering and blend regions.
- **Constraints honored:** terrain and water as blocking layers [M]; pinned content (anything in a manual layer); *persistent incremental edits across regeneration* (the paper's central claim). Walls and districts can be expressed as region layers. Population is not modelled.
- **Complexity:** Medium-high. The graph-cut merge on planar street graphs and the stitch of parcel boundaries at layer seams is the hard part; a simpler "mask + re-stitch at the boundary" approximation gets most of the value for an overview map.
- **Relevance: highest.** It is the published answer to "the user pins a landmark / redraws a street and then regenerates." Design implication: store user edits as separate layers keyed to stable anchors (not to generated IDs), regenerate the procedural layers, then re-merge.

### 2.5 Surveys
- **Vanegas, Aliaga, Wonka, Müller, Waddell, Watson 2010, "Modelling the Appearance and Behaviour of Urban Spaces", CGF 29(1):25-42 [V].** URL: https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Vanegas-CGF-2010-Modelling_the_Appearance_and_Behaviour_of_Urban_Spaces.pdf . Covers urban modelling (L-systems, tensor fields, example-based), rendering, visualization and *behavioural* simulation models (UrbanSim, cellular automata, agent-based). Useful map of which graphics methods are geometric vs. behavioural. Companion: Vanegas et al. 2009 "Interactive Design of Urban Spaces using Geometrical and Behavioral Modeling" (SIGGRAPH Asia) [V abstract], https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Vanegas09ToG.pdf : a dynamical loop where behavioural demand and geometry are iterated to equilibrium; interactive edits over 200 km2 in seconds.
- **Kelly & McCabe 2006, "A Survey of Procedural Techniques for City Generation", ITB Journal 14 [V].** URL: https://www.citygen.net/files/images/Procedural_City_Generation_Survey.pdf . Categorises road generation (L-systems, templates, agent-based, Voronoi), lot subdivision and building generation; notes Voronoi road networks "look dynamic but not realistic" [V quote via search snippet]. **Citygen (Kelly & McCabe 2007, GDTW) [V bib]:** interactive primary-road network drawn by user with snapping/adaptive road sampling to terrain, secondary roads grown as a grid/organic L-system within cells; buildings by lot subdivision. Good model for "user draws arterials, generator fills."
- **Smelik, Tutenel, Bidarra, Benes 2014, "A Survey on Procedural Modelling for Virtual Worlds", CGF [V bib].** DOI 10.1111/cgf.12276. Broader (terrain, vegetation, roads, cities), with a useful taxonomy of *control* mechanisms (parameters, sketches, examples, constraints).
- **Aliaga 2012, "3D Design and Modeling of Smart Cities from a Computer Graphics Perspective" [V bib]**, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.5402/2012/728913 ; also the SIGGRAPH 2011 course notes on urban layouts: https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/urban/sg_2011_course/umc_SG11_02_urban_layouts.pdf (concise summary of Parish & Müller global goals vs local constraints).

### 2.6 Emilien et al. 2015 "WorldBrush" and Benes et al. 2011 "Guided Procedural Modeling"
- **WorldBrush [V bib, M method].** ACM TOG 34(4), DOI 10.1145/2766975. https://history.siggraph.org/learning/worldbrush-interactive-example-based-synthesis-of-procedural-virtual-worlds-by-emilien-vimont-cani-poulin-and-benes/ . Learns statistical distributions (pair-correlation functions between element categories, plus terrain-slope conditions) from a small example region, then lets the user *brush* that distribution onto new areas, with copy/paste, move and blend operations; roads are synthesized as graphs connecting brushed villages. Handles slope constraints [V]. Relevance: example-based *style transfer* of element density; weaker as a street generator. Interesting for "make this district look like that one."
- **Guided Procedural Modeling [V abstract, M details].** CGF 30(2):325-334, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2011.01886.x; PDF https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Benes-Computer_Graphics_Forum-2011-Guided_Procedural_Modeling.pdf . A procedural model is split into *guides*: closed regions, each running its own L-system/grammar; guides are connected by *links* through which messages pass (e.g. a road grammar inside guide A requests a connection at a point on guide B's border). Gives top-down, region-by-region control and locality: editing one guide re-expands only that guide. Street-layout example shown [V]. Relevance: cleanest published formulation of "districts as regions with their own generator, with explicit interface constraints at shared borders" — maps directly onto Voronoi wards each running their own street/block filler.
- **Emilien et al. 2012 "Procedural Generation of Villages on Arbitrary Terrains", Visual Computer 28:809-818 [V].** https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00694525 ; PDF https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2012-villages.pdf . Three stages: (1) iterative settlement + road growth driven by *interest maps* (slope, sun exposure, access to water, distance to existing roads; roads attract new houses, houses extend roads), (2) anisotropic land conquest for parcels, (3) slope-adaptive shape grammar. Adapted by Ubisoft for Ghost Recon Wildlands [V, lab page]. Relevance: the best published model of *organic pre-industrial growth from a nucleus on terrain*; the interest-map formulation is cheap and fully spec-drivable (weights from the spec).

### 2.7 Medieval / pre-industrial / organic town generation
- **Watabou, Medieval Fantasy City Generator (2017-, Haxe, source on GitHub) [V].** https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator , https://watabou.github.io/city.html . Early versions were "city-shaped Voronoi diagrams": random points, Voronoi cells = wards, a central plaza/castle ward, wall polygon around the inner wards with gates where main roads exit, ward types assigned by distance from centre (market, craftsmen, merchants, slums outside walls, farms), each ward subdivided into blocks with a recursive split/inset, buildings packed within. Later versions (0.11.x) replaced the subdivision with a *straight-skeleton*-based algorithm [V, devlog, exact pipeline stage unverified] and reworked districts for more organic streets. Author says "loosely based on Parish & Müller without the L-system" and that the goal is a nice-looking map, not a realistic city [V].
- **Mas, Martin, Patow 2020, "Simulating the Evolution of Ancient Fortified Cities", CGF 39(1) [V abstract].** https://diglib.eg.org/items/9a5a8549-7ca9-4e05-ac56-0c548beed419/full ; DOI 10.1111/cgf.13897. Interactive year-by-year loop; user authors events (battles, growth, wall creation/expansion); walls created first then expanded to enclose facilities; battle simulation finds weak points and strengthens them. Validated on Carcassonne, Gerunda, Barcelona with historians. Relevance: the only paper with first-class *walls as a constraint that grows with the town*; the "enclose what must be protected, add gates where roads cross" logic is directly reusable even without the battle simulation.
- **Bulbul 2023, "Procedural generation of semantically plausible small-scale towns", Graphical Models 126 [V abstract].** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1524070323000012 . Six weighted principles (security, privacy, sustainability, social life, economy, beauty) steer (1) site selection on a large terrain and (2) iterative construction for a *target population*; explicitly aims at pre-industrial settlement patterns and notes growth is "driven by individual decisions, not central planning". Relevance: a scoring-function formulation that maps naturally to spec sliders.
- **Groenewegen, Smelik, de Kraker, Bidarra 2009, "Procedural City Layout Generation Based on Urban Land Use Models", EG Short [V abstract].** https://graphics.tudelft.nl/~rafa/myPapers/bidarra.EG09.pdf (thesis: https://graphics.tudelft.nl/Publications-new/2009/Gro09/Thesis-SGroenewegen-lowres.pdf ). Inputs: city size, location, *historical background*; districts placed by constraints from classic land-use models (Burgess concentric, Hoyt sector, Harris-Ullman multi-nuclei) in seconds. Relevance: a district-placement stage driven by a few high-level spec fields; the "historical background" selects which land-use model applies — directly analogous to choosing a medieval concentric model (core, inner wards, extramural suburbs).
- **Lechner, Watson, Wilensky et al. 2004/2006, "Procedural City Modeling" / "Procedural Modeling of Urban Land Use" [V bib, M method].** http://ccl.northwestern.edu/2004/ProceduralCityMod.pdf ; https://ccl.northwestern.edu/papers/2006/TR-2007-33.pdf . Agent-based (NetLogo): "extender" agents grow roads toward unserved land, "connector" agents add loops, "developer" agents assign residential/commercial/industrial by land value and adjacency; seeded from a nucleus on a terrain/water map. Slow but conceptually the purest "growth from a nucleus" model.
- **Krecklau et al. 2012, "Procedural Interpolation of Historical City Maps", CGF [V bib only].** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03048.x . Interpolates between dated historical maps of the same city; relevant only as evidence that medieval street graphs can be treated as time-indexed layers.
- **Urban morphology (Conzen, Caniggia, Space Syntax) [V, secondary sources].** Conzen's town-plan analysis decomposes a town into streets/street system, plots/plot pattern, and building arrangement, and identifies *plan units* (morphological regions) with distinct histories; Slater extended it with a method for delimiting plan units in medieval towns (Baker & Slater 1992) [V snippet]. The street pattern is the most persistent element of the townscape [V snippet]. Caniggia/Maffei: building -> urban tissue -> settlement, with "matrix routes" (pre-existing roads), "planned building routes" branching off them, and "connecting routes" closing loops [M]. Space Syntax (Hillier & Hanson 1984) [M]: axial-line *integration* and *choice* measures; markets and main churches in medieval towns sit on high-integration axes. No published generator uses Conzen/Caniggia explicitly [V, search gap]; a 2024 MDPI paper scores diffusion-generated road layouts by predicted space-syntax integration/choice [V], https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/12/3720 . Relevance: (a) Caniggia's route hierarchy is a ready-made three-pass road generator (matrix routes = inter-town roads through gates to market; building routes branch; connecting routes close loops); (b) Conzen plan units = districts with their own grain; (c) Space Syntax integration is a cheap graph metric for *validating* that the market ends up at the most integrated node and for expressive-range plots.
- **Terrain-driven village/road precedents:** Galin et al. 2010/2011 "Procedural generation of roads" (anisotropic shortest path on terrain cost) [M] underlies Emilien 2012; useful for the "road enters town from the pass/ford" constraint.

### 2.8 Constraint-based and mixed-initiative PCG
- **Smith & Mateas 2011, "Answer Set Programming for Procedural Content Generation: A Design Space Approach", IEEE TCIAIG [V bib].** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224240207 . Encode the *design space* declaratively: generate facts (choice rules), test constraints (integrity constraints), optionally optimise; the solver (clingo) enumerates answer sets = artifacts. Shown for maze/dungeon maps, Refraction puzzles, and later Smith 2012 "A case study of expressively constrainable level design tools" ( https://grail.cs.washington.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/smith2012acs.pdf ) adds designer-pinned cells and global properties. Horswill 2022 "ASP for PCG: the Good, the Bad, and the Ugly" ( https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3217/paper19.pdf ) [V bib]: honest account that grounding blows up on large grids and distances, that ASP is excellent for small discrete *macro* decisions and poor for geometry. Graph-based dungeon generation with ASP then geometry (Dormans-style mission/space split) [V snippet]: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327635798 .
  - For your spec: ASP (or a MiniZinc/CP-SAT model) is ideal for the macro stage on a coarse ward graph of ~20-80 cells: "castle on highest cell adjacent to cliff", "temple within 2 cells of market", "docks must touch water", "slums outside the wall", "exactly 3 gates, one per inter-town road", "total ward population = N". Solve times are milliseconds to seconds at that size; the Horswill caveats apply only if you try to do it at block resolution.
- **Tanagra (G. Smith, Whitehead, Mateas 2011), IEEE TCIAIG 3(3) [V bib].** Reactive planning (ABL) proposes platformer level segments; a numeric constraint solver (Choco) keeps pinned designer segments fixed and repairs neighbours for reachability. Model for *mixed-initiative repair*: user pins, system re-solves only the unpinned remainder.
- **WaveFunctionCollapse (Gumin 2016; Karth & Smith 2017 "WFC is constraint solving in the wild", FDG, DOI 10.1145/3102071.3110566) [V bib].** Local adjacency constraints, min-entropy heuristic, no backtracking in the original. Known weakness: *no non-local constraints* [V snippets] — extensions add global constraints, multi-layer generation and distance constraints (2024, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381382612 ), hierarchical semantic WFC (AIIDE 2023, https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3582437.3587209 ), mixed-initiative miWFC, and a WFC-for-video-game-cities paper ( https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336732478 ). Karth & Smith's reframing in ASP lets you add global constraints at the cost of solve time. For city overview maps WFC is a reasonable *block-texture* filler inside a ward (which building footprints/shapes sit next to which), not a layout engine.
- **Sentient Sketchbook (Liapis, Yannakakis, Togelius 2013) [V].** https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar//bitstream/123456789/29607/1/Sentient_sketchbook_computer-assisted_game_level_authoring_2013.pdf . Designer sketches a low-res strategy map (bases, resources, passable/impassable); a two-population feasible/infeasible GA proposes playable variants in real time, with connectivity guaranteed. The "coarse sketch = spec, generator proposes alternatives, user adopts" loop is the right UX for your spec editor; the feasible/infeasible split is a practical way to handle hard constraints in a search-based generator.
- **Hybrids of a discrete solver for macro placement + geometric generation [V, dungeon domain only].** Published mainly for dungeons (graph of rooms via ASP/grammar, then geometry); for cities the closest analogues are Groenewegen 2009 (district placement by rules, then streets) and Benes 2011 (guides). No paper found that does ASP-ward-assignment + tensor-field streets [U], so this hybrid is novel-but-obvious.

### 2.9 Evaluation: expressive range and generator bias
- **Smith & Whitehead 2010, "Analyzing the Expressive Range of a Level Generator", PCG Workshop [V].** https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~ejw/papers/smith-pcg-2010.pdf . Generate thousands of outputs, compute 2+ global metrics per output (they used linearity and leniency), plot 2D histograms (heatmaps) and compare across parameter settings; found an unintended linearity bias caused by a late implementation detail [V]. The point is exactly your worry: the heatmap shows *where the generator cannot go*.
- **Summerville 2018, "Expanding Expressive Range: Evaluation Methodologies for Procedural Content Generation", AIIDE [V].** https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/13012/12860/16529 . Critiques 2-metric heatmaps: proposes corner plots (all metric pairs), comparing the generator's distribution against a *corpus* of real/human examples (so you can see bias *away* from real towns, not just within-generator variety), and statistical distance measures.
- **Cook, Gow, Colton 2016, "Danesh" [V].** https://mkremins.github.io/refs/Danesh.pdf . Tool that randomly samples generator parameters, computes metrics, visualises the expressive range interactively, and offers automated parameter tuning toward a target region. Model for a dev-only "ERA dashboard" in your repo.
- **Quality-Diversity / MAP-Elites (Gravina et al. 2019 "PCG through Quality Diversity" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.04053 ; Alvarez et al. "Interactive Constrained MAP-Elites" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03377 ) [V bib].** Instead of only measuring diversity, fill a grid of feature bins with the best artifact per bin; guarantees coverage of the feature space. "Level Generation with Constrained Expressive Range" (2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05334 ) [V bib] uses expressive range as the target space directly.
- **Kolmogorov-style argument: Cook 2023 "Why Oatmeal is Cheap" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02131 [V bib]:** cheap variety (noise) vs. meaningful variety.
- Suggested city metrics (own proposal [U]): street-graph stats (mean degree, dead-end ratio, orientation entropy à la Boeing 2019, block-area Gini), wall-enclosed fraction, radial density slope (how fast density falls from the market), space-syntax integration of the market node, distance-of-landmarks-to-spec-targets, and a per-run "spec satisfaction" vector. Plot corner plots per population class and against a corpus of ~30 real medieval town plans (Conzen/Slater literature or OSM historic cores) to catch single-sample calibration bias.

### 2.10 LLM-driven PCG (2023-2026)
- **Surveys [V bib]:** Maleki & Zhao 2024 "Procedural Content Generation in Games: A Survey with Insights on Emerging LLM Integration", AIIDE-24, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15644 (taxonomy: search-based, ML, noise/other, LLM, *combined*; abstract only read). Gallotta et al. 2024 "LLMs and Games: A Survey and Roadmap" arXiv 2402.18659. Mao et al. 2024 "PCG via Generative AI" arXiv 2407.09013. Overview page: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/procedural-content-generation-with-llms .
- **LLM generates geometry/tiles directly:** Todd et al. 2023 "Level Generation Through LLMs", FDG, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05817 (Sokoban; performance scales with fine-tuning data; novelty/playability tension) [V abstract]. **Word2World** (Nasir, James, Togelius 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06686 ) [V full]: story -> extracted character/tileset/goal legends -> LLM emits an ASCII grid in two passes (environment, then entities) -> *code-level repair* (duplicate removal, row padding) -> evaluation (A* playability, novelty, LLM coherence judge) -> feedback rounds. Playable in 9/10 runs with the full pipeline, ~60% with one round; multi-tile objects unsupported; $0.5-1 per world. Word2Minecraft 2025 extends to 3D ( https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.16536 ). Lesson: direct grid emission needs a repair+verify loop and still has poor geometric control; it does not scale to continuous street geometry.
- **LLM writes a spec/configuration consumed by a classical generator (the architecture your project is adopting):**
  - *Zero-shot 3D Map Generation with LLM Agents: a Dual-Agent Architecture* (Dec 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10501 ) [V abstract]: Actor sets configuration parameters of "arbitrary PCG tools", Critic evaluates renders/metrics and requests refinement; training-free; outperforms single-agent baselines; outputs "structurally valid" because the engine guarantees validity.
  - *CityGenAgent* (Feb 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05362 ) [V abstract]: LLM emits schema-constrained *Block Programs* and *Building Programs* (BlockGen/BuildingGen, SFT + RL with spatial-alignment and visual-consistency rewards); non-self-intersecting polygons enforced by schema; natural-language editing of the program.
  - *Yo'City* (Nov 2025, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18734 ) [V abstract]: LLM hierarchical planner designs the layout, specialised 3D generator renders, self-critic expansion.
  - *CityCraft* (2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04983 ) [V abstract]: diffusion transformer makes the 2D layout, LLM assigns land use from prompt; i.e. LLM as *semantic labeller*, not geometer.
  - *URBANE* (2025/26, Springer) [V bib]: VLM natural-language editing of existing block layouts.
  - *Conversational Interactions with Procedural Generators using LLMs* (FDG 2025, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723498.3723788 ) [V bib only; 403 on fetch]: LLM mediates between user chat and generator parameters.
  - *Database-driven 3D level generation with LLMs* (2025, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18533 ) [V bib]: LLM queries an asset/rule DB, engine assembles.
  - *From World-Gen to Quest-Line: dependency-driven prompt pipeline for RPGs* (2026, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25482 ) [V bib]: ordered prompt DAG so later artifacts (quests) depend on earlier (world).
  - Consensus across these [V pattern, U as a formal claim]: let the LLM produce a *typed, schema-validated intermediate representation* (land-use plan, program, parameter set) and let a deterministic engine produce geometry; add a verifier/critic loop that reads back metrics or a render. This matches your "requirements spec consumed by a classical generator" design and is where the 2025-26 literature has converged.

### 2.11 Red Blob Games: polygon map generation (2010) and mapgen4 (2018-2025)
- URLs: http://www-cs-students.stanford.edu/~amitp/game-programming/polygon-map-generation/ ; https://simblob.blogspot.com/2010/09/polygon-map-generation-part-1.html ; https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/mapgen4/ ; https://github.com/redblobgames/mapgen4 ; https://www.redblobgames.com/x/1721-voronoi-alternative/ ; 2025 posts on trade routes https://www.redblobgames.com/blog/2025-05-08-mapgen4-trade-routes/ and the renderer https://www.redblobgames.com/blog/2025-09-29-mapgen4-renderer/ .
- **2010 polygon maps [V]:** random points -> Lloyd relaxation (2 iterations, "averaging corners") or Poisson-disc sampling -> Voronoi polygons stored as a *dual graph* (Delaunay for adjacency, Voronoi for shapes, with corner and centre nodes); island shape from a radial/noise function on corners; elevation = distance from coast with redistribution; rivers flow downhill corner-to-corner from random mountain corners; moisture propagated from rivers/lakes; biomes from elevation x moisture; noisy edges for rendering. Alternative: non-Voronoi polygons by placing the second point set at centroids [V].
- **mapgen4 [V]:** paint-driven (user paints mountains/valleys/ocean), elevation from coastline distance, wind/evaporation/rainfall simulated on the mesh for rivers and biomes, full regen targeted at ~30 ms with multithreading; 2025 additions include trade routes (least-cost paths over the mesh) and a WebGL2 renderer.
- **What transfers to city scale:** (1) Poisson-disc / Lloyd-relaxed Voronoi as the *ward* mesh with the dual Delaunay graph for adjacency and the macro constraint solver (identical to Watabou's ward partition, with better spacing control); (2) "distance from X with redistribution" as the density/land-value field (distance from market/gate instead of coast); (3) downhill corner-to-corner flow for rivers and *drainage/sewer lines*, and least-cost path for the roads that enter town; (4) noisy edges for hand-drawn look; (5) the paint-then-regen-in-30-ms loop as the UX target for incremental edits; (6) the Delaunay/Voronoi dual as the data structure in which Lipp-style layers and graph cuts are easy to implement. What does *not* transfer: biome logic, the climate sim, and the one-island assumption.

## 3. Recommended hybrid architecture (one page)

**Principle (from Parish & Müller, Benes, Lipp, ASP-PCG, and the 2025-26 LLM-configurator papers):** separate *what must be true* (discrete, solver-checked, cheap to re-solve) from *how it looks* (continuous geometry, cheap to re-trace), and keep user edits in their own layers so both stages can be re-run without losing them.

### Stage 0 — Spec (the contract)
Typed JSON: population, era/culture preset, terrain (height field or sketch), water bodies, wall count/era, named landmarks each with constraints (`near:market`, `touches:water`, `on:highest`, `inside:walls`, `pinned:(x,y)` optional), district list with size/population weights, inter-town roads with entry side. Produced by hand or by an LLM; *always* schema-validated. (Pattern: CityGenAgent, Word2World's legends, dual-agent configurator.)

### Stage 1 — Macro layout on a coarse ward mesh (discrete constraint stage)
1. Poisson-disc/Lloyd-relaxed Voronoi mesh of 30-120 cells over the site (Red Blob / Watabou), clipped by water; dual Delaunay graph gives adjacency, cell attributes: elevation, slope, water-adjacent, distance-to-entry-roads.
2. Solve ward assignment with a CP/ASP model (clingo or OR-Tools CP-SAT): assign each cell a district type or landmark; constraints from spec (landmark placement, districts contiguous, slums/farms outside walls, docks on water, castle on defensible cell, total cell-population within tolerance of spec population, exactly `k` gates on cells crossed by entry roads); objective: compactness + spec soft preferences. Pinned landmarks/user edits enter as fixed atoms. Solve time: ms-s at this size (Horswill's warning only bites at block resolution).
3. Derive the wall as the boundary of the "intramural" cell set (smoothed, with towers at convex corners and gates where entry roads cross — Mas 2020 logic). Optional second ring for growth phases.
4. Expressive-range gate: at this stage compute cheap metrics (enclosed fraction, radial density slope, landmark-to-market graph distance, Space-Syntax integration of market node on the ward graph) and reject/resample degenerate solutions; log all solutions for the ERA dashboard (Smith & Whitehead, Summerville corner plots, Danesh).

### Stage 2 — Geometric stage (streets, blocks, buildings)
1. **Primary roads (Caniggia "matrix routes"):** least-cost paths over the terrain from each gate to the market/castle and between major landmarks (mapgen4 trade-route style); these are the persistent skeleton.
2. **Street field per ward (Chen 2008 + Benes guides):** build a tensor field per ward from its type: radial element at plazas/castle, boundary-following along walls, rivers and primary roads, grid for planned extensions/new towns, noise for organic wards. Trace major/minor hyperstreamlines inside each ward; ward borders act as guide links so streets meet at shared gates/cross points.
3. **Blocks and parcels:** polygonize the street graph, recursive/straight-skeleton subdivision (Watabou 0.11, Vanegas 2012 "Procedural Generation of Parcels" DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03047.x); density from distance-to-market field; building footprints by packing or WFC-style tile rules inside parcels for texture.
4. **Landmark footprints** stamped at solver-chosen cells with their own templates (church + square, castle + bailey, docks + quay); streets re-traced around them (they are obstacles in the field).

### Stage 3 — Layers and incremental edits (Lipp 2011)
Every output of stages 1-2 is a layer keyed to *stable anchors* (ward IDs from the mesh seed, landmark names, spec road names), never to generated geometry IDs. User edits (move a landmark, redraw a street, retype a ward) are stored as a manual layer plus a mask; on regen, Stage 1 re-solves with those as fixed atoms, Stage 2 re-traces only wards whose inputs changed, and the merge stitches at mask boundaries (a simplified graph cut: snap re-traced street ends to the retained ones). Target: sub-second regen like mapgen4's paint loop.

### Stage 4 — Render
Hand-drawn style (noisy edges, hatching), layered SVG/Canvas with wards, walls, water, landmarks and labels as separate groups so the UI can toggle them; export spec + seed + edit layers so any map is reproducible.

### Risks / open questions
- Novelty: no paper does ASP-ward-assignment + tensor streets [U]; expect integration work at the ward-border seams (Benes links help).
- Bias: calibrate ward-type priors and tensor parameters per *era preset*, not from one sample; run ERA against a small corpus of real medieval plans before shipping presets.
- LLM in the loop: keep it at Stage 0 (spec writing, naming, editing the spec from chat) and optionally as a critic reading Stage-1 metrics; never emit geometry directly (Word2World results).
